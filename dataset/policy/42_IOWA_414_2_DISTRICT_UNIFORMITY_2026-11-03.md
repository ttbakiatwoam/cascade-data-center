## Statutory text (verbatim)

**414.2 Districts.**

> For any or all of the purposes of this chapter, the local legislative body, referred to as the council, may divide the city into districts, including historical preservation districts but only as provided in section 15.459, of such number, shape, and area as may be deemed best suited to carry out the purposes of this chapter; and within such districts the council may regulate and restrict the erection, construction, reconstruction, alteration, repair, or use of buildings, structures, or land. All such regulations and restrictions shall be uniform for each class or kind of buildings throughout each district, but the regulations in one district may differ from those in other districts.

## Interpretation for the proposed M-2 zoning ordinance

**General rule.** Section 414.2 requires uniformity **for each class or kind of buildings within each zoning district**. It expressly allows different regulations **between** different zoning districts. The wording does **not**, by itself, create an absolute prohibition on every regulation that distinguishes between business types or objectively defined classes of buildings.

**Recommended consistent application.** Cascade's M-2 proposal should regulate comparable external physical effects through uniform standards or clearly defined, objectively supported physical/operational thresholds. Noise, water, lighting, storage, electrical demand, generators, setbacks, stormwater, and emergency planning should not be selectively applied or waived based merely on an operator's name, tenant, customer, or computing workload.

**Distinguish the use listing from performance standards.** Adding “Data Center or High-Density Computing Facility” to permitted M-2 principal uses as B(19) establishes a land-use category; it does not mean every impact-based standard can lawfully target that category alone. A properly drawn class-specific standard **might** be permissible if the City establishes statutory authority, a rational land-use basis, district-wide uniformity within that class, and consistent procedures. City Attorney review is essential.

**Preserve the current draft's neutral thresholds.** The proposal's 5 MW coincident demand, 25,000 gallons/24 h water, 2 MW generation, and 5 MWh storage review triggers are proposed local policy choices, **not thresholds specified by Iowa Code §414.2**. The evidence supporting those thresholds and their consistent treatment should be documented before enactment.

**Source status.** The linked official statute is controlling source text, not a judicial interpretation or City Council finding. This note is noncodified legal research, not a statement that the proposed Cascade ordinance has been adopted.
