# Iowa municipal data-center ordinances: hyperscale versus ordinary facilities

**City-first legal comparator | Research cutoff: November 11, 2026 | For the November 18 committee meeting**

**Research status:** This report distinguishes *enacted ordinances*, *temporary moratoria*, *ordinances under consideration*, and the *Cascade working proposal*. It does **not** treat a county ordinance as city law. Verify late municipal votes and obtain the signed/code-published ordinance before relying on a final legal status.

## 1. Define the comparison population — no micro-data-center matrix

This **municipal comparison covers** (a) facilities explicitly classed by an Iowa city as *hyperscale* or another large-scale tier, and (b) standalone **ordinary/standard commercial data centers** regulated as a principal land use, typically in industrial districts. The phrase **“ordinary/standard” is this report's descriptive category, not a separate Iowa Code definition**. City ordinances may call these “small,” “Tier 1,” “Tier 2,” or simply “Data Centers”; translate each city’s actual threshold rather than assume equivalence.

**Exclude micro/edge/ancillary facilities from the comparison matrix.** A server closet, room supporting a hospital or school, modular edge unit, or a jurisdiction's special “micro data center” category should not be counted as a standalone ordinary data center *without establishing that it is actually the principal regulated use*. This is a **research-scope exclusion**, not a new statewide definition or an automatic exemption from Cascade zoning.

The Iowa Code data-center wording in [§423.3(95)(f)(1)–(2)](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/2026/423.3.pdf) belongs to a **tax-program** context; it does not create state-wide municipal “micro,” “ordinary,” or “hyperscale” zoning tiers. Each city must identify what its own ordinance actually covers.

### How the same project can be treated differently

* An **exactly 100,000-sq-ft center drawing 40 MW** meets **Dubuque's** square-foot trigger (OR) and **Central City's** proposed Tier 3, but **does not** meet **Cascade's** *two-part* Hyperscale definition because 40 MW does not exceed 60 MW. Under the **new Cascade proposal**, it would be evaluated as an ordinary/non-hyperscale Data Center subject to other M-2 and major-industrial requirements.
* An **80,000-sq-ft center drawing 80 MW** meets **Dubuque's** >75 MW trigger but **does not** meet **Cascade's** Hyperscale definition because it is below 100,000 sq ft. It would still trigger other impact reviews if enacted.
* An **exactly 100,000-sq-ft center drawing 60 MW** does **not** meet Cascade's Hyperscale definition because the demand must **exceed** 60 MW. This example illustrates why **and/or**, ≥/> and campus aggregation rules must be explicit.

**The examples compare classification alone.** They do not establish permitting, environmental compliance, ownership control, or utility feasibility. They do not justify treating unrelated micro/edge equipment as a principal commercial data-center use.

### Statutory cautions for the proposed citywide prohibition

1. A city may restrict a well-defined land use through an ordinance authorized and enacted under **Chapter 414**, but the committee must separately assess statutory limitations, the city's comprehensive plan, the treatment of similarly situated uses, existing lawful nonconforming uses, due process, and the precise powers of the board of adjustment. This report **does not assert** that a ban based on the two Hyperscale thresholds is guaranteed to survive litigation.
2. **“100,000 square feet or larger” and “electrical demand exceeding 60 MW” are joined by AND** in Cascade's participant-reported committee definition, unlike Dubuque's **OR** test. Whether area is per building, integrated development, campus or cumulative gross floor area, and how electrical demand is calculated, must be specified consistently with the separate reported vote.
3. Because **MW denotes power** rather than an annual energy quantity, an eventual calendar-year measurement convention would be a **new wording decision** requiring the committee's vote, not a purported correction to the November 11 5–3 definition approval.
4. The City Council's **operative permitted-use/prohibition text**, not a bare glossary definition, controls which land uses are proposed to be allowed or disallowed if the ordinance is enacted.

## 4. County ordinances — later separate phase, NOT included in this municipal ranking/comparison

**Deferred county docket for a separate report:** Dubuque County, Jones County, Linn County, Allamakee County, and other counties with data-center regulations. Compare their **enacted versus proposed** ordinances separately under [Iowa Code Chapter 335](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/2026/335.pdf); a *City of Dubuque* hyperscale moratorium is **not** a *Dubuque County* ordinance. County rules can provide examples of technical drafting, but they cannot silently be treated as the City's legal authority.
