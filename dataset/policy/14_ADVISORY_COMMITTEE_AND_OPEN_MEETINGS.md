# Advisory Committee and Iowa Open-Meetings Research

> This file summarizes public-governance research relevant to a City-created data-center advisory body. It omits committee-member names and all private correspondence.
>
> Last reviewed: 2026-09-18

## Cascade action

On July 27, 2026, Cascade City Council minutes record approval of Resolution 70-26 creating an Advisory Committee to Prepare a Data Center Ordinance. On August 10, 2026, council minutes record approval of Resolution 79-26 appointing committee members.

Because the committee was formally created by a public governing body to perform public-policy work, Iowa Open Meetings Law is a central procedural reference.

## September 14 proposed clarification

The September 14 City Council packet included proposed Resolution 92-26 to clarify committee structure and procedure. The draft proposed eight voting members—two City Council representatives, two Cascade Economic Development Corporation representatives, two Planning & Zoning Commission representatives, and two citizen representatives—with non-voting technical/advisory participants excluded from quorum. It proposed a quorum of five voting members.

The draft describes the committee as advisory only and says it may research and recommend policy concerning zoning, land use, water/wastewater, electricity, cooling, noise, environmental issues, fire protection, construction, economic impacts, public safety, community safeguards, and ordinance language. It expressly withholds authority to approve/deny projects, spend City funds, execute agreements, or grant permits.

The draft also provides for Chapter 21 compliance, posted agendas, official minutes, public attendance, and reasonable conduct rules, while stating that committee agendas would not contain a standing public-comment area.

**Status:** as of September 18, the City's records index had not posted September 14 meeting minutes. This dataset therefore treats the resolution as proposed packet language rather than confirmed adopted text.

## Conflict-of-interest statutes: scope matters

Iowa Code §362.5 prohibits specified interests by a **city officer or employee** in a contract, job of work, material, profits, or services involving that person's city, subject to statutory exceptions. Iowa Code §362.6 addresses conflicts of interest in voting and provides procedures including declining to vote and recording the declaration.

Those provisions should be applied to concrete facts: the person's legal status, the specific measure or contract, and the actual financial or other interest involved. Organizational affiliation by itself should not be converted into a conclusion that committee service is automatically unlawful without the statutory elements and facts needed for that conclusion.

This repository therefore records conflict-of-interest allegations as legal questions unless supported by an official determination or sufficient facts to establish the statutory elements.

## Iowa Code Chapter 21

Iowa Code Chapter 21 generally requires meetings of covered governmental bodies to be open to the public unless a statutory exception permits a closed session. Key research points include:

- definitions of covered governmental bodies in §21.2;
- open-meeting and minutes requirements in §21.3;
- notice requirements, generally at least 24 hours, in §21.4;
- narrowly authorized closed sessions in §21.5;
- authority to use reasonable rules for meeting conduct in §21.7.

A desire for candid discussion, convenience, or controversy is not itself a statutory basis for closing a meeting. A specific Chapter 21 exception must apply.

## Public attendance vs. public comment

Iowa Public Information Board guidance explains an important distinction: Chapter 21's open-meeting requirement gives the public a right to observe covered meetings, but the law does **not** generally require a public-comment period at every meeting.

If a body chooses to permit public comment, reasonable rules can be adopted for time, order, relevance, and conduct. Such rules should be applied consistently and without viewpoint discrimination.

This supports a committee structure in which meetings remain publicly observable while the committee uses an orderly agenda and may limit or omit public comment where lawful and appropriate.

## Disruption and meeting rules

Chapter 21 and IPIB guidance support reasonable conduct rules. A public body's rules can address disruption while preserving public access. Removal/ejection decisions should be grounded in actual disruption or applicable law/rules, not disagreement with a speaker's viewpoint.

## Minutes and transparency

Open sessions should produce minutes consistent with Chapter 21. If a lawful closed session occurs, the body should follow the separate statutory recording/minutes requirements applicable to that closed session and preserve the record as required by law.

## Cascade local board/commission code issue

Cascade's 2021 Code §2-2-9 includes provisions relating to City residency and states that boards/commissions are to be gender balanced in accordance with Iowa Code §69.16A.

Iowa Acts 2024, Chapter 1004, §48 repealed Iowa Code §69.16A effective in 2024. Thus Cascade's local code appears to contain a cross-reference to a repealed state statute. This dataset does **not** make a legal determination about the continuing effect of the local wording; it flags the provision for City Attorney/code-maintenance review.

## Committee-design questions considered in the project

Without naming participants, the project evaluated:

- voting vs. non-voting/advisory seats;
- defined subject-matter representation;
- quorum;
- odd vs. even voting membership;
- residency requirements;
- attendance and vacancy rules;
- public observation vs. public-comment policy;
- disruption/conduct rules;
- closed-session limits;
- minutes and public records;
- whether composition rules in local code need updating.

The objective is a committee whose process is legally defensible, transparent, workable, and focused on technical/policy recommendations rather than advocacy theater.
