### 1. Iowa state laws — official complete verbatim text

The following links point to the **full verbatim public legal texts**, not summaries. To avoid detaching individual rules from exceptions and applicability language, the exact official sections should be distributed with the committee packet.

| Citation | What to read in the official text | Authority / scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Iowa Code §455B.131 (2026)](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/2026/455B.131.pdf) | Subsections (1) **air contaminant**, (2) **air contaminant source**, (3) **air pollution**, (6) **emission**, and (8) **major stationary source** | State air-quality statutory definitions |
| [Iowa Code §455B.133 (2026)](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/2026/455B.133.pdf) | State Environmental Protection Commission rulemaking authority | Scope and legal framework for implementing air requirements |
| [Iowa Code §455B.144 (2026)](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/2026/455B.144.pdf) | Local air pollution control programs | Not the same thing as unilateral city authority to issue state air permits |
| [Iowa Code §455B.145 (2026)](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/2026/455B.145.pdf) | State review/role in local programs | Review this with §455B.144, rather than assuming an automatically delegated local program |
| [Iowa Administrative Code 567—Chapter 22](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/iac/chapter/567.22.pdf) | §22.1 construction permit; review exemptions including §22.1(2)(r) concerning certain engines below 400 brake horsepower and conditions | Iowa air construction permits; actual applicability depends on engine facts |
| [Iowa Administrative Code 567—Chapter 23](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/iac/chapter/567.23.pdf) | §23.1 incorporation of federal New Source Performance Standards (NSPS) and National Emission Standards for Hazardous Air Pollutants (NESHAP) | State incorporation and administration of applicable federal source standards |

**Reading notes:** Under §455B.131, **air contaminants**, **air pollution**, **emissions**, and **a regulated stationary source** are related but not interchangeable. A diesel generator is ordinarily a stationary combustion source, while a computer server is not itself a diesel exhaust source. Engine size, aggregate facility emissions, fuel, use, installation date, and permit status all matter. An exemption from one state permit requirement **does not automatically exempt a generator from federal emissions rules**. Neither does the presence of state air permits automatically approve a proposed *land use*.

**Agency guidance:** Iowa DNR, [Regulations for Data Centers](https://www.iowadnr.gov/programs-services/business-regulatory-assistance/regulations-data-centers), identifies air-permit issues associated with backup generators and explains the separate roles of state environmental programs and local land-use decisions. Ask for any site-specific construction permit, operating permit, registration or exemption basis, and the complete generator equipment schedule.

| [40 CFR §60.4214](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-40/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-60/subpart-IIII/section-60.4214) | Notification, reporting and recordkeeping where applicable | Are hours, operating categories and emissions records documented? |
| [40 CFR Part 63, Subpart ZZZZ](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-40/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-63/subpart-ZZZZ) | Stationary reciprocating internal-combustion-engine NESHAP (air toxics) | Do area/major-source, existing/new-engine and engine-specific provisions apply? |
| [EPA's stationary engines compliance guidance](https://www.epa.gov/stationary-engines/compliance-requirements-stationary-engines) | EPA explanation of NSPS and RICE NESHAP coverage | Identify overlapping versus separately applicable requirements; guidance is not a replacement for CFR text |

**Emergency hours nuance:** Section 60.4211(f) distinguishes real emergency operation, maintenance/readiness testing, and restricted nonemergency uses. The widely cited **100 hours/year** for maintenance/readiness testing and limited other uses is not automatically **100 plus an additional 50**; specified limited nonemergency hours are within that allowance, subject to paragraph-specific conditions. Genuine emergency operation has different rules. **Do not assume “Tier 4” is a universal precondition for all stationary emergency diesel engines under federal law.** Tier/model-year applicability must be checked engine by engine. It is also possible for a proposed county zoning draft to call for Tier IV as a **local proposal** even where the federal rule would apply a different standard.

**Jurisdiction test:** Iowa counties typically operate under Iowa Code Chapter 335 zoning authority; municipal zoning under Chapter 414. An air permit under Chapter 455B is neither a zoning permit nor a blanket authorization to ignore local land-use conditions. Any attempt to add local emissions thresholds, operating restrictions, engine technology specifications or permit requirements should receive specific City Attorney / DNR review for legal authority, preemption, evidence, and enforceability. Uniformity under [Iowa Code §414.2](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/2026/414.2.pdf) deserves attention if Cascade proposes industrial standards applying only to one named business type.

### 6. Fact requests and practical agenda checklist

1. Obtain **every engine's** make, model, fuel, brake horsepower, generator kW/MW, engine manufacture date, installation/construction date, aftertreatment and EPA certification or emissions specification. Avoid relying on total MW without individual engine data.
2. Obtain DNR **construction and operating permit numbers**, permit-exemption or registration determinations, federal NSPS/NESHAP applicability determinations, and any special conditions on hours and emissions.
3. Distinguish **emergency** runtime, **maintenance/readiness** runtime, and any **nonemergency** operation; request load-dispatch plans and metered hour records.
4. Require an air dispersion analysis **only if legally supported and tied to a defined use, source, receptor and defensible measurement method**; establish what the DNR permit already evaluates before duplicating it.
5. Compare noise and air contaminants separately: dBA/dBC acoustic levels and particulate matter (PM), nitrogen oxides (NOx), sulfur dioxide (SO2), carbon monoxide (CO), and hazardous-air-pollutant standards are distinct.
6. Ask County and City staff to certify which ordinance texts are **currently effective** on November 18 versus proposed; obtain official November 11 vote minutes as well.
7. Ask whether any existing Iowa DNR air permit sets more specific limits, testing, reporting, or operating hours than broadly applicable federal engine rules.
