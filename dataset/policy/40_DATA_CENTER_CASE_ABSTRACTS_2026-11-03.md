# Data center and industrial land-use litigation: 27 case abstracts

**Compilation date:** November 3, 2026. **Underlying case-status research cutoff:** October 28, 2026. The November edit adds abstracts and Chicago-style source notes; it does **not** imply a new court-docket check for every case through November 3. This is a selected national inventory, not an exhaustive PACER or fifty-state audit.

**Scope:** Local-government zoning, moratoria, property-use and entitlement disputes involving proposed data centers and cryptocurrency-computing facilities. A cryptocurrency mining case is included only as a land-use and noise comparator, not as proof that every data center has the same legal status.

## How the abstracts should be read

**A settlement is not a contested ruling.** A preliminary injunction is temporary. Denial of a preliminary injunction is also not necessarily final resolution. Dismissal and loss on the merits differ. A project under construction is not equivalent to a completed or approved project. The actual court is listed separately from a *geographic* federal district, which is not a claim that a state proceeding was filed in that federal district.

**Cascade jurisdiction:** Dubuque and Jones Counties, Iowa, fall geographically within the Northern District of Iowa and the Eighth Circuit. State-court decisions from other states generally do not bind Iowa courts. District-court orders elsewhere in the Eighth Circuit are persuasive at most on analogous federal issues; published Eighth Circuit appellate decisions can bind N.D. Iowa courts on applicable federal law.[^28]

**Citation method:** Superscript Chicago-style numbered endnotes. A case note links directly to available decisions, dockets, government statements, or identified journalism. When underlying court orders were unavailable, the source is labeled secondary and the case remains unverified at the merits level.

### 1. Related Digital / RD Michigan Property Owner I LLC v. Saline Township (MI)

**Abstract.** This dispute concerned denial of data-center rezoning and alleged exclusionary zoning. The developer and township reached a consent judgment establishing a negotiated path, not a judicial holding that the township's zoning was unconstitutional. As recorded in the source review, the reported procedural posture was **Oct. 2025 consent judgment after rezoning rejection**, and the development status was **Construction underway June 2026; not fully built**.[^1]

**Court:** Washtenaw County Circuit Court (state). **Docket:** 2025-001577-CZ. **Federal geographic mapping:** E.D. Michigan (geographic mapping). Circuit: Sixth. **Precedential status:** Actual court and procedural posture control; geographic circuit mapping is informational, not a claim of binding precedent.

### 2. Jones Digital LLC v. Arkansas County (AR)

## Conclusions for Cascade's proposed M-2 ordinance

1. **Require evidence and apply uniform standards.** An ordinance grounded in Iowa Code §§414.1-414.3 and connected to documented land-use effects is more legally defensible than a personal or company-specific restriction.[^29]
2. **Use clear numerical terms and established decision processes.** Define sound monitoring, electrical load, equipment setbacks, and application criteria. Keep the Board of Adjustment's special-exception and variance authority consistent with §414.12, including its 2025-added practical-difficulties provision.[^30]
3. **Treat procedural cases as procedural.** Consent judgments and early injunctions show litigation and settlement risk, not a categorical prohibition on industrial regulations.
4. **Distinguish federal precedent from geography.** Jones Digital and NewRays arose in the Eastern District of Arkansas (Eighth Circuit). Their district-court results are not independently binding in the Northern District of Iowa; cases in Missouri state courts are not federal Eighth Circuit decisions.
5. **Track unresolved records.** Several cases rely on reporting or trackers instead of full state-court dockets. A City Attorney should obtain actual pleadings and final orders before relying on them in a legislative finding or brief.
