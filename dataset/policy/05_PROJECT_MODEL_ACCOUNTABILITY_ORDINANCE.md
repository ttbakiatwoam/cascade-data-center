# Project-Generated Cascade Data Center Accountability Ordinance

> **Status: project-generated model proposal — not adopted law.**
>
> The June 21, 2026 draft was created for public-policy discussion and attorney review. It must not be represented as an ordinance adopted by the City of Cascade.
>
> Last reviewed: 2026-09-10

## Purpose of the model

The model ordinance was developed around a simple policy premise: Cascade does not need to prohibit data centers categorically, but it can evaluate whether a proposed facility can be conditioned to reduce water, noise, infrastructure, fiscal, emergency-response, and neighboring-property impacts while producing enforceable local benefits.

## Major provisions proposed in the model

### Application and project definition

The draft uses a broad definition of data center encompassing cryptocurrency mining, AI computing, cloud, infrastructure-as-a-service, colocation, disaster recovery, enterprise hosting, and similar commercial computing uses. It proposes treating phased or affiliated development as one project for threshold purposes.

### Required application materials

The model calls for, among other items:

- complete site and phase plans;
- power demand by phase and full buildout;
- water demand by phase and full buildout;
- cooling-system narrative and sealed engineering drawings;
- traffic/road impact analysis;
- stormwater and construction plans;
- waste-management plan;
- generator/backup-power plan;
- emergency-response plan;
- decommissioning plan;
- workforce/training plan;
- development/community-benefit agreement.

### Independent review

The draft proposes applicant-funded, City-selected or City-approved independent review covering environmental impacts, water, wastewater, drainage, traffic, noise/vibration, generator air emissions, emergency services, utility infrastructure, neighboring land use/property effects, and fiscal impacts.

### Cooling and water

The model favors closed-loop, dry, immersion, or other low-water cooling, with evaporative cooling requiring additional findings. It proposes a project-wide water limit, metering, reporting, drought-response provisions, and an independent water study and Water Use Agreement.

### Noise

The project has consistently favored measurable property-line/sensitive-receptor standards, consideration of low-frequency/tonal sound, independent pre/post construction testing, complaint-triggered testing, and enforceable corrective action rather than relying on general promises that a facility will be “quiet.”

### Infrastructure and community benefits

The model proposes making the applicant responsible for infrastructure improvements caused by the project and using a contractual development/community-benefit agreement rather than inventing a local tax that may exceed municipal authority.

The draft contains illustrative community-benefit payment formulas. Those figures are policy examples only and require legal, fiscal, and negotiation review before any use.

### Workforce and emergency response

The model proposes local recruitment/training pathways, coordination with schools and workforce programs, and applicant support for specialized emergency-response training/equipment reasonably required because of the facility.

### Transparency, enforcement, and decommissioning

The draft proposes annual compliance reporting, public access to non-confidential information, enforcement remedies, a decommissioning plan, possible financial security, and fresh review of material expansion rather than an automatic vested right to expand.

## Iowa models used in drafting

The draft explicitly used public Iowa examples as structural references:

- Linn County — water study, Water Use Agreement, Economic Development Agreement/community fund, setbacks, noise, roads, emergency planning;
- Palo — Data Center Exclusive Use District and major site-plan approach;
- Norwalk — developer-funded project infrastructure and water-capacity planning;
- West Des Moines — examples of property-tax and infrastructure scale;
- Iowa municipal home-rule law — reason to distinguish contractual community-benefit terms from an unauthorized new local tax.

## Important legal-review flags

The draft itself identifies provisions needing attorney review, especially:

- zoning-district/overlay/special-use structure;
- restrictions on tenant or workload mix;
- community-benefit payment authority and structure;
- water-limit placement in ordinance vs. utility/development agreements;
- state/federal preemption involving air, water, generators, or environmental permits;
- public-record exemptions for security, tenant, financial, or infrastructure information.
