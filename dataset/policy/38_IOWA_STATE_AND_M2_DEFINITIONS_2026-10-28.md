# Iowa state-law definitions and proposed Cascade M-2 glossary

**Research date:** October 28, 2026. **Status:** independent policy research and discussion draft, **not adopted City law**.

This is the source-linked companion to the [October 26 M-2 proposed ordinance](https://github.com/ttbakiatwoam/cascade-data-center/blob/master/proposals/DRAFT-ORDINANCE-M2-DATA-CENTER-PERFORMANCE-STANDARDS-2026-10-26.md). It covers terms relevant to that ordinance, **not an exhaustive inventory of every definition in Iowa law**.

**How to read this table:** The 'State definition or statutory meaning' column contains an accurate short quotation where marked with quotation marks, or a **condensed restatement of the actual enacted state definition** where longer. Open the cited Iowa statute or rule for the **full, controlling verbatim wording and exclusions**. Statutory terms generally apply only **within the chapter, section, or administrative program specifying them**. This glossary does not automatically incorporate every definition into Cascade's zoning code, and Iowa's sales-tax definition does not control zoning eligibility.


> **Dictionary comparison methodology (October 31, 2026):** Three new columns provide concise, **original editorial comparisons**, not copyrighted verbatim dictionary entries. The Oxford public links are from the **Oxford Advanced Learner's Dictionary**, a distinct Oxford University Press work; the **Oxford English Dictionary (OED)** full text could not be accessed independently, so its other entries are linked as searches and explicitly marked unverified. Similarly, Merriam-Webster links are identified as checked entries or unverified searches. **Black's Law Dictionary, 12th edition (2024)** is the recognized U.S. standard legal dictionary, but its current entries require print or licensed Westlaw access; **Cornell Law School's Wex** supplies openly accessible legal comparisons where an actual Wex page was checked. A related Wex page is **not** asserted to define an exact compound term. For legal enforcement, the applicable Iowa statute, regulation, and adopted municipal ordinance control over a general dictionary gloss. [Oxford Learner's](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/) · [OED](https://www.oed.com/) · [Merriam-Webster](https://www.merriam-webster.com/) · [Black's 12th, publisher](https://legal.thomsonreuters.com/en/products/law-books/blacks-law-dictionary) · [Wex](https://www.law.cornell.edu/wex).

## D. Drafting rules and cross-checks

1. **Do not mislabel a paraphrase as the full verbatim statute.** The cited source contains the actual enacted text. Keep any adopted cross-reference tied to the specific controlling provision.
2. **Preserve existing City definitions unless deliberately amended.** The project has a transcription of Cascade's base §165.41, but the City Attorney should compare all later amendments and the certified current text.
3. **Avoid accidentally importing unrelated state programs.** For example, a 'major stationary source' in §455B.131 is an air-permitting term; a 'Major Industrial Facility' in the proposal is a local land-use threshold. Likewise, 'ambient air' is not ambient noise.
4. **Do not use pending bills as enacted definitions.** A proposed 2026 bill's wording about battery energy storage is not automatically Iowa law.
5. **Specify noise measurement requirements.** Final §165.11(G)(3) should identify Leq,1h, background subtraction, weighting, representative conditions, calibration, receptor positions, tonal detection, and limits on unrelated sound contributions. The 65/75 and 55/65/50/60 numerical proposals have **not** been changed in this update.
6. **Use a neutral physical-impact framework.** The 5 MW, 25,000-gallon, 2 MW generation, and 5 MWh storage triggers remain proposed policy choices, not state-law zoning thresholds. The 25,000-gallon figure aligns with a water-law term but does not mean every such site automatically needs the same Iowa DNR permit.
7. **Obtain City Attorney review.** Determine which definitions belong in ordinance §165.41, which belong in permit applications, and which require a separate fee, fire, environmental, or infrastructure instrument.
