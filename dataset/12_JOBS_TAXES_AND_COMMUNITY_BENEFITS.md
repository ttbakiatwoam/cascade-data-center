# Jobs, Taxes, and Community Benefits

> Economic claims should be separated into construction employment, permanent operations employment, taxable value, public incentives, utility/infrastructure investment, and contractual community benefits. None should be inferred from another data center without project-specific documentation.
>
> Last reviewed: 2026-10-06

## Construction jobs vs. permanent jobs

Data centers can support substantial temporary construction employment while using comparatively small permanent operations teams relative to their capital investment. For Cascade, both numbers matter and should be reported separately.

Useful project-specific measures include:

- construction job-years and expected construction period;
- number of permanent full-time-equivalent positions;
- job classifications;
- wage/salary ranges and benefits;
- local vs. commuting workforce assumptions;
- contractor/subcontractor labor;
- apprenticeships, internships, and training commitments;
- changes in staffing at later phases or after automation.

At the October 1, 2026 forum, Simple Mining projected approximately **20–25 permanent positions** supporting a 24/7/365 operation. The company discussed operations/site management, maintenance, electrical, mechanical, security, and IT-related roles. It projected an average salary of roughly **$75,000–$80,000**, with benefits, while explaining that entry-level positions would be below the average and some skilled roles could exceed $100,000. These are company projections, not a binding staffing agreement.

## October 1 investment and tax representations

Simple Mining projected approximately **$400 million** of total capital investment and said it intended to use local contractors and Iowa suppliers where practical. Capital investment is **not the same thing as assessed or taxable value**.

The company said it expected to use existing Iowa data-center sales/use-tax incentives and stated that it was not then seeking additional local or federal incentives. It did not provide a verified assessed value or annual City/county/school property-tax forecast. Those figures still require assessor/fiscal analysis and review of any final incentive or development agreement.

See [`28_SIMPLE_MINING_COMMUNITY_FORUM_2026-10-01.md`](28_SIMPLE_MINING_COMMUNITY_FORUM_2026-10-01.md).

## Workforce development

A locally beneficial agreement could connect the project to regional education and training in electrical, mechanical, HVAC/cooling, network/IT, facilities, controls, cybersecurity, and emergency-response skills. The project-generated model ordinance proposed good-faith local recruitment and collaboration with education/workforce programs, subject to legal review.

Such commitments are more useful when measurable: number of internships, apprenticeships, training seats, local interviews, or dollars committed rather than a generic promise to “hire local.”

## Automation and job-count uncertainty

Data-center operations are increasingly automated in software and, at some hyperscale operators, experimental robotics are being tested for physical maintenance. This does not prove that Simple Mining plans to automate Cascade jobs, but it reinforces the need to obtain the applicant's own permanent staffing estimate and not extrapolate employment from construction activity.

A separate Iowa news example previously reviewed involved nearly 200 state IT layoffs connected with a cloud/outsourcing transition. It is relevant only as general workforce-transition context; it is not evidence that a Cascade data center would create or eliminate that number of jobs.

## October 1 investment and workforce representations

Simple Mining estimated approximately **$400 million** in total project investment and said it intended to use local contractors and Iowa suppliers where practical. It also stated an intent to recruit locally and work with regional education/workforce partners, including NICC.

During the tax/incentive discussion, the company said it expected to use Iowa's existing data-center sales/use-tax incentives and represented that it was **not seeking additional local or federal incentives at that time**. The presenter discussed a potentially material property-tax base but did not provide a verified assessed value or annual City/county/school tax-revenue estimate.

The forum statements should be treated as company projections/positions until supported by an application, assessor analysis, development agreement, incentive filing, or other primary records. See [`28_SIMPLE_MINING_COMMUNITY_FORUM_2026-10-01.md`](28_SIMPLE_MINING_COMMUNITY_FORUM_2026-10-01.md).

## Property tax

The economic question for Cascade is not simply the developer's capital expenditure. The relevant public-fiscal data include:

- land and improvement assessed value;
- taxable value after any exemptions/abatements;
- tax levy allocation among city, county, school, and other taxing bodies;
- annual property-tax estimate by phase;
- depreciation/assessment treatment of equipment;
- TIF or other incentive effects;
- public-service and infrastructure costs.

No verified project-specific final assessed/taxable value or annual property-tax estimate has been located. The October 1 forum's roughly $400 million capital-investment projection should not be converted directly into taxable value.

## West Des Moines comparator

A West Des Moines Chamber fact sheet, based on City information, describes more than six million square feet of Microsoft data-center space and gives examples of large annual property-tax contributions and public-infrastructure investment from several projects. It also reports $21 million in Microsoft property taxes for FY2024–25.

These figures show that data centers **can** become major tax-base contributors in a specific negotiated and assessed context. They are not a forecast for Cascade, whose scale and facility type appear very different from Microsoft's campuses.

## Incentives and clawbacks

If Cascade or another public body offers tax abatement, TIF support, grants, utility concessions, infrastructure assistance, or other incentives, a complete public analysis should show:

1. gross public benefit;
2. incentive cost;
3. infrastructure/public-service cost;
4. net fiscal benefit;
5. promised jobs/investment;
6. performance milestones;
7. clawback or repayment terms if promises are not met.

## Community Benefit Agreement concept

The project-generated model ordinance proposed a contractual Community Benefit Agreement supporting items such as public infrastructure, water/sewer systems, public safety, workforce training, environmental protection, and other public purposes.

The model included illustrative payment formulas tied to energized MW, square footage, and financial performance. Those formulas were drafting concepts only and require attorney/fiscal review. Iowa municipalities cannot simply invent a new local tax outside state-authorized taxing power, which is why contractual development-agreement mechanisms were explored.
