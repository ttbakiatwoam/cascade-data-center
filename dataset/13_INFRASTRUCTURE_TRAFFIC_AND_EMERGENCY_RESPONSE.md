# Infrastructure, Traffic, and Emergency Response

> The relevant question is not whether data centers use infrastructure, but which additional infrastructure the Cascade project requires, who pays for it, and what long-term obligations remain with the public.
>
> Last reviewed: 2026-10-06

## Infrastructure categories

A complete site review can involve:

- electrical substations, feeders, transmission, and switchgear;
- fiber/telecommunications;
- public or private water connections;
- sanitary sewer and pretreatment;
- stormwater detention and drainage;
- roads, intersections, heavy-haul routes, and construction staging;
- fire access and hydrants;
- fuel storage and delivery;
- security fencing/access controls;
- exterior lighting;
- screening and landscaping.

## Developer-paid project infrastructure

The project has consistently evaluated the principle that costs created primarily by the new development should be assigned to the developer through lawful agreements rather than silently transferred to existing taxpayers or ratepayers.

Norwalk's Project West materials are a useful Iowa example: the public project page states that project-specific infrastructure is developer funded, while broader improvements may use project-generated financing mechanisms. Linn County's ordinance also uses road-use/restoration agreements for large data-center development.

## October 1 Cascade forum update

The forum supplied several preliminary infrastructure/emergency representations:

- the conceptual site plan showed a utility substation/electrical area, cooling yard, parking, screening, and one permanent building;
- Simple Mining said the site would use the industrial park's **regional stormwater basin** and represented that a separate onsite retention basin would not be required, subject to final civil review;
- approximately **15 diesel backup generators** were estimated, with exact models/capacity/testing still unresolved;
- the company said fire suppression would be built to code and expressed willingness to coordinate a required **emergency action plan** with local police, fire, and emergency-management personnel;
- the utility representative said project-driven electrical transmission/substation/distribution upgrades would be paid by Simple Mining upfront.

These are forum representations and should be checked against final civil drawings, utility agreements, fire plans, air permits, and emergency-response documents.

## Traffic

Construction traffic can be substantially different from normal operations. A useful traffic study should distinguish:

- excavation/concrete/steel/equipment delivery period;
- oversized/overweight loads;
- worker traffic at construction peak;
- normal operational staffing;
- routine replacement-equipment deliveries;
- fuel deliveries if generators are used;
- emergency access.

The Cascade project should provide construction and operational traffic estimates rather than applying truck counts from unrelated data centers.

## Stormwater and drainage

Industrial structures, equipment yards, roads, and parking increase impervious area. Site review should examine:

- pre-development and post-development runoff;
- detention/retention capacity;
- downstream culverts and drainage;
- erosion/sediment controls;
- fuel/coolant spill containment;
- emergency overflow;
- long-term maintenance responsibility.

## Fire and emergency response

Data-center emergency planning should be based on the actual equipment installed. Potential hazards include:

- electrical faults/arc hazards;
- transformers and switchgear;
- UPS batteries or battery-energy-storage systems;
- generator fuel;
- coolant/chemical leaks;
- server/electronics fires;
- severe weather;
- utility failure;
- security incidents.

The questions in this repository ask the applicant to disclose the proposed fire-suppression system, UPS/BESS type and capacity, hazardous materials/fuels, specialized responder needs, and whether project-specific training/equipment will be funded by the developer.

## Iowa comparator practices

### Linn County

The county's ordinance/public materials address road impacts, emergency planning, water, site-plan review, and large-project agreements.

### Jones County

Jones County requires emergency-operation information, utility documentation, and decommissioning-related planning. The ordinance is useful as a nearby rural/industrial comparator.

### Project-generated Cascade model

The June 2026 model ordinance proposed an emergency response plan covering fire, severe weather, security incidents, utility failure, generators, fuel leaks, chemical spills, battery-storage incidents, cooling failure, drought/water emergencies, medical emergencies, evacuation, access, and communications. It also proposed developer responsibility for specialized local-responder training/equipment reasonably required by the facility. This is a **proposal**, not adopted Cascade law.

## Recommended public record before construction

1. site/civil drawings;
2. road/traffic impact analysis;
3. utility-impact letters and cost allocation;
4. water/sewer capacity analysis;
5. stormwater plan;
6. generator and fuel plan;
7. UPS/BESS inventory;
8. fire-code and suppression design;
9. hazardous-material inventory appropriate for public disclosure;
10. emergency-response plan reviewed with local responders;
11. funding agreement for project-specific public infrastructure and emergency needs.
