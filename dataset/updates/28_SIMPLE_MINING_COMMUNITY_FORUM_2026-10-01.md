## Why this forum materially changes the project record

Earlier public discussion and March 2026 reporting described a substantially different, containerized Bitcoin-mining concept. At the October 1 forum, Simple Mining presented a more conventional permanent-building data-center concept and supplied preliminary figures for land area, building size, electrical demand, jobs, backup generation, cooling, investment, and construction timing.

The company also repeatedly described the current design as preliminary. Detailed engineering, final equipment selection, tenant/customer selection, acoustic modeling, agreements, permitting, and final site plans remain incomplete or subject to later review.

## Cooling and water

The company described the current concept as **closed-loop hydrocooling** with an **air-cooled condenser / dry heat-rejection approach** rather than an evaporative cooling tower. The presenter said the cooling loop would not rely on Cascade municipal water for ordinary heat rejection and that the coolant charge would be brought to the site.

The company said municipal water would instead be expected primarily for normal building/domestic uses and possibly limited humidification. It expressed willingness to accept an enforceable monthly or project water-use cap.

The presenter also described the coolant as a glycol solution and, in discussion of another Simple Mining facility, characterized it as biodegradable/corn-based. Product selection, coolant inventory, SDS documentation, exact annual/peak water demand, and final mechanical drawings were not supplied at the forum.

## Noise

The forum supplied more specificity than the earlier public record but **did not provide a final acoustic study**.

The presenter described hydro/liquid cooling as producing a comparatively low mechanical hum and referenced roughly **50 dB** in discussing cooling infrastructure and/or expected property-line conditions. During Q&A he clarified that the company was **not guaranteeing 50 dB continuously under every operating condition** and acknowledged hotter-weather cooling can be louder.

The company repeatedly said it wanted a clear, enforceable property-line standard and discussed an upper limit around **65 dB** as an example it believed it could design to. It also said backup generators would be enclosed.

No final frequency/tonality analysis, receptor model, hot-day full-load model, generator-test model, or independent post-construction acceptance test was presented. When asked about propagation modeling, the response indicated that modeling could be performed rather than demonstrating that a final study already existed.

## Electricity and Maquoketa Valley Electric Cooperative

A Maquoketa Valley Electric Cooperative representative said Simple Mining was already a cooperative member through another facility and was in discussions about the Cascade project.

Important utility representations made at the forum included:

- **No executed/signed service contract for the Cascade project as of October 1.**
- The proposed Cascade load was described as about **40 MW**.
- A transmission-capacity study conducted with Central Iowa Power Cooperative was described as finding adequate transmission capacity for the proposed load while reserving capacity for ordinary/native growth.
- Simple Mining would be expected to pay **100% of project-driven transmission, substation, and distribution upgrade costs upfront**, subject to later true-up.
- The utility said a load of this size would be required to bring/add **new generation** sufficient for the project.
- The utility representative stated that, if structured as described, the project should not increase rates for other cooperative members and could be neutral or beneficial.

These are utility statements from the forum. The underlying load study, interconnection/service agreement, generation arrangement, tariffs, and cost-allocation documents should be obtained before treating them as final contractual facts.

## Backup generation and emergency planning

Simple Mining described the generators as **backup power for outages**, not ordinary primary generation. Later Q&A identified diesel as the planned fuel and placed the approximate count near **15 units**, while emphasizing that detailed engineering was not final.

The presenter said generator operation/testing would be subject to state air-permitting requirements and indicated willingness to coordinate an emergency action plan with local responders. The exact generator model, aggregate MW, fuel storage, runtime, emissions, test cadence, and acoustic performance were not finalized. When asked specifically about testing frequency, the presenter did not give a firm schedule and said the issue would require follow-up.

## Jobs and workforce

The presenter estimated an average salary around **$75,000–$80,000**, with medical benefits, paid time off, and a 401(k), while noting that entry-level roles would be below the average and some skilled facility roles could exceed $100,000.

- decommissioning/cleanup financial assurance such as a surety bond;
