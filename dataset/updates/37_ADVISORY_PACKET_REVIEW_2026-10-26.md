## Changes actually proposed in October 26 draft

- Add generator equipment schedule and air-permit status documentation. Preserve IDNR/federal jurisdiction over air-emission limits.
- Require documented modeling of foreseeable simultaneous generator use, load conditions, and nearby receptors.
- Add a sixty-day post-startup acoustic evaluation option and a three-year recurring interval, subject to properly reviewed alternatives.
- Require an annual March 1 certification for qualifying Major Industrial Facilities.
- Provide for reasonable, documented independent technical review with safeguards against developer control, where legally authorized.
- Add proportionate pre-/post-construction road surveys and optional financial security through a separately authorized road-use agreement.
- Expand decommissioning provisions: updated professional estimate at least every three years, successor assurance, and owner/operator obligations where lawful.
- Add a specific noncodified city/county jurisdiction and two-mile legal-review section.

## Deliberately not adopted into the proposed language

- No county-wide or extraterritorial two-mile ban.
- No automatic transplant of Jackson County's 500-foot or 1,000-foot setbacks, which could make an industrial site unbuildable.
- No vibration ceiling copied from Jackson County's *draft* without engineering review.
- No local replacement of applicable IDNR or federal air permits.
- No arbitrary percentage surcharge on road security or unreviewed new fee.
- No claim that Greene County, Decorah or Jackson County provisions automatically govern Cascade.

## Remaining applicant evidence

Final generator list/model and emissions-permit determinations, hot-weather full-load and combined backup-noise model, surveyed layout, independent testing plan, utility agreement, fuel containment and emergency-response details, road-load plan, and third-party decommissioning estimate remain to be established by appropriate final submissions.
