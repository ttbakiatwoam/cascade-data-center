# November 12, 2026 — Deanna: November 11 definitions, voting and recording

**Document status:** Author-requested reconstruction of the earlier drafted email based on the author's November 12 instructions. **Not a verified verbatim sent message.** Sending, receipt and exact official roll calls remain unconfirmed. The recipient's private contact details are omitted.

**Intended recipient:** Deanna (Cascade City staff)  
**Draft subject:** November 11 Committee Meeting — Approved Definitions, Votes, and Recording Cutoff

## Reconstructed email text

Deanna,

I want to follow up with the definitions approved at the November 11 Data Center Advisory Committee meeting and make sure that the voting record is accurately documented.

The recording cuts off around the **60-minute mark**, and **the votes all occurred after that point**. The available recording therefore does not document the votes on these definitions.

The committee approved these four definitions **for the working draft ordinance**:

**Data Center:** A structure or building rehabilitated or constructed to house a group of networked server computers in one physical location in order to centralize the storage, management, and dissemination of data and information pertaining to a particular business, taxonomy, or body of knowledge. A data center business’s facility typically includes the mechanical and electrical systems, redundant or backup power supplies, redundant data communications connections, environmental controls, and fire suppression systems.

**Data Center Business:** An entity whose business among other businesses, is to operate or lease a data center in the city of cascade.

**Sustainable Design:** Construction design that reduces negative environmental effects and improves occupant health and comfort through resource conservation, waste minimization, and healthier environments.

**Hyperscale Data Center:** A Data Center meeting **both** of the following conditions:

1. **100,000 square feet or larger.**
2. **Electrical demand exceeding 60 megawatts.**

Hyperscale passed by a reported **5–3 vote**. Both thresholds apply (**AND**, not OR), and **“annually” was not part of the approved wording**. Sustainable Design passed **unanimously**. The exact vote totals for Data Center and Data Center Business need verification (reported as **5–3 or 6–2**).

The five recurring affirmative voters on contested motions were **Mike Delaney, Mark Weber, Andrew Saunders, Gayle Walter, and Doug Frey**. **Michael Rogers** and **Dave Mehrl** voted yes on some motions; **Rob McNally** consistently voted no on contested motions. That is a general participant-reported voting pattern, **not a certified individual roll call on every vote**. It does not contradict the unanimous Sustainable Design approval.

The remaining definitions were **not approved**. Most were **skipped, not defeated in a vote**, because they are already codified in Iowa law or covered in Cascade's existing zoning code and do not need separate local definitions.

Please ensure the official minutes reflect the approved definitions, distinguish skipped definitions from motions actually voted on, and capture the votes that the recording missed. The votes concern a **committee draft** and are not adoption of a City ordinance.

Thank you,

Andrew Saunders
