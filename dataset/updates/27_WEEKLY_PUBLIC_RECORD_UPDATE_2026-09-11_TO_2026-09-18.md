## 2. September 14 City Council packet — proposed Resolution 92-26

The September 14, 2026 City Council agenda listed proposed Resolution 92-26, titled:

**“Resolution Approving Clarification on Membership, Voting Authority, Quorum, Procedures and Rules for the Data Center Advisory Committee.”**

The accompanying City Administrator memo said clarification was needed before scheduling the committee's first meeting.

### Proposed voting structure

The packet proposed **eight voting seats**:

- two City Council representatives;
- two Cascade Economic Development Corporation representatives;
- two Planning & Zoning Commission representatives; and
- two citizen representatives.

The packet also listed non-voting technical/advisory participants. This public dataset continues to omit individual committee-member names.

### Proposed authority and scope

The draft says the committee is advisory and may research and make recommendations concerning:

- zoning and land use;
- water and wastewater;
- electricity;
- cooling systems;
- noise;
- environmental issues;
- fire protection;
- construction;
- economic impacts;
- public safety;
- community safeguards; and
- proposed ordinance language.

It also says the committee would **not** have authority to:

- approve or deny a project;
- spend City funds;
- execute agreements;
- grant permits; or
- exercise authority reserved to the City Council or another governmental body.

Proposed ordinance language would be reported to the Planning & Zoning Commission and City Council.

### Proposed quorum and meeting procedure

The draft provides for:

- a quorum of five of the eight voting members;
- selection of a Chair and Vice Chair by voting members;
- posted agendas;
- official minutes;
- compliance with Iowa Code Chapter 21;
- public attendance/observation;
- no standing public-comment area on committee agendas; and
- reasonable rules against interference or disruption.

The draft characterizes the committee meetings as working sessions rather than debate forums.

### Adoption status

As of the September 18 review, the City's 2026 Council records page lists the September 14 agenda/packet but does **not** yet list September 14 meeting minutes.

Accordingly, this repository classifies Resolution 92-26 as **proposed packet language / disposition awaiting official confirmation**.

The unsigned packet draft contains a “passed and approved” signature block dated September 14. That pre-filled language is not, by itself, treated here as proof of adoption.

## 3. Resolution 57-26 moratorium — public-document duration discrepancy

A fresh comparison of the June 22 agenda packet with the subsequently posted meeting minutes identified a material discrepancy in the public record.

### June 22 memo

The City Administrator's June 18 memo describes a **short-term moratorium** and says that if an ordinance were approved sooner than 90 days, the Council could rescind the moratorium.

### June 22 packet draft

The packet draft of Resolution 57-26 contains two different duration formulations:

- a recital says the Council intends the moratorium to remain in effect **indefinitely unless and until terminated** by later Council resolution; while
- Section 5 says the moratorium continues for **90 days or until terminated** by later Council resolution.

### June 22 adopted-action record

The posted June 22 City Council minutes record a different action: the Council approved Resolution 57-26 as a **12-month moratorium** and added **60–90-day benchmarks**.

### Dataset treatment

The packet draft should not be treated as the final operative text where it conflicts with the minutes.

Until a signed/executed final Resolution 57-26 reflecting the Council's amendment is obtained, the safest public-record description is:

> The June 22, 2026 City Council minutes record approval of a 12-month data-center construction moratorium with 60–90-day benchmarks, while the pre-meeting packet draft contains conflicting 90-day and indefinite-duration language.

The signed final resolution remains a useful document to request.

## 4. Municipal conflict-of-interest statutes — narrower than some public claims

Recent discussion raised questions about whether participation by particular committee members automatically violates Iowa conflict-of-interest law. The statutory text is more specific and should be applied to concrete facts rather than labels.

### Iowa Code §362.5 — interest in public contract

Section 362.5 applies to a **city officer or employee** having a direct or indirect interest in a **contract, job of work, material, profits, or services** involving the officer's or employee's city, subject to listed exceptions.

This section is important if a City contract or other covered financial interest is actually involved. It does **not**, on its face, establish that every advisory-committee appointment or organizational affiliation is automatically a prohibited public-contract interest.

### Iowa Code §362.6 — conflict of interest in a vote

Section 362.6 addresses the effect of a conflict of interest when a city officer votes on a measure. It provides, among other things, that an officer may decline to vote because of a conflict and that the declaration is entered in the record.

Whether a specific person is disqualified, whether a particular interest constitutes a conflict, and what consequence follows depend on the person's legal status, the specific matter before the body, and the underlying facts.

### Repository rule

This dataset will not label a committee participant's service “illegal,” “corrupt,” or a statutory conflict solely because of organizational affiliation. Any such conclusion should be supported by:

1. the exact statute;
2. the legal status of the individual under that statute;
3. the specific contract, measure, financial interest, or duty involved; and
4. where appropriate, City Attorney or other qualified legal analysis.

## 5. Open meetings, public records, and confidentiality

### Open meetings

Iowa Code Chapter 21 remains the principal procedural framework for the City-created advisory committee. The proposed Resolution 92-26 expressly says the committee will follow Chapter 21, post agendas, hold open sessions, and maintain minutes.

Chapter 21 generally requires covered governmental-body meetings to be open unless a statutory closed-session exception applies.

### Public observation vs. comment

The proposed Resolution 92-26 permits public attendance but does not create a standing public-comment segment for committee meetings. Iowa open-meetings law distinguishes the right to observe a covered meeting from a general right to speak at every meeting.

### Public records and confidentiality

Iowa Code Chapter 22 defines public records broadly for cities and their boards, commissions, councils, and committees, subject to statutory confidentiality provisions and other lawful exceptions.

Accordingly, any confidentiality agreement, NDA, or request to withhold committee-related material should be evaluated against Chapter 22 and any specific statutory exemption. A private agreement should not be treated as a substitute for identifying the legal basis for confidentiality.

This is a legal-research point, not a conclusion about any specific agreement.
