# Simple Mining and Project Scope

> Source type warning: statements about Simple Mining's fleet and technology are company claims unless independently corroborated. Cascade-specific descriptions from press coverage are not substitutes for final plans or permits.
>
> Last reviewed: 2026-10-06

## What Simple Mining publicly describes itself as

Simple Mining's current documentation describes an Iowa-based company focused on selling, hosting, and repairing Bitcoin miners. Its hosting materials describe a business model in which customers place mining equipment at company-operated facilities and the hosting service provides power infrastructure, cooling, networking, security, monitoring, facility operations, and standard maintenance.

Current public company pages are not perfectly synchronized: the hosting page advertises 11 Iowa data centers and more than 150 MW under management, while an August 2026 company timeline described 10 active facilities and 150 MW. This should be treated as a company-page timing/count difference, not silently reconciled.

## Relevant operating examples

Simple Mining's Site 8 page describes a 50+ MW hydro-cooled location hosting more than 10,000 miners. That example demonstrates that Simple Mining has experience with liquid/hydro cooling at meaningful scale, but it does **not** establish the engineering design, water use, or noise profile of the proposed Cascade site.

The company's cooling and water articles say its current fleet uses engineered airflow and closed-loop cooling approaches and makes specific low-water-use claims. Those claims are useful evidence of the company's stated operating model, but an independent project review should verify Cascade's exact cooling loop, external heat rejection, makeup-water needs, coolant, peak conditions, and metering.

## Cascade proposal: early concept vs. October 1 concept

March 2026 trade reporting described the proposed Cascade project as roughly four acres with around 40 containerized cryptocurrency-mining units of roughly 160 square feet apiece. At the October 1, 2026 community forum, Simple Mining presented a materially different preliminary concept: a permanent data-center building of about **60,000 square feet** on approximately **15.2 gross acres**, with a proposed electrical load around **40 MW**.

The company said the project had shifted from the earlier Bitcoin-mining concept toward a conventional data-center development and that no tenant/customer was committed as of the forum. The presenter said the building could support AI-related computing, but the forum does **not** establish a signed AI tenant or a workload-specific commitment.

Other October 1 company projections included **20–25 permanent jobs**, approximately **$75,000–$80,000 average salary**, roughly **$400 million** in project investment, and about **15 diesel backup generators** subject to final engineering. A 12–18 month construction-to-operation window was estimated from the point the project is authorized to proceed.

These figures are company representations and estimates from a preliminary plan, not final permit/application values. The raw source is a YouTube automated-caption WebVTT transcript and is not human-verified.

Accordingly, comparisons should match at least these variables before being treated as useful evidence:

- electrical load;
- physical footprint;
- containerized vs. building design;
- workload type;
- cooling architecture;
- external heat rejection;
- water source and use;
- generator/UPS design;
- distance to residences;
- applicable noise limits;
- climate;
- utility arrangement;
- final buildout.

## Questions that remain open for Cascade

The October 1 forum narrowed several questions but did not replace final project documents. Still needed are:

1. final utility-confirmed initial/maximum load and executed service/generation agreements;
2. final site, architectural, electrical, and equipment plans;
3. tenant/customer identity and enforceable treatment of future workload/physical-impact changes;
4. final cooling drawings, coolant/SDS, and quantified average/peak/annual water use;
5. project-specific acoustic model and post-construction acceptance testing;
6. final generator models, capacity, fuel storage, emissions, runtime, and testing schedule;
7. UPS/BESS design and fire-safety plan;
8. binding staffing/wage commitments, if any;
9. assessed/taxable value, annual tax revenue, and final incentive treatment;
10. ownership/operator structure, development agreement, expansion controls, and decommissioning responsibility.
