## Three distinct USGBC paths

| LEED document / credit | Content and eligibility | Does it directly protect Cascade homes? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **LEED v4.1 BD+C: Data Centers**, **Indoor Environmental Quality Acoustic Performance** | **Optional one-point credit** for acoustics of **occupied spaces within the project**. Meet two of three relevant areas: HVAC background noise, sound transmission, reverberation. Compliance is supported by calculations/measurements or qualified acoustical documentation. | **No specific exterior property-line or bedroom limit** follows from this credit. |
| **LEED v5 BD+C: New Construction**, **Occupant Experience, Option 4 Sound Environment** | Current USGBC guidance says new whole-building data centers may use v5 **New Construction**. Optional **1-point path** maps desired acoustic conditions in indoor/outdoor spaces; **2-point path** uses calculations, modeling and/or measurements to demonstrate acoustic design criteria in at least 75% of occupied spaces and all classrooms/core learning spaces as applicable. | It considers the project's indoor/outdoor sound environment, but **is not an automatic off-site residential dBA cap**. |
| **USGBC Pilot Credit 57: Enhanced Acoustical Performance – Exterior Noise Control** | **Voluntary pilot with changed criteria.** The early version specified project noise **at least 5 dBA below pre-project ambient Ldn and no more than 60 dBA**. The **June 4, 2015 revision** instead calls for a site noise assessment and separate property-boundary standards: **adjoining residential buildings, Leq(8h) no more than 45 dBA, plus Lmax no greater than existing ambient or 60 dBA**; **other boundaries, Ldn or CNEL no more than 60 dBA**. Emergency equipment is exempt from these numeric pilot limits but requires a testing plan. | **Potential voluntary exterior-noise comparator only**, if the project and credit are eligible. No Simple Mining commitment or applicable credit registration was verified. |

### Historical and revised Pilot Credit 57 criteria: correction to this research

This file previously reported the **older 5 dBA-below-ambient approach** as if it were the only published rule. **That was incomplete.** The [original USGBC exterior-noise pilot PDF](https://www.usgbc.org/sites/default/files/Enhanced%20acoustical%20performance%20-%20exterior%20noise%20control.pdf) and archived version specify: new project sound at the nearest property line/right of way at least **5 dBA below the pre-project ambient Ldn**, with an upper bound of **60 dBA**.

On **June 4, 2015**, Pilot Credit 57 was **revised**. The [revised USGBC credit text reproduced by LEEDuser](https://leeduser.buildinggreen.com/credit/Pilot-Credits/EQpc57) instead specifies:

- **At a boundary adjacent to residential buildings:** exterior **Lmax may not exceed existing ambient levels and may not exceed 60 dBA**. Project exterior **Leq, 8-hour must be no more than 45 dBA**.
- **At a boundary not adjacent to residential buildings:** **Ldn or CNEL no more than 60 dBA**.
- **Site assessment:** qualitative assessment of nearby sources and sensitive receivers and/or at least **24-hour noise measurement** at one location, with documentation of how existing ambient was established.
- **Emergency equipment:** numeric restrictions in this pilot do not cover emergency generators, but their **periodic testing plan** and timing rationale must be documented.
- **Interior bedroom criteria:** an additional requirement for **residential project buildings**. These limits do **not** automatically turn the proposed nonresidential data center into a regulated residence.

**Which version would apply to Cascade?** Not established. The project has not identified its LEED rating system or credits. The pilot's current availability, eligibility, registration date, and version would require confirmation by USGBC/GBCI. **LEED certification itself does not obligate a project to select this optional credit.**

This research project's author reports a **personal ambient sound-meter reading of approximately 60 dB** near the area. The reading is a **personal observation**, not a calibrated, independently repeated acoustic baseline. The location, measurement time, A/C weighting, sample duration, equipment calibration, measured sound sources and weather were not provided in the source record. It should not be assumed to mean **60 dBA Ldn** or **60 dBA Leq(8h)**.

Nearby [Cascade Manufacturing](https://cascade-mfg-co.com/about/) was established as a subsidiary of Cascade Lumber and manufactures truss components. A [2026 U.S. Department of Labor job posting for Cascade Lumber](https://seasonaljobs.dol.gov/jobs/H-400-25364-516568), for production at **404 Monroe Street, Cascade**, described **truss assembly, hammers, staple guns, banding, presses and nailing**, with one listed schedule **3:30 p.m. to 1:00 a.m.**, Monday-Thursday, subject to changes. [Cascade Manufacturing also publicly advertised a second-shift supervisor](https://careercentral.pitt.edu/jobs/cascade-manfacturing-company-2nd-shift-supervisor/). These sources establish **late-night production activity**, not continuous 24/7 nail-gun/riveter operation; riveters were not confirmed by the cited DOL posting.

**Important inference limit:** Hearing one distant factory does **not** establish that every business between it and a home is audible. Different sources have different acoustic power, tone, operating hours and shielding. Nor does existing industrial noise mean residents **cannot legitimately complain** about an additional source. Added sound can increase overall levels, introduce distinctive tonal noise or disturb sleep, even if another factory is already audible.

**Useful baseline conclusion:** Industrial noise already exists locally. A future impact study needs **multi-day representative day/night ambient measurements**, separate source attribution where possible, time-specific **dBA/dBC**, spectral/tonal evidence, and **cumulative** levels after construction. Identify changes attributable to the proposed development, and impose neutral, enforceable impact-based rules on all similarly situated **M-2** facilities. Avoid promising that a simple arithmetic deduction from a single 60 dB reading establishes compliance.

### Metrics cannot be interchanged

- The early **5 dBA-below-ambient Ldn** criterion is **not the same** as the revised **45 dBA Leq(8h)** residential-adjacent boundary limit.
- **Lmax**, **Leq(8h)**, **Ldn** and **CNEL** represent different acoustic quantities and assessment periods. A one-time **60 dB** observation cannot be plugged into all of them.
- The pilot reports **dBA** and does not itself provide a **dBC low-frequency** or measured ground-vibration criterion.
- Independent measurements and any legally enforceable limits must be specified for each receptor and operating state, including nighttime and worst-case mechanical cooling.

## Main standards versus permits and neighborhood protection

LEED design and construction credits are generally **one-time voluntary certification achievements**. They are not a substitute for enforceable **long-term** performance conditions, independent post-construction testing, nighttime property-line or residential receptor limits, low-frequency/tonal analysis, corrective action, or regulated generator testing.

Relevant technical distinctions:

- **dBA vs. dBC:** LEED's cited exterior pilot criterion is A-weighted; **dBC retains more low-frequency energy** and can be added as a complementary local requirement if legally justified.
- **Ldn vs. Leq/nighttime and Lmax:** Ldn is a weighted day-night average; it can miss short tonal/start-stop events and does not replace peak-noise and quiet-bedroom assessments.
- **Ambient versus project noise:** A requirement relative to pre-project ambient is not interchangeable with a fixed permit threshold and depends on proper baseline measurements.
- **Variable cooling:** Data-center IT operates continuously, but dry-cooler fans may stage/cycle or change speed with outdoor conditions. Pumps, fans or electrical equipment may remain audible. Test realistic **quiet, normal, hot-summer maximum and night** operating scenarios; do not infer either a constant hum or complete nightly shutdown.
- **Generator exception:** The pilot does not itself cap emergency generation noise, though a local ordinance/development agreement can set neutral testing hours, measurement and operating requirements for similarly situated industrial facilities.
