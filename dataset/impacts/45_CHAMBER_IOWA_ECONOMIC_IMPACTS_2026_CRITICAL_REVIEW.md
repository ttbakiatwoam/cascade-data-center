# U.S. Chamber (October 2026): Iowa data-center impacts and applicability to Cascade

> **Reviewed November 4, 2026.** Source: U.S. Chamber of Commerce, *The Community and Economic Impacts of Data Centers* (October 15, 2026). [Original PDF](https://www.uschamber.com/assets/documents/The-Community-and-Economic-Impacts-of-Data-Centers.pdf) · [Publisher](https://www.uschamber.com/infrastructure/the-community-and-economic-impacts-of-data-centers) · [Supplied copy checksum and provenance](../../sources/economic-studies/US_CHAMBER_DATA_CENTER_IMPACTS_2026_SOURCE_RECORD.md). This is a **modeled 50 MW comparator, not a forecast of the 40 MW Cascade project**.

## Findings: Iowa, one hypothetical 50 MW data center

The report's **page 32** contains a state-specific model, not a table of site employees. Its impact figures are average **annual** changes within each modeled phase. [1]

| Iowa impact (report p. 32) | Two-year construction phase, annual | Twenty-year operations, annual |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Employment supported, all industries | **4,947** | **647** |
| State labor force | 1,534 | 699 |
| State GDP | **$592,470,000** | **$138,947,000** |
| State personal income | **$343,235,000** | **$96,904,000** |
| Construction-industry employment | 1,172 | 0 |
| Information-industry employment | 18 | 53 |
| Retail-trade employment | 1,276 | 188 |
| Utility-industry employment | 6 | 11 |

**Critical distinction:** The 647 includes indirect/induced jobs throughout Iowa; it is **not 647 permanent direct jobs at the facility or within Cascade**. Page 68 assumes just **50 direct on-site data-center employees** (plus separately modeled utility employees). The label "permanent" in the page-32 headline should be read in light of this model definition and 20-year operations period. Avoid multiplying 647 by the industry average wage or treating the figure as municipal payroll. [1][2]

## What the Chamber assumes, compared with the developer's public description

| Parameter | Chamber's modeled 50 MW site | Cascade project discussion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Capacity | **50 MW** | **Approximately 40 MW** publicly represented |
| Capital spending during construction | **$1.69 billion** over 2 years | **Approximately $400 million** publicly projected by Simple Mining |
| Direct on-site data-center jobs | **50** | **20–25** publicly projected at October 1 forum |
| Electrical utility workers | 5 additional | No signed local staffing commitment demonstrated |
| Water utility workers | 5 additional | No signed local staffing commitment demonstrated |
| Road and local infrastructure | **$60 million** modeled | Actual Cascade budget, public share and agreements unknown |
| Grid improvements | **$20 million, assumed developer-funded** | MVEC described developer-funded project upgrades; executed agreement remains unverified |
| Water-system upgrades | **$10 million, assumed developer-funded when water cooling is used** | Actual water system needs and cooling design remain unverified |
| Hardware replacement | **$1 billion every fifth operating year** | Equipment ownership, refresh spending and local sourcing unverified |
| Operating horizon | 20 years | Not a binding site life or local employment guarantee |

Chamber assumptions are detailed on **pages 68–69**. Cascade comparisons are **company representations**, documented separately in the [October 1 forum source review](../updates/28_SIMPLE_MINING_COMMUNITY_FORUM_2026-10-01.md) and [jobs/tax research](12_JOBS_TAXES_AND_COMMUNITY_BENEFITS.md). They are not final agreements. The Chamber's model includes direct spending, suppliers, purchases by workers, and induced economy-wide activity using REMI PI+. An **80% pro-rata conversion from 50 MW to 40 MW is not a validated local forecast**, particularly with the reported capital assumptions so far apart. [2][3]

## Wages: useful statewide industry context, not promised employee pay

Page 9 reports Iowa **$67,340** annual earnings across all industries and **$117,260** annual earnings for NAICS 518210 computing infrastructure/data processing/hosting, a premium of **$49,920 (74.1%)**. Page 11 attributes the figures to **U.S. Census Bureau Quarterly Workforce Indicators, 2025 Q4**, and says earnings **include bonuses and other compensation** without adjustment for job/occupation mix. They are **not** a specific wage promise by Simple Mining; other forms of industrial computing may have different occupational and pay mixes. [4]

## Revenue: four non-Iowa county examples do not forecast Cascade receipts

The report (pp. 12–16) offers **gross** five-year fiscal scenarios for **Dallas County, TX ($174m)**; **Prince William County, VA ($114m)**; **Fulton County, GA ($111m)**; and **Franklin County, OH ($96m)**. They model sales, real-estate and business-property tax effects, **before abatements/exemptions**, with simplified tax/asset treatments. Pages 70–71 acknowledge local tax-law differences. The Prince William County example applies a **6% sales-tax rate described as including both state and local components**; any claim that all of it accrues to county government needs separate verification.

**No county fiscal case is from Iowa.** These totals cannot determine Cascade municipal receipts, Delaware/Dubuque County taxes, franchise fees, utility payments in lieu of taxes, or local school revenue. The [Cascade MVEC 40 MW franchise-fee model](36_CASCADE_ELECTRIC_FRANCHISE_FEE_AND_40MW_FISCAL_SCENARIOS.md) is a **separate project-generated scenario**, not Chamber evidence. [5]

## Resource and ordinance implications

- **Electricity rates:** On p. 4 the Chamber cites E3, LBNL and Brattle to argue increased data-center demand does not *necessarily* cause rising statewide rates. Those studies do **not** test MVEC/CIPCO pricing for Cascade or prove a neutral local cost allocation.
- **Water:** The modeled $10m water upgrades are **conditional on using water for cooling**; the report does **not** provide Cascade gallons per year or verify the local cooling plan.
- **Roads and utilities:** Assumed $60m roads / $20m grid / conditional $10m water upgrades (p. 68) are **model inputs, not City-funded costs or signed developer commitments**.
- **Noise/land use:** Although pp. 2 and 6 acknowledge noise, traffic, water, siting and community trust, this source provides **no enforceable dBA/dBC limits, indoor baseline study, or Iowa zoning-law rule**.
- **Direct employment:** The model treats industry-wide job effects broadly; preserve the company's 20–25 direct projected jobs, and separately label any community hypothetical of 40 workers at $80,000.

## Assessment and recommendation

**Strengths:** Public issuer and date; Iowa-specific industry breakdown; explicit 50 MW assumptions and time horizon; labor-income and supply-chain context; recognizes local externalities; full methodology pages.

**Limits:** Advocacy-oriented sponsor; forecast model rather than observed outcomes; the hypothetical spend is far above the Cascade public estimate; indirect effects may disperse across the state/nation; four fiscal examples outside Iowa; no project's local tax structure, rate design, costs or environmental measurements. A positive economic model does **not** erase local concerns or excuse missing commitments.

**Committee-ready phrasing:** *The Chamber models one larger 50 MW, $1.69 billion Iowa data center as supporting about 4,947 statewide jobs per year during construction and 647 statewide jobs per year during operations. It assumes just 50 direct on-site employees. Those figures cannot be treated as guaranteed jobs, wages or revenues within Cascade.*

**Use:** Include as a clearly qualified economic-benefits source in the research library, fiscal evaluation, and public FAQ. Ask for an **independent Cascade-specific net fiscal estimate** of taxable values, exemptions, actual direct jobs, local procurement, utility fees, infrastructure costs and binding developer obligations. **Do not modify the M-2 ordinance's acoustic and zoning standards based on this economic model.**
