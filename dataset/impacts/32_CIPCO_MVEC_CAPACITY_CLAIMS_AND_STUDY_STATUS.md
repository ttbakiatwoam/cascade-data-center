# CIPCO and MVEC: Generation, Transmission, and Cascade Capacity Study

> Last reviewed: 2026-10-13. **Scope:** Preliminary approximately 40 MW Simple Mining data-center concept in Cascade, Iowa, not a generic hyperscale data center.
>
> **Evidence status:** Official CIPCO/MVEC publications and attributed comments by MVEC CEO Jeremy Richert at the October 1, 2026 Cascade public forum. The forum's preserved transcript was generated automatically by YouTube and has **not** been certified verbatim. The project-specific CIPCO/MVEC study was **referenced publicly but has not been located or independently reviewed**.
>
> The report described on October 1 should not be confused with CIPCO's system-wide Integrated Resource Plan or CIPCO's later procurement and generation planning.

## What is being compared?

- **Generation / resource adequacy:** whether a utility has or can procure accredited generating capacity to satisfy expected demand and reliability requirements.
- **Transmission capacity:** whether the high-voltage network can carry additional load under modeled conditions.
- **Distribution and substation service:** physical upgrades and equipment needed to deliver power to the specific customer.
- **Service availability:** a commercial and technical result requiring completed studies, contracts, generation arrangements, schedules, tariffs, and potentially additional approvals.

A shortage of already-secured generating capacity does **not**, by itself, contradict a study indicating sufficient transmission capability for a conditional future project. Neither one guarantees final service.

## What CIPCO's 2025 capacity shortfall does and does not prove

CIPCO's February 24, 2025 [Integrated Resource Plan article](https://www.cipco.net/news/irp-verifies-new-generation-needed) says its system faced an immediate shortfall of **more than 150 MW of capacity**, with about 200 MW required by 2030. This concerns its generation resource portfolio and planning obligations. It predates the Cascade forum and does not mention a rejection of the proposed Cascade data center.

A [January 20, 2026 capacity RFP](https://www.naema.com/news/aces---cipco---miso-capacity-rfp) documents CIPCO's effort to obtain capacity in MISO Zone 3. That is further evidence of planning and procurement, not an assurance that future load can be served without conditions.

CIPCO's [Hickory Grove project page](https://www.cipco.net/hickory-grove-generating-station) describes approximately **240 MW**, planned to be online in **late 2029**, while expressly denying that the project depends on a single data center. It should **not** be treated as approved or committed generation for Simple Mining.

## Locate the CIPCO/MVEC Cascade study: search result and outstanding evidence

**Current status: referenced in a named public presentation; actual study not found in publicly indexed records as of October 13, 2026.** Searches of public CIPCO/MVEC pages, documents indexed on the web, and relevant City/project materials did not locate an identifiable report for a 40 MW Cascade load. The absence of a public copy does **not** establish that the study is nonexistent or that the utilities have refused its release.

CIPCO's broader Integrated Resource Plan and earlier transmission project documents are **different studies**. They cannot substitute for the Cascade-specific load assessment.

### Request to CIPCO and MVEC

Ask both utilities, ideally copied to City staff and the advisory committee, for:

1. The full **Cascade / Simple Mining ~40 MW transmission or capacity study** cited by Jeremy Richert at the October 1 forum, or a nonconfidential summary if grid-security restrictions apply.
2. Exact study title/ID, preparation date, authors, entities that commissioned or reviewed it, planning horizon and modeled load shape.
3. **Study conclusion in writing:** maximum additional MW supported, conditions, headroom for native growth, required transmission/substation/distribution upgrades, and contingencies evaluated.
4. What is **already available** versus what becomes available **only after upgrades and new generation arrangements**.
5. Generation needed for the full 40 MW, procurement or new-build plan, commercial commitments, and any conditions precedent.
6. Confirmation of whether CIPCO agrees with Richert's October 1 descriptions; any material developments since that meeting.
7. What portions may be published, and a written explanation of any specific withheld sections rather than an unsupported blanket confidentiality assertion.
8. Whether a later service agreement, engineering amendment, or revised study has changed the result.

## Evidence distinctions and claims not established

- **Established:** CIPCO publicly reported a system-wide shortage of accredited generating capacity in 2025.
- **Established as an attributable statement:** MVEC CEO said the jointly evaluated **transmission** system was adequate for roughly 40 MW under a study.
- **Established as an attributable statement:** the same CEO said **new generation** would be required and the developer would prepay project-driven network upgrades.
- **Not independently verified:** the study's actual calculations, exact transmission headroom, feasibility dates, cost estimates, and final grid/utility commitments.
- **Not established:** an attributable CIPCO officer or published CIPCO meeting record expressly saying **the Cascade project cannot receive electric service**, or that CIPCO rejected the project.
- **Unsubstantiated as a conflict:** a report that someone heard CIPCO say "there isn't enough power" without the speaker, date, meeting and whether they meant generation today, future supply, or local transmission.
