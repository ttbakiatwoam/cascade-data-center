# Naval Shipboard Noise, Sleep, and Extended-Exposure Research

> Reviewed: 2026-10-13. **Research comparator, not a measurement of Cascade.** These are peer-reviewed occupational and sleep studies involving aircraft carriers, other naval vessels, and submarines. **Do not treat prolonged shipboard exposure or military selection as proof that an audible industrial hum is either safe or harmful at nearby homes.**
>
> **Duration caveat:** An at-sea deployment can last months, but studies below commonly collected noise dosimetry over 1-5 days or sleep information over 30-70 days. They did **not** directly measure every day of an uninterrupted six-to-twelve-month noise exposure. Do not describe these designs as year-long controlled tests.

[Main Cascade noise and acoustics research](08_NOISE_AND_ACOUSTICS.md) | [Issue-by-issue evidence review](../evidence/31_PUBLIC_OBJECTIONS_EVIDENCE_REVIEW.md) | [Website summary](../../docs/public-objections.md)

## Central findings

- **Hearing injury:** Strongest occupational evidence concerns exposure at high A-weighted levels, including flight-deck and machinery operations. Hearing-damage thresholds are not the same as health-protective residential nighttime limits.
- **Sleep and well-being:** Noise reaching sleeping areas can impede auditory recovery and disturb sleep. Objective maritime studies link cabin noise and the frequency of noise events to sleep movement and efficiency. Sound is one factor among watch shifts, lighting, workload, temperature, and stress.
- **Duration:** A 72-hour laboratory experiment cannot resolve years-long exposures, but neither does a several-week submarine mission isolate long-term noise effects. Study design, controls and acoustic similarity matter as much as calendar duration.
- **Submarines:** Published submarine deployment studies document sleep and fatigue outcomes during sustained isolation, but the selected studies generally do **not** measure in-berth dBA/dBC or isolate machinery hum as the cause. One French 70-day patrol with controlled conditions showed stable measured sleep between days 21 and 51.
- **Cascade:** At the October 1, 2026 forum, Simple Mining supplied preliminary noise statements, not a completed spectrum-based model or monitoring record at nearby residences. Naval sound levels must not be substituted for site-specific predictions.

## Aircraft carriers: measured sound and auditory-health outcomes

### 1. Schaal et al. (2019), *Living at Work: 24-hour Noise Exposure Aboard US Navy Aircraft Carriers*

- **Design:** Personal noise dosimetry for **59** sailors in seven occupational exposure groups aboard a Nimitz-class carrier during a **January 23-28, 2014** underway period. The published paper appeared in 2019. It measured 24-hour, on-duty and off-duty exposure, not an entire deployment.
- **Results:** **93%** exceeded the ACGIH **80 dBA 24-hour occupational** threshold; **68%** exceeded the **85 dBA eight-hour occupational** threshold; and **61%** exceeded the **70 dBA off-duty 'effective quiet' threshold** used for recovery from temporary noise-induced hearing shifts.
- **Interpretation:** Flight deck and support trades had especially high exposure, and even off-duty areas did not always permit quiet recovery. The authors identified risks of acoustic trauma and permanent hearing injury.
- **Limit:** Exceedance is **not proof that 93% developed hearing loss**. The **70 dBA metric is a hearing-recovery comparison**, not a permissible nighttime level for Cascade homes. These were predominantly very loud shipboard workplaces and living areas.

Primary: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30855086/ ; full text https://academic.oup.com/annweh/article/63/3/316/5373581

### 2. Schaal, Majar & Hunter (2019), *Sound Level Measurements in Berthing Areas of an Aircraft Carrier*

- **Design:** **60 sound measurements** taken at **eight berthing/sleeping locations** on a Nimitz-class aircraft carrier during flight and non-flight operations; acoustic analysis included octave bands.
- **Results:** Sleeping areas averaged **6.4 dBA louder during flight operations** than during non-flight operations; forward and mid-ship areas were roughly **15 dBA louder** than aft locations. **72% of area measurements** exceeded the **70 dBA auditory-recovery comparison**.
- **Interpretation:** Sound exposure depends on **where people sleep**, even inside the same vessel. The investigators considered the levels high enough to present sleep-related risks, but they did not conduct a randomized study proving illness.
- **Limit:** **72% of measurements is not 72% of sailors**. A high carrier-berthing reading cannot serve as a forecast for Cascade residences.

Primary: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31260521/ ; DOI https://doi.org/10.1093/annweh/wxz051

### 3. Rovig, Bohnker & Page (2004), *Hearing Health Risk in a Population of Aircraft Carrier Flight Deck Personnel*

- **Design:** **205** sailors matched by years of service: **76 flight deck, 77 engineering, 52 administrative**.
- **Results:** Flight-deck noise averaged **109 dBA for an approximately 11.5-hour workday**. Hearing impairment as defined in the paper appeared in **17% of flight-deck**, **27% of engineering**, and **4% of administrative** personnel.
- **Interpretation:** Shipboard job type and cumulative noise exposure matter. The percentages do not prove a strictly monotonic dose-response or isolate every cause of hearing impairment.
- **Limit:** This is evidence on **intense occupational noise and hearing thresholds**, not on low-level residential infrasound or a roughly 50-65 dB preliminary data-center estimate.

Primary: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15281670/ ; DOI https://doi.org/10.7205/MILMED.169.6.429

### 4. Schaal et al. (2019), *Noise at Sea: Characterization of Extended Shift Noise Exposures among U.S. Navy Aircraft Carrier Support Personnel*

- **Design:** 47 carrier personnel in support groups, comparing **12-hour duty**, **12-hour off-duty**, and **24-hour** measurements during an at-sea qualification period.
- **Results:** Mean measured 24-hour levels among exposure groups spanned about **69-88 dBA**; **22%** exceeded the 80 dBA 24-hour comparator, while **95%** exceeded the **70 dBA off-duty recovery** comparator.
- **Interpretation:** Sleeping/rest environments deserve assessment beyond jet operations. Do not conflate auditory rest with residential sleep recommendations.
- **Limit:** Snapshot while at sea, not 6-12 months of longitudinal exposure.

Primary: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30457491/

## Naval berthing, measured nighttime sound and well-being

### 5. Royal Norwegian Navy (2016), *Noise and Sleep on Board Vessels in the Royal Norwegian Navy*

- **Design:** **68** sailors across **21 vessels** contributing **83 monitored sleep periods**. Researchers measured cabin noise with dosimeters and sleep activity with actigraphy, with statistical adjustment for selected factors.
- **Results:** Equivalent sound levels and noise events per hour were associated with greater movement during sleep. More noise events per hour were associated with reduced sleep efficiency.
- **Interpretation:** **Direct objective evidence** that nighttime shipboard noise can disturb sleep. A continuous average alone may miss the importance of events and peaks.
- **Limit:** Observational association; vessel mix should **not** be labeled exclusively submarines or carriers. Results cannot establish long-term disease caused by a particular low-frequency sound.

Primary: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26960785/ ; free text https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4918679/

### 6. Matsangas et al. (2020), *Habitability in Berthing Compartments and Well-Being of Sailors Working on U.S. Navy Surface Ships*

- **Design:** Naturalistic longitudinal study with **1,269 sailors aboard six surface ships**. Sailors reported noise, light, temperature, and other sleeping-area conditions; sleep and mood were assessed with questionnaires and actigraphy.
- **Results:** Noise was among the frequently reported habitability concerns. Greater numbers of habitability complaints were associated with more daytime sleepiness, insomnia symptoms, poorer perceived sleep quality, and worse mood.
- **Limit:** Environmental effects are **bundled**; this is not a controlled experiment measuring the independent effect of noise, and it is **not a submarine study**.

Primary: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32109155/

### 7. Merchant seafarers (2020), *Physical Influences on Seafarers ... A Maritime Field Study*

- **Design:** Maritime field work with noise measurements by location and interviews/questionnaires.
- **Results:** Mean **sleeping cabin noise was about 57 dBA** (reported range **36-66 dBA**); noise and vibration were frequently reported sources of stress. This provides a somewhat lower-level shipboard sleeping-area comparison than aircraft-carrier flight operations.
- **Limit:** **Merchant shipping, not military submarines or data centers**. Measures also involve vessel motion, engine/mechanical events, shift work, and other conditions. No direct inference to any Cascade property-line noise limit.

Primary: https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0231309

## Submarine missions: what weeks-to-months research actually shows

### 8. U.S. Navy (2024), *Life Onboard a Submarine: Sleep, Fatigue, and Lifestyle Behaviors of Sailors on a Circadian-Aligned Watchstanding Schedule*

- **Design:** **58** submariners during **30 days underway** on a fast-attack submarine. Researchers used wrist actigraphy, self-reports, and mood measures.
- **Results:** Mean **objective sleep 6.62 hours per day**, mean **subjective sleep 5.90 hours**. **77.8% reported poor sleep quality**, **56.1% high daytime sleepiness**, and **27.5% clinically relevant insomnia-level symptoms** while underway. Many sailors reported poor sleep *before* sailing as well.
- **Interpretation:** Deployment sleep problems are real even after a shift to more circadian-aligned watchkeeping.
- **Crucial limit:** These measurements **do not isolate continuous sound, low-frequency machinery hum, or a noise dose**. Researchers discuss lighting, watch schedule, workload, lifestyle, confinement, and circadian factors. It would be wrong to claim 77.8% slept poorly *because of submarine noise*.

Primary: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S000368702400098X ; DOI https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apergo.2024.104321

### 9. French Navy (2015), *Sleeping under the Ocean: Despite Total Isolation, Nuclear Submariners Maintain Their Sleep and Wake Patterns throughout Their Under Sea Mission*

- **Design:** **19** crew members during a **70-day nuclear ballistic-missile submarine patrol**. Researchers compared objective sleep recordings on **days 21 and 51**.
- **Results:** No significant deterioration between those time points in total sleep time, recorded sleep quality, or most subjective sleep/mood measures.
- **Interpretation:** Stable sleep over weeks is possible during an organized patrol with controlled environmental conditions. The paper discusses deliberate control of noise for submarine quieting, as well as lighting, temperature, and routines.
- **Crucial limit:** No before-deployment sleep baseline and **no isolated noise-effect experiment**. Comparing days 21 and 51 does not prove that the first 21 days or years of exposure are harmless.

Primary: https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0126721

### 10. Other extended submarine evidence

- **Van Puyvelde et al. (2022):** **67-day undersea mission** with a six-hours-on/six-hours-off watch schedule examined circadian hormones and sleep. **Work/rest and isolation effects**, not a continuous-noise dose-response study. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35142558/
- **2025 study, *Sleep in Military Submariners: Exploring its Dynamics ...*:** Studied a **three-week** mission and reported sleep-duration/quality changes associated with mission and psychological factors. It does not establish noise as the independent cause. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40326443/
- **Marando et al. (2023) scoping review:** Submarine watch schedules are associated with sleep and circadian problems; the review focuses on watchkeeping and cognition, **not demonstrating mechanical noise causes chronic illness**. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37677995/

## What a 72-hour infrasound study can establish

Marshall et al. (2023), a randomized double-blind crossover study, studied **37 noise-sensitive but otherwise healthy adults** exposed over **72-hour sessions** to **inaudible simulated wind-turbine infrasound (1.6-20 Hz)**, sham exposure, and audible traffic noise as a control. The **inaudible infrasound did not significantly worsen measured sleep/other outcomes** relative to sham, whereas audible traffic events worsened the principal sleep measure.

**Correct interpretation:** This is evidence about a *specific inaudible exposure* over three days under controlled laboratory conditions. It is **not** a test of **months or years**, of **audible low-frequency tonal cooling equipment**, or of a neighborhood with windows, bedrooms, changing weather, and intermittent generator operation. The different measures (infrasound **dB peak**, general **dBA**, **dBC**, and vibration) are **not directly interchangeable**.

Primary: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10032045/ ; DOI https://doi.org/10.1289/EHP10757

A 2016 systematic review of **seven observational studies** found associations between everyday low-frequency sound and **annoyance, sleep complaints, concentration difficulty, and headaches**, while reporting **very limited evidence about chronic diseases**. Association does not establish unique causation, but dismissing sleep-related harm outright would be unsupported.

Primary: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26994804/

## What can and cannot be inferred for Cascade

| Proposed inference | Evidence assessment |
| --- | --- |
| Months aboard loud ships can expose crew to substantial occupational noise | Supported as a real concern, with **short-period measurements**, not fully monitored deployments |
| Flight-deck noise can contribute to noise-induced hearing loss | Supported; measured exposures much higher than preliminary Cascade statements |
| Noise reaching sleeping rooms can reduce sleep quality | Supported by naval objective studies and general community-noise evidence |
| All submarine sleep complaints are caused by the reactor/machinery hum | **Not supported** by these studies |
| Stable sleep in one 70-day submarine study proves low-frequency noise is harmless for life | **Not supported** |
| Lack of harm in a 72-hour inaudible-infrasound trial proves decades of audible data-center hum are safe | **Not supported** |
| Any continuous industrial hum necessarily causes chronic disease | **Not supported** by these studies |
| A measured navy berthing dBA figure can be compared directly with a hypothetical Cascade dBC figure | **Not valid** without weighting, location, timing, spectrum and exposure normalization |

**Guidance versus enforceable limits:** WHO provides health-based **sleep guidance**, including **below 30 dBA inside bedrooms at night for good sleep** and **below 40 dBA annual-average Lnight outdoors** for its night-noise guideline. These metrics have different averaging and receptor definitions and are **not current Cascade law**. The shipboard occupational **70 dBA auditory-rest** comparison is different and is **not** a nighttime residential limit. https://www.who.int/europe/news-room/fact-sheets/item/noise

## What this means for neutral M-2 protections

Set impact-based acoustic performance rules **for all similarly situated M-2 businesses**, not standards naming only Simple Mining. The evidence supports asking for:

1. **Independent preconstruction modeling at actual nearby homes**, not only the parcel boundary. Include evening/night, full summer cooling, steady hum, tonality and other operating modes.
2. **Calibrated baseline measurements** at selected residences, including nighttime levels and the difference between background and new sources.
3. **Separate LAeq and LCeq reporting**, plus narrow-band or one-third-octave frequency spectra, tonal characteristics, Lmax events, and actual building/ground vibration where a vibration concern is alleged. dBC is **not** ground vibration.
4. **Clear daytime and nighttime compliance criteria** at defined receptor points, with appropriate tonal penalties. Distinguish ordinary cooling hum from short generator-testing events.
5. **Post-build independent acceptance measurements** during realistic operations plus periodic and complaint-triggered verification.
6. **Enforceable mitigation and correction deadlines** applying to any industrial operator with the same impacts.

**Data gap:** No completed Cascade acoustic model or independent measured spectrum/ambient nighttime results for the proposed equipment were established at the October 1 forum. These naval references cannot fill that gap.

## Source-handling rules

- Identify which studies directly **measure noise** and which study **sleep under multiple conditions** without isolating sound.
- Do not confuse **% monitored samples**, **% sailors**, or **% with diagnosed disease**.
- Distinguish **dBA occupational exposures**, **dBA indoor bedroom guidance**, **dBC low-frequency screening**, and **unweighted peak infrasound**.
- Do not treat watch patterns, confined-ship conditions, or jet operations as equivalent to a residential outdoor cooling unit.
- Use study times accurately: measured **days or weeks**, rather than asserting year-long continuous instrumentation.
