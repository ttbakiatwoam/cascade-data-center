# Electric franchise fees, the 40 MW proposal, and job-related taxes

**Independent Cascade, Iowa research | Updated October 22, 2026 | Model, not adopted policy or a verified utility contract.**

**Bottom line:** Iowa Code §364.2(4)(f) generally allows Cascade to collect an electric franchise fee of **up to 5% of an outside utility's gross revenue from sales within the city**. If Maquoketa Valley Electric Cooperative (MVEC) actually sells electricity to a data center **inside Cascade's incorporated limits**, the city may have a substantial revenue opportunity. **Neither MVEC's project tariff, the final service territory arrangement, any existing MVEC/Cascade franchise agreement, nor the proposed project's tax eligibility has been verified.** The City Attorney must approve the legal path, especially any attempt to confine a fee to M-2 zoning.

## 1. Exactly what Iowa law allows

- **[Iowa Code §364.2(4)(a)–(f), 2026](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/2026/364.2.pdf):** A city may grant a **nonexclusive utility franchise by ordinance**, for no more than **25 years**. A public hearing and publication are required. For an electric franchise, the council may dispense with an election unless a valid petition requests one or the franchise applicant asks for one. Written acceptance is needed. A city of Cascade's size may charge **no more than 5% of gross revenues from the franchisee's sales within the city**. The special 7.5% ceiling applies only to cities with more than 200,000 residents, not Cascade.
- **[§364.2(4)(f)(2)–(3)](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/2026/364.2.pdf):** Before adopting or increasing the franchise-fee rate, prepare and publish a **revenue-purpose statement**, including anticipated property-tax relief if listed, hold the applicable hearing, and notify the Iowa Department of Revenue of a fee ordinance adoption, amendment, or repeal. The city itself cannot be assessed its own franchise fee as an electric customer.
- **[§384.3A, 2026](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/2026/384.3A.pdf):** The franchise-fee account is part of the general fund, with legally specified uses. After costs of franchise regulation, authorized uses include **property-tax relief, public improvements, infrastructure, public safety/emergency services, low-income energy programs, and economic development**. Funds are not unrestricted cash.
- **[§423B.5, 2026](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/2026/423B.5.pdf):** Iowa's **1% local option sales/services tax** cannot apply to otherwise covered electricity transactions **during the period the electricity is subject to a franchise or user fee**. LOST on nonutility retail transactions remains separate. A franchise fee is **not** a replacement for property taxes.
- **[§384.89, 2026](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/2026/384.89.pdf):** A **city-owned utility** cannot simply be treated as an outside franchisee. Transfers of city-utility **surplus** to other city funds are possible after required debt/reserve obligations and governing-body approvals. This is the municipal-utility **payment in lieu of taxes (PILOT)** pathway.

These statutes authorize a framework, not a guaranteed new tax on one named company.

## 2. Cascade Municipal Utilities versus MVEC

[Cascade Municipal Utilities' electric page](https://www.cityofcascade.org/vnews/display.v/SEC/Municipal%20Utilities%7CElectric) confirms the city operates its own electric utility and posts its local tariff. **An existing municipal utility does not automatically bar an MVEC franchise.** Iowa electric service territories and actual delivery points matter; two separate utility businesses can be relevant near a city's boundary.

| Situation | What Cascade can potentially collect | Critical condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| MVEC makes **retail electric sales inside Cascade city limits** | Franchise fee under §364.2 | Existing franchise, service territory, ordinance, billing and service-point details verified |
| Cascade Municipal Utilities makes the retail sale | Municipal utility surplus transfer/PILOT, **not** a franchise fee on itself | Profitable surplus, restrictions and debt/reserve covenants under §384.89 |
| MVEC supplies a facility **outside** city limits | **No Cascade franchise fee on those out-of-city sales** | Exact incorporated boundary and point of sale/use |
| A different entity sells power directly or customer self-generates | Not automatically MVEC retail sales; may be no MVEC sales base to assess | Actual service structure, wholesale/retail agreement and legal review |

The [Iowa League of Cities' Municipal Policy Leaders' Handbook (2026)](https://ia-fairfield.civicplus.com/DocumentCenter/View/12239/2026MunicipalPolicyHandbook) states that a municipal utility does not need a franchise, and the [Iowa Clerks Handbook (2024)](https://cms2.revize.com/revize/northiacog/Document%20Center/Resources/2024ClerksManual.pdf) advises that a rural electric cooperative's franchise fee should be no higher than the fee imposed on another electric franchisee or, if a municipal utility serves the city, the comparable municipal-utility PILOT. **That comparator is handbook guidance requiring project-specific legal review, not an automatic 5% authorization.**

The October 1 forum record describes MVEC/CIPCO's approximately **40 MW** service proposal and reports **no executed service agreement at the time**. This project does not possess a signed tariff/service contract, and MVEC did not commit to a city franchise fee at that forum. [Read the separate power-study analysis]({{ '/cipco-mvec-power/' | relative_url }}).

## Verified MVEC franchise agreements in nearby Iowa cities

This is not just theoretical: [Maquoketa's Code of Ordinances, Chapter 113, §113.09](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/maquoketaia/latest/maquoketa_ia/0-0-0-4009) imposes a **5% electricity franchise fee on MVEC gross revenue from sales inside city limits**. MVEC must show the fee separately on customer bills and remit collections quarterly. This is a drafting/billing comparator, **not** proof of Cascade's franchise authority over this exact project.

[Manchester's Code of Ordinances, Chapter 112](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/manchesteria/latest/manchester_ia/0-0-0-23750) grants an MVEC electric franchise. [§112.11](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/manchesteria/latest/manchester_ia/0-0-0-23772) restricts increases beyond **3% for the first five years** of the agreement's 25-year term. Both municipal codes are online publisher copies; attorneys should obtain official signed versions. **Neither enacted example establishes legal authority to single out M-2 lots or one data-center customer.**

## 3. Could the fee apply *only* in M-2 Heavy Industrial?

**Not established.** The Simple Mining parcel is mapped as **M-2 Heavy Industrial** in Cascade's zoning layer, but **zoning and a municipal utility franchise are different legal powers**. §364.2 measures franchise fee revenues by **sales within the city**, not by zoning district. It does not expressly provide an M-2-only rate.

There is some evidence of **customer-class-specific** franchise rates in Iowa: the [Iowa Utility Association's January 15, 2026 listing](https://www.iowautility.org/about/franchise-fees/) reports **Hull at 5% residential, 1.5% nonresidential, 0% public**, **Inwood at 5% residential, 3% nonresidential, 0% public**, and **Hastings at 5% residential**. Those are **reported practices, not a legal ruling** approving a special M-2-only Cascade franchise fee. Differentiating by customer class is not equivalent to targeting a particular zoning parcel or single company.

Three structures for the City Attorney to examine:

1. **MVEC-wide within city limits:** A franchise and fee imposed on covered MVEC retail sales inside Cascade's city boundary. This is closest to the statute's territorial gross-receipts language. Check effects on any other MVEC customers.
2. **Neutral high-voltage or major-load customer class:** A uniformly defined, objective rate class based on delivery voltage, firm peak demand (for example 10 MW or greater), or an existing utility tariff category. Legal review is still essential, including consistency with MVEC's special contract and any municipal PILOT comparability.
3. **Voluntary development/community-benefits agreement:** A negotiated payment for substantiated city costs or agreed public benefits, separate from the franchise. Do **not** assume a city may disguise an unauthorized, one-project tax as a contract condition or zoning fee. Counsel must review authority, consideration, nexus, and enforceability.

**Suggested approach:** Do not initially propose an “M-2 data center tax.” Seek a written opinion on the service area, existing franchise, allowable uniform class definitions, PILOT comparison, and legal treatment of a specialized high-voltage franchise category. Use M-2 for neutral land-use performance standards, not as an assumed taxation boundary.

## 4. Forty megawatts: what does the *published MVEC tariff* imply?

**40 MW is demand/capacity, not “40 MW per year.”** Annual usage equals load in MW multiplied by operating hours and load factor. At a constant 40 MW for 8,760 hours, annual energy use is **350,400,000 kilowatt-hours**.

For an *illustrative, published large-load benchmark*, MVEC's [Master Electric Tariff, Rate Schedule 7, sheets 17-13 and 17-14, effective August 1, 2024](https://mvec.coop/sites/default/files/MVEC%20Master%20Tariff%20-%20June%202024.pdf) lists **Special Time of Use Large Power Service**, for qualifying transmission-level members under a **special agreement** and applicable CIPCO service rules:

| MVEC Rate 7 published component | Published value | Assumed 40 MW annual cost |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Energy | **38.521 mills/kWh = $0.038521/kWh** | **$13,497,758.40** |
| Seasonal demand | **$6.54/kW/month** | **$3,139,200.00** |
| Monthly coincident demand | **$8.34/kW/month** | **$4,003,200.00** |
| Basic service | **$125/month** | **$1,500.00** |
| **Total before adjustments** | | **$20,641,658.40 per year** |

**Assumptions:** 40,000 kW continuously; full 40,000 kW chargeable for **both** demand components in each of 12 months; 8,760 hours; no outages; no tax, credits, discounts or pass-through; and no nonstandard contract terms. This is a **load scenario**, not a prediction of the billed amount.

**Major missing price inputs:** Rate 7 is subject to **Power Cost Adjustment No. 4**, possible power-factor/meter adjustments, and a special service contract. The August 2024 tariff may have been amended by 2026. Published Rate 7 is **not confirmed to apply to the Simple Mining proposal**. Demand charges are **not** merely the energy/kWh rate.

## 5. Franchise versus local option sales tax and data-center exemptions

**Do not add franchise receipts to an assumed 1% LOST receipt on the same electricity.** Under [§423B.5](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/2026/423B.5.pdf), otherwise taxable electricity subject to a franchise/user fee is exempt from LOST for the fee period. With the modeled $20.64 million bill, a *hypothetical* 1% local tax base would be about **$206,417 before allocation rules**. This is **not** established Cascade LOST revenue and cannot be simply netted against the full city franchise collection without seeing the actual distribution.

A second, more important issue is the **Iowa data-center electricity sales/use tax exemption**. The [Iowa Department of Revenue guidance](https://revenue.iowa.gov/taxes/tax-guidance/sales-use-excise-tax/data-centers), based on [§423.3(95)](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/code/2026/423.3.pdf), describes qualifying data centers investing **at least $200 million within six years**, a **minimum 5,000-square-foot** operations facility, required design standards, registration and reporting, and eligible electricity purchases. For new operations from June 6, 2025 in cities **not exceeding 30,000 population**, qualified electricity purchases can be exempt from state sales/use tax for **the first 15 years**. Since LOST ordinarily follows the state taxable base, qualified exempt electricity generally generates **no LOST in the first place**. A local franchise fee may therefore represent new local revenue even while the eligible data center's purchases are exempt from sales tax.

**Qualification is not established.** The company has discussed roughly $400 million in potential capital spending and state tax incentives, but investment, statutory data-center definition (including the nature of computing/crypto or hosting workloads), engineering compliance, registration, commencement date, and actual exemption certificates must be verified. An electricity sales-tax exemption **does not, by itself, establish an exemption from a municipal gross-receipts franchise fee**. Counsel and Iowa DOR should confirm the specific interplay.

Finally, **property tax** is a different revenue stream. A franchise fee neither repeals real-estate assessment nor eliminates any state-authorized exemption/abatement. Separate verified assessments and city/county/school levies are needed before calculating property taxes.

## 6. Income taxes from **40 jobs at $80,000 each**

**This is a requested illustrative 40-job scenario, not the documented Simple Mining hiring projection.** The [October 1 forum research](https://github.com/ttbakiatwoam/cascade-data-center/blob/master/dataset/impacts/12_JOBS_TAXES_AND_COMMUNITY_BENEFITS.md) records the company's projection of **about 20–25 permanent positions at roughly $75,000–$80,000 average salary**, not a firm commitment for 40 jobs.

**Gross annual wages = 40 × $80,000 = $3,200,000.** For an illustrative, easy-to-reproduce 2026 tax scenario, assume all workers have only this wage income, are Iowa-taxable, file **single**, take the **2026 federal $16,100 standard deduction**, claim no tax credits, receive no special deductions, and have no Iowa taxable-income modifications:

| Per person | All 40 employees |
| --- | ---: |
| Gross annual wages | **$3,200,000** |
| Federal taxable wages/income after standard deduction | **$2,556,000** |
| **Estimated 2026 federal individual income tax** | **$350,800** |
| **Estimated 2026 Iowa individual income tax** | **$97,128** |
| **Combined estimated individual income taxes** | **$447,928** |
| **Direct income taxes paid to City of Cascade** | **$0** |

Per worker: federal taxable income **$63,900**; federal tax **$1,240 + $4,560 + $2,970 = $8,770** under [2026 IRS single filing brackets](https://www.irs.gov/irb/2025-45_IRB); Iowa **$63,900 × 3.8% = $2,428.20**, based on [Iowa's 2026 flat 3.8% rate](https://revenue.iowa.gov/press-release/2025-10-21/idr-announces-2026-individual-income-tax-and-interest-rates) and [federal taxable-income starting point](https://revenue.iowa.gov/taxes/tax-guidance/individual-income-tax/1040-expanded-instructions/conformity-irc).

For scale only, **3.8% of gross $3.2 million = $121,600** is an **overstated gross-payroll shortcut**, *not* the proper Iowa tax estimate. Tax credits, married filing status, retirement deductions, dependents, overtime/tips provisions and residence may alter actual liabilities. Separate Social Security/Medicare payroll contributions are **not income taxes** and do not go to Cascade. Neither federal nor Iowa income tax is directly remitted to the City of Cascade. City benefits from jobs arise indirectly through taxable spending, housing/property activity, and local business activity, which cannot be quantified without separate assumptions.

## 7. Documents and decisions Cascade needs before acting

1. **Cascade clerk and City Attorney:** Is there an existing MVEC/Cascade franchise ordinance? When was it adopted, what territory/class scope does it cover, and is it in force?
2. **City utilities department:** What is Cascade Municipal Utilities' existing PILOT/surplus-transfer formula and recent amounts? Does the cooperative franchise rate face a comparability limit?
3. **Iowa Utilities Commission / MVEC:** Confirm the actual **certified service territory, legal city boundary, point of delivery, retail provider, invoicing entity**, and high-voltage service classification for Simple Mining.
4. **MVEC:** Obtain its **current 2026 Rate 7 tariff, Power Cost Adjustment, any project-specific electric service and generation agreements**, and annual estimated billed retail gross receipts at realistic demand/load factors.
5. **City Attorney and MVEC:** Obtain a **written opinion** on customer-class rates, whether a rate can be limited to M-2 or to objectively defined major industrial demand, existing utility PILOT parity, pass-through treatment, and whether a separately negotiated benefit payment is allowed.
6. **City/Department of Revenue:** Establish LOST status and whether project electricity qualifies under **§423.3(95)**. Obtain project exemption documentation or official confirmation rather than assuming an exemption.
7. **Finance committee/council:** Prepare a published **revenue-purpose statement**, notice/hearing, lawful ordinance language, effective date, and a conservative net fiscal projection. Distinguish **City** revenue from **Iowa, federal, county, and school** receipts.
8. **Developer:** Reconcile the **20–25 jobs** described October 1 against the hypothetical **40-job** scenario; request documented minimum jobs, wages, utilization forecast, phase schedule and enforceable public benefits.

**Interpretation:** On these assumptions, a lawful 3% franchise on a 40 MW MVEC retail account would correspond to **approximately $619,250 annually for Cascade**, separate from an illustrative **$447,928 in federal/state individual income tax** from **40 hypothetical $80k jobs**. The two numbers are paid to **different governments**, and neither is a finalized project forecast.
