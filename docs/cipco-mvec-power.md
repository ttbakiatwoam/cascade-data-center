# CIPCO, MVEC and Power Availability for Cascade

> **Research status, October 13, 2026.** Independent research, not a statement by the City, CIPCO, or MVEC. The proposed Cascade data center was described as approximately **40 MW**, not a typical hyperscale campus. A project-specific utility study was **described publicly but has not been obtained or reviewed**.

**The central distinction:** **Generating capacity** is the ability to produce or secure enough power. **Transmission capacity** is the ability to move that power through the high-voltage network. **Distribution/substation upgrades** and an executed service agreement are additional requirements. A statement about a shortage in one category is not automatically a contradiction about another.

## Direct public evidence

| Date | Who said it | What it establishes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| February 24, 2025 | **CIPCO**, in its integrated resource plan summary | Reported a system-wide shortage of **more than 150 MW of generating capacity** and additional resource needs through 2030. It did **not** say the Cascade data center had been rejected. |
| September 11, 2026 | **MVEC CEO Jeremy Richert**, in his published CEO Insights | MVEC evaluates large-load requests **alongside CIPCO** and expects customers to fund project-driven infrastructure. |
| October 1, 2026 | **Jeremy Richert**, at the Cascade community forum | Described a joint capacity study finding **adequate regional transmission for the proposed approximately 40 MW load**, with room reserved for normal growth. |
| October 1, 2026 | **Jeremy Richert**, same presentation | Stated that a load of this size **must bring additional generation**, and that Simple Mining would have to prepay **100% of project-driven transmission, substation and distribution upgrades**. |
| October 1, 2026 | **Jeremy Richert**, same presentation | Stated that **no Cascade service contract had been executed** as of that date. |

## Has the CIPCO–MVEC study been located?

**Not yet.** Searches of publicly indexed CIPCO and MVEC publications, official documents and project-related references through October 13, 2026 did **not** identify a publicly downloadable Cascade-specific study. The October 1 forum itself identifies a study, but does not supply the report, model assumptions, cost estimates, or completed generation arrangements.

This is **different from CIPCO's system-wide Integrated Resource Plan**, which assesses regional generation needs, and from its capacity procurement and Hickory Grove generation initiatives.

The next evidence step is a **direct joint request to CIPCO and MVEC** for the study or a nonconfidential summary that names the study date, scope, assumed load, conditions, grid constraints, required upgrades, generation arrangements and conclusion. Public absence does not mean the study does not exist.

## Why the public claims may not conflict

- CIPCO's official **2025** statement addresses insufficient generation resources within its overall portfolio.
- MVEC's **October 2026** presentation addresses its described **ability to transmit** electricity to Cascade, while requiring **new generation** for that customer.
- An unnamed person's description of a CIPCO meeting is not evidence of a Cascade-specific rejection without a date, speaker, minutes or exact statement.

**Open question:** Does CIPCO itself confirm the October 1 account of the joint study, and has any new information changed the result?
