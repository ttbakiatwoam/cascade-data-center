# Independent repository audit and corrections — November 16, 2026

**Independent research; not adopted Cascade law, an official municipal report, or legal advice.** On November 16, the project received an **11-page outside audit** reporting **25 items: four critical, nine high, nine medium and three low**. The auditor expressly stated that several principal repository documents and the live website **could not be opened**. This review is useful as a research prompt, **not an authoritative finding that the repository lacks material the auditor could not inspect**.

**[Read the 25-finding disposition, original-source identification and primary legal citations](https://github.com/ttbakiatwoam/cascade-data-center/blob/master/dataset/evidence/53_INDEPENDENT_FACTUAL_LEGAL_TECHNICAL_AUDIT_2026-11-16.md).** The original 11-page PDF was supplied by the project author, preserved by SHA-256 in the source record; the GitHub document is a **structured review of its findings, not a binary copy of that PDF**.

## Substantive changes to current *proposed* texts

- The **Major Industrial Facility** §165.41 term now says **meeting or exceeding** any §165.11(D)(8) threshold, to match the special-exception trigger (5 MW electrical demand, 25,000 gallons per 24 hours, 2 MW stationary generation, or 5 MWh energy storage). The change concerns an **unadopted** proposal.
- Ordinary elevated-noise generator testing is now proposed **8 a.m.–6 p.m., Monday–Friday**, rather than beginning at 6 a.m. during the proposed 10 p.m.–7 a.m. nighttime noise period. Genuine emergencies and the other specified exceptions are distinct; applicable noise standards still apply.
- The proposed applicant schedule now requests engine brake horsepower, displacement per cylinder, model year, EPA certificate/engine family, and Iowa DNR air-permit, exemption and potential-to-emit documentation as applicable.
- The four **November 11 participant-reported committee-approved definitions are reproduced without changing the words or the thresholds.** Their approval is **not** adoption of any ordinance or the separate proposed citywide ban.

## Important distinctions made explicit

**Hyperscale:** The voted-for-draft definition requires **both** at least 100,000 square feet **and** demand **exceeding 60 MW**. A hypothetical 40 MW facility would not meet that proposed definition on load alone; separate Major Industrial Facility provisions may apply if enacted. This is a question for the committee, **not permission to silently change a reported vote**.

**Air emissions:** Engine exhaust certification limits, diesel **fuel sulfur**, and **outdoor ambient air** standards are different legal measurements. A 15-ppm sulfur limit generally relates to the under-30-L/cylinder diesel-fuel rule; 40 CFR 60.4207(d) also specifies 1,000 ppm for the >=30-L/cylinder class, subject to other applicable requirements. There is no verified numeric stack ppm for the preliminary Cascade scenario.

**PTE / Title V:** The audit's hypothetical 132 short tons per year used a **combined NMHC+NOx** engine limit; it is not proof of 132 tons of **NOx**. A conditional **500-hour Iowa potential-to-emit calculation assumption** is not a universal new-engine default or authority to operate for 500 hours. Every permit determination requires the specific fleet, pollutants, limits and agency review.

**Noise and franchise fees:** The current drafts already provide substantial acoustic measurement mechanics, and existing fiscal research already labels MVEC franchise amounts as **conditional scenarios**. These items still warrant specialist/legal review, not an assertion they were absent.
