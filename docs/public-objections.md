# Public Concerns: What the Evidence Shows

This page reviews recurring questions about the proposed Cascade data center without attributing them to individual residents. It distinguishes **documented research**, **statements made at the October 1, 2026 public forum**, **unanswered project questions**, and **proposed community protections**.

> This independent website is not a City of Cascade publication. The proposal, committee recommendations, and zoning standards linked here are working documents, not adopted law. Much of the broader data-center research concerns **hyperscale sites**, not the approximately **60,000-square-foot, 40 MW** concept discussed for Cascade.

[Full detailed objection-by-objection research](https://github.com/ttbakiatwoam/cascade-data-center/blob/master/dataset/evidence/31_PUBLIC_OBJECTIONS_EVIDENCE_REVIEW.md) · [Original October 1 forum evidence](https://github.com/ttbakiatwoam/cascade-data-center/blob/master/dataset/updates/28_SIMPLE_MINING_COMMUNITY_FORUM_2026-10-01.md)

## Low-frequency noise, vibrations, and health

**What is known:** dBA and dBC measure sound with different frequency weighting; dBC is not a vibration measurement. A 2016 review associated low-frequency noise with annoyance and sleep complaints, but said evidence of chronic medical disease was very limited. A controlled 2023 study did not find adverse measured outcomes from **inaudible simulated infrasound** in its participants. That result cannot be applied automatically to an audible industrial hum. Existing health conditions can increase vulnerability, but healthy people can also experience noise-related sleep disturbance.

**Longer-duration Navy research:** Aircraft-carrier studies found high measured noise during duty and in sleeping areas. A 2007 review of nearly 268,000 sailors' hearing records also found greater modeled hearing-loss risk with longer surface-warship service. A Norwegian Navy study directly associated measured cabin noise and nighttime noise events with impaired sleep measures. In contrast, **submarine sleep studies over 30 and 70 days did not isolate engine or ventilation hum as the cause of health problems**; one found poor sleep was common, while the other found stable sleep across sampled patrol days. These occupational exposures differ greatly from the **unmeasured proposed** Cascade sound. A short infrasound trial cannot prove safety after years, and naval hearing-loss findings cannot prove disease from a quiet data-center hum.

[Naval noise, submarine deployment and long-term study review](https://github.com/ttbakiatwoam/cascade-data-center/blob/master/dataset/impacts/33_NAVAL_SHIPBOARD_NOISE_SLEEP_AND_LONG_DURATION_EXPOSURE.md) · [Carrier dosimetry study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30855086/) · [Norwegian Navy noise-and-sleep study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26960785/)

**Will the cooling create one constant hum?** Not necessarily. Data-center IT loads generate heat all day, but specific cooling components may vary their speeds, cycle, or stage as the equipment load and outdoor weather change. Variable-speed fans and reduced compressor operation are established data-center designs. Simple Mining described **closed-loop liquid cooling with dry/air-cooled heat rejection**, so it is premature to describe this site as using ordinary home-style refrigerant compressors. Even if some fans slow or switch off, other fans, pumps or transformers could remain audible. Neither a uniform 24/7 hum nor regular shutoffs at a thermostat setpoint have been demonstrated. The actual equipment schedule and nighttime operating noise remain unknown. [ENERGY STAR fan controls](https://www.energystar.gov/products/data_center_equipment/5-simple-ways-avoid-energy-waste-your-data-center/replace-standard-fans-variable-speed-fans) · [ASHRAE dry-cooler and free-cooling principles](https://www.ashrae.org/technical-resources/topics-and-initiatives/ai-data-center/energy-and-thermal-efficiency) · [Detailed Cascade noise evaluation](https://github.com/ttbakiatwoam/cascade-data-center/blob/master/dataset/impacts/08_NOISE_AND_ACOUSTICS.md)

**LEED certification and exterior noise:** At the October 1 forum, Simple Mining described LEED as a **design goal**, not an obtained certification. LEED v4.1 Data Centers has an **optional indoor Acoustic Performance** credit, and LEED v5 New Construction has an optional **Sound Environment** pathway. A separate [USGBC exterior-noise pilot](https://www.usgbc.org/sites/default/files/Enhanced%20acoustical%20performance%20-%20exterior%20noise%20control.pdf) provides a more directly relevant voluntary benchmark: **project noise at least 5 dBA below pre-project ambient Ldn, and no greater than 60 dBA**, evaluated at the nearest property line or public right of way. It also calls for background measurements, a mitigation plan, and a generator-testing plan. **Emergency equipment is exempt from the pilot's numerical noise limit**. The pilot's availability/eligibility for Cascade has not been confirmed. LEED certification itself does **not** impose those limits, and **45 dBA over eight hours is not the exterior pilot's requirement**.

[LEED acoustics and neighborhood noise guide]({{ '/leed-noise/' | relative_url }}) · [Detailed USGBC document comparison](https://github.com/ttbakiatwoam/cascade-data-center/blob/master/dataset/impacts/34_LEED_CERTIFICATION_ACOUSTICS_AND_EXTERIOR_NOISE.md)

**For Cascade:** The company provided preliminary noise figures but **no completed independent full-load, nighttime residential acoustic model**. Compare the actual dBA/dBC, frequency content, ambient baseline, and maximum cooling scenarios against enforceable limits.

## Wildlife: Do noisy facilities create bird habitat?

**What is known:** A 2009 peer-reviewed study of **New Mexico natural-gas compressor sites**, not data centers, found higher nesting success for some birds after predator-prey interactions changed, alongside **lower bird-species richness**. It does not prove that data-center noise repels cats or foxes, increases insects, or restores habitat in Cascade.

[Original bird study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19631542/) · [How the study applies and does not apply](https://github.com/ttbakiatwoam/cascade-data-center/blob/master/dataset/impacts/08_NOISE_AND_ACOUSTICS.md)

## Setbacks and nearby homes

The project-generated **draft** proposes a 100-foot building setback and a 200-foot setback for significant exterior equipment relative to certain **residential zoning boundaries**, not house front doors. The company described preliminary building setbacks around **216-291 feet** in parts of its October 1 plan. **Distances from the actual sound sources to individual homes remain unverified.** Sound from an ideal point source generally drops about 6 dB each time distance doubles; final noise needs an engineered site model.

## Diesel generators and emissions

Approximately 15 **diesel backup generators** were proposed, subject to final design. Testing intervals, engine types, emissions, fuel storage, and permit conditions are not final.

Cascade Municipal Utilities Board's August 12, 2026 claims list records a **$14,375.18** payment to McDermott Oil Co. That is evidence of a payment, **not proof of 15 operating days or an itemized generator-only fuel bill**. Municipal operation and the proposed facility should be compared using actual engine emissions and annual hours.

## Power capacity and electricity rates

At the October 1 forum, Maquoketa Valley Electric Cooperative described a capacity study with Central Iowa Power Cooperative supporting the proposed **40 MW** load while preserving growth capacity. The cooperative said the developer would be required to pay **100% of project-driven transmission, substation, and distribution upgrades upfront** and obtain additional generation. It anticipated a **neutral or positive effect on cooperative rates** if the arrangements are completed as described. It also distinguished ordinary rate increases from costs caused by a large customer. **Rate stability does not mean rates can never rise.** The final contract was not signed at the forum.

**Generation versus transmission:** CIPCO publicly reported a system-wide generation-capacity shortfall in February 2025. That does not independently refute MVEC's October 2026 account of a jointly studied **transmission** path, especially because MVEC explicitly required **new generation**. The underlying CIPCO-MVEC Cascade study has **not been located in public records** and remains a major evidence gap. ## Lithium-ion fires, lead-acid batteries, and fire suppression

**Battery type for Cascade is unknown.** Standard data-center UPS designs may use lead-acid (VRLA) or lithium-ion. Lead-acid is often cheaper **to buy**, but lithium-ion may be cheaper **over the full life of the installation** because it can last longer and require fewer replacements. Tenant rack batteries and central UPS equipment have potentially different owners. We do not know the ownership or allowed configurations proposed here.

Lithium-ion battery thermal runaway is a genuine fire-service hazard. Fire departments prepare with battery inventories, isolation, specialized response plans, gas monitoring, protective equipment, cooling and containment, and post-incident monitoring. Data centers may use early detection, sprinklers or pre-action systems, clean-agent systems, and battery-specific protection. **A clean-agent discharge is not automatically enough to stop lithium-ion thermal runaway.** Fire and electrical rules can apply even to privately owned, tenant-installed equipment.

There is no sound evidence that **one cellphone battery** has the **same ignition probability** as a large data-center battery system.

[Detailed battery chemistry, costs, fire suppression, and firefighter response research](https://github.com/ttbakiatwoam/cascade-data-center/blob/master/dataset/impacts/30_UPS_BATTERIES_LITHIUM_ION_FIRE_AND_SUPPRESSION.md) · [US Fire Administration response guidance](https://www.usfa.fema.gov/a-z/lithium-ion-batteries/risks-and-response-strategies/) · [Fire Safety Research Institute incident analysis](https://fsri.org/research-update/report-four-firefighters-injured-lithium-ion-battery-energy-storage-system)

## Cooling water and chemical releases

The company described a **closed-loop liquid cooling system with dry/air-cooled heat rejection**, not routine cooling from the municipal water system. The final mechanical design, coolant type and quantity, water consumption, spill controls, and enforceable cap are still needed.

## Public safety, roads, runoff, and cleanup

Plans should address stormwater calculations, fuel/chemical inventories, construction traffic, fire and EMS access, responder training, and project-funded infrastructure costs. A change in equipment or battery capacity can require updated emergency planning. Decommissioning and financial assurance should be defined before operations.

## AI, cryptocurrency, customers, and private property

Local zoning primarily regulates **land use and measurable impacts**, not which lawful application or customer uses a private server. But private ownership does **not** exempt a facility or tenant from zoning, fire, building, electrical, or environmental codes. A change in computing workload matters for land use if it changes approved power, sound, cooling, physical buildout, fire hazards, or other regulated impacts.

[Iowa zoning authority analysis](https://github.com/ttbakiatwoam/cascade-data-center/blob/master/dataset/policy/04_CASCADE_ZONING_AND_LOCAL_AUTHORITY.md) · [Workload comparisons](https://github.com/ttbakiatwoam/cascade-data-center/blob/master/dataset/technology/03_DATA_CENTER_WORKLOAD_TYPES.md)

## Property values and project scale

No established study in the repository isolates the property-value impact of this **specific** proposed Cascade facility. Claims based on large hyperscale facilities need matching scale, house distance, local market data, and reliable controls before they can be applied here.

## Evidence still needed

Before treating preliminary public statements as final requirements, obtain the utility service documents, stamped site plan, acoustic study, verified residential distances, final generator specifications, UPS/BESS inventory and suppression plans, coolant/water budget, stormwater plans, and enforceable monitoring, remediation, and closure provisions.
