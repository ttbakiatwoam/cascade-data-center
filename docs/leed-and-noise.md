# LEED Certification and Noise in Cascade

> **Research status: October 13, 2026.** Independent community research, not an official City of Cascade page. Simple Mining's proposed roughly **40 MW** facility is still a concept, and **its LEED registration, rating system, acoustic credits and final cooling noise remain unverified**.

At the **October 1, 2026 public forum**, Simple Mining described **LEED certification** as a project design goal. This is a company representation, **not proof the project has registered for or earned certification**, selected an acoustic credit or agreed to any exterior noise cap.

## What LEED addresses

| Program | Acoustic scope | What it does **not** guarantee |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **LEED v4.1 Building Design + Construction: Data Centers** | Optional **one-point Acoustic Performance credit** for **occupied indoor spaces** including HVAC background sound, transmission isolation and reverberation. | Does not establish an outdoor property-line noise limit for adjacent homes. |
| **LEED v5 Building Design + Construction: New Construction** | Optional **Sound Environment** design pathway worth 1–2 points, considering indoor and outdoor acoustic expectations, with modeling or measurements for higher credit. USGBC lists v5 New Construction as a possible data-center path. | Does not automatically cap noise at neighboring homes or enforce ongoing sound levels. |
| **LEED Pilot Credit 57: Exterior Noise Control** | Voluntary exterior-noise benchmark: **project sound at least 5 dBA below pre-project ambient Ldn and no more than 60 dBA** at the nearest property line or public right of way. Calls for baseline measurements, projected equipment-noise calculations and a mitigation narrative. | Pilot eligibility and current availability for this data-center project are **unverified**. This is **not adopted Cascade law**, and the pilot exempts emergency-generator noise from its numeric threshold while requiring a testing plan. |

[Official LEED v4.1 rating system](https://www.usgbc.org/sites/default/files/2020-11/LEED-v41-BD-C-Rating-System_tracked_7-2020.pdf) · [Official LEED v5 rating system](https://www.usgbc.org/sites/default/files/2026-03/LEED%20v5%20BD%2BC%20Rating%20System_February%202026.pdf) · [USGBC exterior pilot PDF](https://www.usgbc.org/sites/default/files/Enhanced%20acoustical%20performance%20-%20exterior%20noise%20control.pdf)

### A correction about the 45 dBA figure

The exterior-noise pilot **does not** set a universal **45 dBA eight-hour limit** for data centers. That figure appeared in a separate LEED **transit-station** rating guide. **Do not combine it with the USGBC Exterior Noise Control pilot.** The pilot's actual criteria are relative to **existing ambient day-night sound levels (Ldn)** and impose a **60 dBA ceiling**. Local codes may use different dBA, dBC and nighttime measurements.

## What this means for the proposed facility

**LEED certification is not a substitute for enforceable City noise protections.** A project might satisfy voluntary indoor credits while its outside dry coolers, pumps, transformers or standby generator tests still require independent review.

Air-cooled dry-cooler fans may change speed or stage based on outdoor temperature and demand. That does **not** establish that cooling fans shut off every night, nor that noise stays constant. The October 1 forum did not provide a final site-specific frequency spectrum or independently validated nighttime acoustic model.

A defensible Cascade review should obtain the actual mechanical design, **residential receptor** model under full summer cooling and quiet night conditions, baseline dBA and dBC plus tonal analysis, and independent after-construction verification. Industrial rules should apply consistently to **all similarly situated M-2 uses** or neutral noise-impact classes, not just to the proposed data center.

## Questions to ask the developer

1. Which **LEED version and rating system** will be used? Is the project actually **registered**?
2. Which **acoustic and exterior noise credits** will it pursue? Has USGBC confirmed eligibility for the pilot credit?
3. Will it disclose baseline **Ldn**, nighttime dBA/dBC, tonal sound spectra and predictive models for **nearby homes**?
4. What do its models show for **full-load summer cooling, winter operation, compressor/fan stages and generator testing**?
5. Will it accept **independent measurement and legally enforceable limits** regardless of LEED certification?
