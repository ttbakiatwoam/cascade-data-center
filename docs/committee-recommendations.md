# Proposed Recommendations for Cascade Data Center / Major Industrial Facility Zoning

> **DRAFT FOR DISCUSSION - NOT ADOPTED CITY POLICY OR LAW**  
> Prepared for the Cascade Data Center Advisory Committee and Planning & Zoning review.  
> Proposed next advisory committee meeting date: **October 19, 2026**.  
> Prepared October 6, 2026. This is an independent working proposal and should be reviewed by the City Attorney, City staff, Planning & Zoning, qualified engineers where appropriate, and the City Council before introduction or adoption.

## Executive recommendation

Cascade now has enough project-specific information, nearby Iowa comparators, and local zoning research to move from repeatedly identifying concerns to reviewing actual ordinance language.

The recommended approach is to:

1. expressly recognize **Data Center / High-Density Computing Facility** as a principal use in the M-2 Heavy Industrial District;
2. create a **Major Industrial Facility** special-exception category using neutral physical-impact thresholds that apply to any M-2 business meeting them;
3. adopt measurable district-wide standards for noise, lighting, screening, large water use, generator operation, emergency planning, stormwater, major utility demand, infrastructure impacts, decommissioning, and material expansion;
4. keep project-specific economic or voluntary commitments - such as LEED certification, local hiring, wages, community-benefit payments, incentive terms, and project-specific infrastructure reimbursement - in development or other lawful agreements rather than disguising them as project-specific zoning rules; and
5. transmit a marked-up ordinance to Planning & Zoning and the City Attorney on a defined schedule instead of allowing the process to remain open-ended.

**No project approval is requested by this recommendation package.** The purpose is to establish the rules that would govern a complete application and any similarly situated future M-2 development.

## 1. Why action is appropriate now

The October 1 community forum materially narrowed the factual uncertainty surrounding the current Simple Mining concept. The company described a conventional data-center building of approximately 60,000 square feet on a 15.22-acre parcel, approximately 40 MW of electrical demand, 24/7 operation, closed-loop hydrocooling with dry/air-cooled heat rejection, approximately 15 backup diesel generators, and roughly 20-25 projected permanent positions. Those statements remain preliminary representations rather than final engineered or contractual facts.

The public record also now independently establishes that the current parcel is **PIN 1932176027, 113 Industrial St SE, Lot 2 Cascade Industrial Park 14th Addition**, owned by Simple Mining LLC, containing **15.22 acres / 662,983 square feet**. The City ArcGIS zoning layer identifies the site area as **M-2 Heavy Industrial**, while retaining an older parcel number in the zoning-layer attributes.

The October 1 forum also produced useful negotiating information. Simple Mining expressed willingness in principle to work within clear and enforceable requirements involving noise, water use, emergency planning, screening/lighting, decommissioning financial assurance, and expansion limits. Those statements are not enforceable until incorporated into an ordinance, permit condition, executed agreement, bond, or final approved plan.

Nearby Iowa jurisdictions have already supplied workable policy models. Linn County and Jones County in particular provide examples of measurable sound limits, acoustic studies, cooling/water documentation, emergency planning, utility verification, site-plan review, and decommissioning requirements. These examples should be treated as comparators, not automatically copied without adapting them to Cascade's zoning structure and project scale.

## 2. Governing policy principle: regulate impacts, not identities

Any update to Cascade's M-2 zoning regulations should be written as a neutral district-wide rule. A data center may be added as a defined use type, but environmental, utility, noise, lighting, screening, infrastructure, emergency-response, and similar performance standards should apply to **all similarly situated M-2 businesses** or be triggered by neutral physical-impact thresholds.

That means the ordinance should not become a “Simple Mining ordinance,” nor should it single out cryptocurrency, AI, a future tenant, or any other lawful computing workload where the actual land-use impact can be regulated more directly through measurable operating limits.

The recommended ordinance therefore creates the concept of a **Major Industrial Facility**. A future factory, refrigeration facility, manufacturing plant, large processing use, or other M-2 business crossing the same thresholds would receive the same additional review.

## 3. Recommended committee actions

### Recommendation 1 - Recognize the land use explicitly

Recommend amending the M-2 Heavy Industrial District to expressly list **Data Center or High-Density Computing Facility** as a permitted principal use, with incidental server rooms and telecommunications equipment excluded when subordinate to another principal use.

This removes unnecessary ambiguity over terminology while preserving review of unusually large facilities through objective impact thresholds.

### Recommendation 2 - Establish a Major Industrial Facility special-exception review

Recommend requiring Special Exception review for any otherwise permitted M-2 use meeting one or more of these working thresholds:

| Impact trigger | Proposed threshold |
| --- | ---: |
| Maximum anticipated coincident electrical demand | **5 MW or greater** |
| Industrial/process/cooling/facility water demand | **25,000 gallons in any 24-hour period** |
| Aggregate stationary electrical generation | **2 MW or greater** |
| Aggregate stationary battery/energy storage | **5 MWh or greater** |
| Material expansion | Crossing a threshold or increasing an approved major impact by **20% or more** |

These are recommended starting thresholds for legal and technical review, not untouchable numbers.

### Recommendation 3 - Adopt measurable noise standards

Recommend objective sound limits rather than subjective “nuisance” language alone:

| Measurement location / time | dBA | dBC |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| M-2 property line, hourly average | **65** | **75** |
| Sensitive receptor, 7:00 a.m.-10:00 p.m. | **55** | **65** |
| Sensitive receptor, 10:00 p.m.-7:00 a.m. | **50** | **60** |

Where a source creates a prominent recurring tone, whine, screech, or hum, reduce the applicable A-weighted limit by **5 dB**.

Require a professional baseline study, predictive model under maximum reasonably anticipated conditions, post-construction acceptance testing, and periodic re-verification for Major Industrial Facilities.

### Recommendation 4 - Regulate generator testing uniformly

Recommend that routine and pre-planned testing of stationary generators or similarly elevated-noise equipment occur only **8:00 a.m.-6:00 p.m., Monday-Friday**, subject to exceptions for emergencies, life/property protection, legally or technically required testing, and specifically authorized unavoidable testing.

For facilities with 2 MW or more of stationary generation, require operating records identifying date, duration, emergency/non-emergency status, reason, and equipment operated, retained for at least three years.

### Recommendation 5 - Use impact-based setbacks, screening, and lighting rules

For new Major Industrial Facilities, recommend:

- at least **100 feet** between a principal building and a property boundary adjoining a residential zoning district;
- at least **200 feet** between specified large exterior mechanical equipment and a residential zoning district boundary, with a limited path to reduction based on an independent acoustic analysis but never below 100 feet;
- fully shielded downward-directed exterior lighting;
- no more than **0.5 foot-candle at a residential zoning boundary**, excluding public street lighting; and
- screening of generators, fuel tanks, exterior mechanical equipment, refuse areas, substations, and comparable equipment when visible from residential districts or public rights-of-way.

### Recommendation 6 - Protect water capacity without writing a project-specific rule

For any M-2 use expected to consume **25,000 gallons or more in a 24-hour period**, recommend requiring average and peak demand estimates, water-source identification, applicable DNR documentation, adequate-supply analysis, practical separate metering, and annual reporting.

Recommend prohibiting **once-through cooling using potable municipal water** for new Major Industrial Facilities. Closed-loop or non-evaporative systems should be preferred, while allowing engineered alternatives when water capacity and off-site impacts are demonstrated to be acceptable.

### Recommendation 7 - Require utility verification for very large electrical loads

Before a building permit is issued to an M-2 use with anticipated demand of **5 MW or more**, recommend requiring written verification from the serving electric utility that the maximum load has been disclosed, needed service infrastructure has been identified sufficiently to provide service, project-specific cost responsibility has been addressed between customer and utility, and the service can be constructed under applicable utility/electrical requirements.

The zoning ordinance should not attempt to regulate electric rates, generation procurement, service territories, or matters otherwise committed to the electric cooperative, state, or federal authorities.

### Recommendation 8 - Require emergency-response and hazardous-material planning

For Major Industrial Facilities, require an Emergency Response Plan identifying emergency contacts, site access, electrical disconnects, generators, fuel quantities and shutoffs, coolant/chemical inventories, battery-energy-storage systems, fire suppression, evacuation, spill response, and responder access.

Require updates after material facility modifications and at reasonable intervals requested by the City or responding agencies.

### Recommendation 9 - Require engineered stormwater review and project-caused infrastructure responsibility

Require all M-2 development to comply with applicable stormwater rules and require Major Industrial Facilities to submit engineered stormwater calculations addressing off-site flooding, erosion, and drainage.

Where a development creates a documented need for public improvements beyond existing capacity, recommend using lawful development/infrastructure agreements to allocate proportionate project-related costs to the applicant. The same rule should apply to every M-2 development creating the same type and degree of burden.

### Recommendation 10 - Require decommissioning and financial assurance

Require Major Industrial Facility applications to include a decommissioning plan covering expected facility life, equipment/structures to be removed, remediation, site restoration, estimated cost, salvage assumptions, and proposed financial assurance.

The City Attorney should determine the appropriate bonding, letter-of-credit, escrow, surety, or other lawful instrument and the method for periodically updating the amount.

### Recommendation 11 - Require renewed review for material expansion

Recommend renewed Special Exception review when an approved Major Industrial Facility increases an approved maximum by **20% or more** for electrical demand, water demand, stationary generation, energy storage, exterior mechanical-equipment capacity, or developed building area. Crossing a Major Industrial Facility threshold for the first time should trigger review regardless of percentage.

### Recommendation 12 - Keep project-specific economic promises outside the zoning text

The following matters should generally be negotiated through development, economic-development, utility, or other lawful agreements rather than inserted as project-specific zoning requirements:

- LEED or equivalent certification;
- local hiring or training commitments;
- wages and benefits;
- community-benefit payments;
- tax-incentive terms;
- local-contractor commitments;
- project-specific infrastructure reimbursement mechanics;
- voluntary operating caps below district-wide standards; and
- representations concerning jobs, investment, tenant mix, or future expansion.

If the City wishes to convert any of these concepts into a zoning rule, it should be rewritten to apply uniformly to similarly situated uses and reviewed for statutory authority.

## 4. Information that should still be obtained before final project approval

The committee does not need every final project document before it can recommend ordinance language. However, the following should be required or obtained before final approval/occupancy at the appropriate stage:

1. final site plan, architectural drawings, equipment schedule, and phasing plan;
2. final applicant/operator/tenant structure;
3. executed utility service/interconnection and cost-allocation agreements or appropriate utility verification;
4. utility load study and generation arrangement, to the extent releasable and relevant;
5. final cooling drawings, coolant inventory/SDS, average and peak water-use estimate;
6. final acoustic model including property lines, nearby residences, tonal/low-frequency sound, hot-weather full load, and generator testing;
7. generator make/model, aggregate capacity, fuel storage, emissions permitting, runtime limits, and testing schedule;
8. final stormwater and civil calculations;
9. fire protection, UPS/BESS, hazardous-material, spill-response, and emergency-response plans;
10. completed-project taxable/assessed value analysis and final incentive requests, if any;
11. decommissioning cost estimate and acceptable financial assurance; and
12. final development schedule and permit path.

## 5. Proposed October 19 committee work product

The October 19 advisory committee meeting should aim to produce a **marked-up recommendation**, not another open-ended list of concerns.

A practical agenda would be:

1. Confirm the district-wide uniformity principle.
2. Review the Major Industrial Facility thresholds.
3. Review the noise table and acoustic-study requirements.
4. Review generator hours/records, setbacks, lighting, screening, water/cooling, utility verification, emergency planning, stormwater, infrastructure, decommissioning, and expansion provisions.
5. Identify provisions requiring engineering input versus legal review.
6. Separate zoning provisions from project-specific development-agreement items.
7. Vote on a recommendation to transmit the draft, with identified revisions, to Planning & Zoning and the City Attorney.
8. Set a concrete next-step schedule for legal redlining, Planning & Zoning review/hearing, and Council consideration.

The committee is advisory. Final ordinance language, required hearings, introduction, amendment, and adoption remain with the bodies and officials that possess those powers under Cascade's code and Iowa law.

## 6. Suggested recommendation motion

> **Recommended motion:** Recommend that the City use the attached draft M-2 Heavy Industrial District Data Center and Performance Standards Amendment as the working document for City Attorney and Planning & Zoning review, subject to committee-approved revisions; that the ordinance continue to regulate similarly situated M-2 uses uniformly through neutral impact thresholds; and that staff distinguish district-wide zoning requirements from project-specific matters better addressed through development, utility, or economic-development agreements.

# Appendix A - Proposed Draft Zoning Ordinance

# DRAFT - M-2 Heavy Industrial District Data Center and Performance Standards Amendment

> **DRAFT - FOR DISCUSSION ONLY - NOT ADOPTED LAW**  
> Prepared October 6, 2026 for policy discussion and City Attorney / Planning & Zoning review.  
> This is a project-generated draft and is not legal advice. Section numbering, amendment mechanics, grandfathering, enforcement, and consistency with Iowa law should be reviewed by the City Attorney before introduction.
>
> **Uniformity principle:** The ordinance is intentionally written so that zoning and performance standards apply throughout the M-2 Heavy Industrial District to all similarly situated businesses. Data centers are added as an expressly recognized use type, while noise, lighting, screening, water, emergency planning, generators, infrastructure, and other impact standards apply district-wide or through neutral, objective impact thresholds.

**CITY OF CASCADE, IOWA**  
**ORDINANCE NO. ______**

## AN ORDINANCE AMENDING CHAPTER 165 OF THE CASCADE ZONING ORDINANCE TO EXPRESSLY PERMIT DATA CENTERS AND HIGH-DENSITY COMPUTING FACILITIES IN THE M-2 HEAVY INDUSTRIAL DISTRICT AND TO ESTABLISH UNIFORM PERFORMANCE STANDARDS FOR M-2 INDUSTRIAL DEVELOPMENT

**NOW, BE IT ENACTED by the City Council of the City of Cascade, Iowa, as follows:**

## SECTION I. SECTION 165.11 M-2 - PERMITTED PRINCIPAL USES

Section 165.11, M-2 Heavy Industrial District, subsection B, is amended by adding the following permitted principal use:

**19. Data Center or High-Density Computing Facility.**

A building or group of buildings principally used to house computer servers, data-processing equipment, networking equipment, electronic storage equipment, or other high-density computing systems, including cloud computing, colocation, artificial-intelligence computing, scientific computing, cryptocurrency computing or mining, and substantially similar uses.

**Minimum required off-street parking:** one space per employee on the largest regularly scheduled shift, plus one space for each company vehicle regularly stationed at the premises.

Computer rooms, telecommunications equipment, and server equipment that are incidental and subordinate to another permitted principal use shall not constitute a separate Data Center or High-Density Computing Facility.

## SECTION II. SECTION 165.11 M-2 - SPECIAL EXCEPTION USES

Section 165.11, M-2 Heavy Industrial District, subsection D, is amended by adding:

**8. Major Industrial Facility.**

Any otherwise permitted principal use within the M-2 District meeting one or more of the following thresholds shall require Special Exception approval pursuant to Section 165.34(2):

1. Maximum anticipated coincident electrical demand of **five megawatts (5 MW) or greater**;
2. Anticipated industrial, process, cooling, or facility water demand of **25,000 gallons or more during any twenty-four-hour period**;
3. Aggregate stationary electrical generation capacity, excluding fire pumps, of **two megawatts (2 MW) or greater**;
4. Aggregate stationary battery or other electrical-energy-storage capacity of **five megawatt-hours (5 MWh) or greater**; or
5. A material expansion of an existing facility that causes the facility to exceed any threshold above.

The requirements of this subsection shall apply **without regard to the business type, technology, product, customer, tenant, ownership structure, or industrial process involved.**

## SECTION III. SECTION 165.11 M-2 - UNIFORM PERFORMANCE STANDARDS

Subsection G, Special Requirements, is amended by adding the following provisions. These requirements supplement all other applicable provisions of Chapter 165.

### 1. Uniform Applicability

The requirements of this subsection apply uniformly to M-2 uses, structures, and operations unless a provision expressly establishes an objective physical or operational threshold. No requirement shall be imposed, waived, or varied solely because of the identity of the owner, operator, tenant, customer, industry, computing workload, product, or business model.

A lawful preexisting use, structure, or condition shall be treated in accordance with Sections 165.14 through 165.20 and other applicable law. To the maximum extent legally permissible, new construction, material expansion, replacement equipment, and materially modified operations after the effective date shall comply with the standards applicable to that work or modification.

### 2. Site Development Plan

Before issuance of a building permit for a new principal M-2 use or Major Industrial Facility, the applicant shall submit an engineered site development plan identifying, as applicable:

- buildings and structures;
- parking and loading areas;
- access roads;
- exterior mechanical and cooling equipment;
- generators;
- electrical substations and transformers;
- energy-storage systems;
- fuel and chemical storage;
- water and sewer connections;
- stormwater facilities;
- exterior lighting;
- landscaping and screening;
- fences;
- property boundaries; and
- existing sensitive receptors located within 1,000 feet of the property.

For a Major Industrial Facility, the plan shall additionally identify maximum anticipated electrical load, water demand, stationary generation capacity, fuel storage, cooling technology, principal noise-generating equipment, and proposed phasing.

### 3. Operational Noise

All M-2 uses shall be designed, constructed, operated, and maintained so that noise attributable to the use does not exceed the following standards, measured in accordance with generally accepted professional acoustic practice:

**a. M-2 property-line standard:** 65 dBA and 75 dBC hourly average at any property boundary, except where the residential/sensitive-receptor standard below is more restrictive.

**b. Residential or sensitive-receptor standard:** At the exterior wall or outdoor use area of an existing dwelling, school, licensed child-care facility, hospital, nursing facility, place of worship, public library, or comparable occupied sensitive use:

- 55 dBA and 65 dBC from 7:00 a.m. to 10:00 p.m.; and
- 50 dBA and 60 dBC from 10:00 p.m. to 7:00 a.m.

**c. Tonal noise:** Where an industrial sound source produces a prominent recurring tone, whine, screech, or hum, the applicable A-weighted limit shall be reduced by five (5) decibels.

**d. Measurement:** Noise measurements and modeling shall use generally accepted professional acoustic practices, including applicable ANSI/ASA S12.9 procedures or successor standards where appropriate.

### 4. Acoustic Study and Verification

Every Major Industrial Facility shall provide, at the applicant's expense:

1. A preconstruction baseline acoustic evaluation;
2. A predictive acoustic model demonstrating compliance at property boundaries and nearby sensitive receptors under maximum reasonably anticipated operating conditions, including hot-weather conditions where relevant;
3. A post-construction compliance test after the facility reaches normal operating load; and
4. A new compliance evaluation at least once every five (5) years, or following a material modification reasonably expected to affect noise levels.

The City may require reasonable corrective measures where testing demonstrates noncompliance.

### 5. Generator and Elevated-Noise Testing

Routine and pre-planned testing of stationary generators or other equipment producing substantially elevated noise shall occur only between **8:00 a.m. and 6:00 p.m., Monday through Friday**, except where operation outside those hours is:

1. Required during an actual emergency;
2. Necessary to protect life, property, or critical equipment;
3. Required by a governmental agency, applicable code, manufacturer, utility, insurer, or reliability authority; or
4. Specifically authorized by the Zoning Administrator because the testing cannot reasonably be completed during ordinary testing hours.

This restriction applies uniformly to M-2 businesses operating such equipment. Emergency-engine operation shall also comply with applicable state and federal air-quality requirements.

### 6. Generator Operating Records

Any M-2 facility having aggregate stationary generation capacity of two (2) MW or more shall maintain records identifying:

- date and duration of each operation;
- whether the operation was emergency or non-emergency;
- reason for operation; and
- equipment operated.

Records shall be retained for at least three (3) years and made available to the City upon reasonable request for zoning-compliance purposes, subject to applicable public-record and confidentiality law.

### 7. Major Industrial Facility Setbacks

Existing M-2 dimensional requirements shall remain applicable generally. In addition, for a new Major Industrial Facility:

1. A principal building shall be located at least **100 feet from a property boundary adjoining a residential zoning district**.
2. Stationary generators, exterior cooling equipment, large transformers, substations, compressors, industrial fans, and comparable exterior mechanical equipment shall be located at least **200 feet from a residential zoning district boundary**.
3. The Board of Adjustment may approve a reduced equipment setback, but never less than 100 feet, where an applicant-funded independent acoustic analysis demonstrates that all applicable noise standards will be met under maximum reasonably anticipated operating conditions.

Nothing in this subsection authorizes a setback reduction that conflicts with any other applicable code, easement, fire requirement, or utility clearance.

### 8. Exterior Lighting

All new or replacement exterior lighting within the M-2 District shall:

1. Be fully shielded and directed downward;
2. Be designed to prevent direct glare onto residential property and public streets;
3. Not exceed **0.5 foot-candle at a residential zoning boundary**, excluding existing public street lighting; and
4. Use automatic controls or reduced lighting levels during periods when full illumination is unnecessary, except for safety, security, and emergency lighting.

### 9. Screening

Exterior mechanical equipment, generators, fuel tanks, refuse areas, substations, and comparable industrial equipment visible from an adjoining residential district or public right-of-way shall be screened through landscaping, berms, walls, fencing, equipment enclosures, or a combination thereof.

Screening shall not interfere with required ventilation, fire access, utility clearance, security, or equipment safety.

### 10. Water Use and Cooling

Any M-2 use reasonably expected to use **25,000 gallons or more of water in a twenty-four-hour period** shall provide:

1. Projected average and peak water demand;
2. Identification of the proposed water source;
3. Applicable Iowa Department of Natural Resources permits, approvals, or documentation;
4. An engineering analysis demonstrating adequate water supply without unreasonable interference with existing users;
5. Separate metering of material industrial or cooling-water use where practicable; and
6. Annual water-use reporting to the City.

**Once-through cooling using potable municipal water is prohibited for new Major Industrial Facilities.**

Closed-loop or non-evaporative cooling shall be preferred. An evaporative system may be approved where professional engineering demonstrates that the alternative will not create an unreasonable demand upon municipal water resources and adequately controls drift, plume, condensation, and icing.

### 11. Electrical Utility Verification

Before issuance of a building permit for any M-2 use having maximum anticipated electrical demand of five (5) MW or more, the applicant shall provide written documentation from the serving electric utility confirming that:

1. The proposed maximum electrical demand has been disclosed;
2. Required utility infrastructure and service upgrades have been identified sufficiently for the utility to provide service;
3. Responsibility for project-specific utility improvements has been addressed between the utility and customer; and
4. The facility's electrical service may be constructed and operated in accordance with applicable utility, electrical, and safety requirements.

Nothing in this ordinance regulates electric rates, utility service territories, generation procurement, or matters preempted or otherwise committed by law to a public utility, electric cooperative, state agency, or federal authority.

### 12. Fuel, Batteries, Hazardous Materials, and Emergency Response

A Major Industrial Facility shall provide an Emergency Response Plan to the City and appropriate emergency-response agencies before occupancy.

The plan shall identify, where applicable:

- emergency contacts;
- facility access;
- electrical disconnects;
- generator locations;
- fuel quantities and shutoffs;
- coolant and chemical inventories;
- battery-energy-storage systems;
- fire-suppression systems;
- evacuation procedures;
- spill response; and
- procedures for providing emergency responders access to the site.

The plan shall be reviewed following material facility modifications and at reasonable intervals requested by the City or responding agencies.

### 13. Heat Rejection, Drift, Condensation, and Icing

No M-2 mechanical exhaust, cooling, or heat-rejection system shall be operated in a manner that causes ice, condensate, cooling-tower drift, mist, or similar discharge to accumulate upon an adjoining property or public right-of-way or create an unreasonable safety hazard.

### 14. Stormwater and Drainage

All new M-2 development shall comply with applicable City, state, and federal stormwater requirements.

A Major Industrial Facility shall submit engineered stormwater calculations demonstrating that development will not cause an unlawful or unreasonable increase in off-site flooding, erosion, or drainage impacts.

The property owner shall be responsible for repair of documented project-caused damage to City drainage infrastructure to the extent permitted by law.

### 15. Public Infrastructure

Where an M-2 development creates a documented need for road, water, sewer, drainage, emergency-service, or other public improvements beyond existing system capacity, the City may require a development or infrastructure agreement allocating proportionate project-related costs to the applicant to the extent permitted by Iowa law.

This provision shall apply equally to every M-2 development creating the same type and degree of infrastructure burden.

### 16. Decommissioning of Major Industrial Facilities

An application for a Major Industrial Facility shall include a decommissioning plan describing:

1. Anticipated facility life;
2. Equipment and structures to be removed;
3. Environmental remediation and site restoration;
4. Estimated decommissioning cost;
5. Salvage assumptions; and
6. Proposed method of financial assurance.

A facility shall be considered abandoned when its principal industrial operation has ceased continuously for twelve (12) months, excluding temporary shutdowns caused by reconstruction, documented market conditions accompanied by evidence of a bona fide intent to resume operations, casualty, litigation, government order, utility interruption, or similar circumstances approved by the Zoning Administrator.

Following abandonment, decommissioning shall be completed within twelve (12) months unless extended for good cause.

The form and amount of financial assurance shall be established in a manner approved by the City Attorney and shall be reasonably related to the documented cost of decommissioning and site restoration.

### 17. Material Expansion

An approved Major Industrial Facility shall require an updated Special Exception review where a proposed modification would increase any of the following by **twenty percent (20%) or more** above the previously approved maximum:

- electrical demand;
- water demand;
- stationary generation capacity;
- energy-storage capacity;
- exterior mechanical-equipment capacity; or
- developed building area.

Crossing a Major Industrial Facility threshold for the first time shall require Special Exception approval regardless of percentage increase.

### 18. Compliance, Inspection, and Corrective Action

The Zoning Administrator may require documentation reasonably necessary to verify compliance with conditions of approval and the objective standards of this section. Where specialized testing or engineering is reasonably required because of a credible compliance question involving a Major Industrial Facility, the City may require the owner or operator to provide qualified professional testing, subject to applicable law and any fee or cost-recovery provisions adopted by the City.

Nothing in this section limits other remedies available under Chapter 165 or applicable law.

## SECTION IV. SECTION 165.41 - DEFINITIONS

Section 165.41 is amended by adding the following definitions:

**Data Center or High-Density Computing Facility:** A building, structure, or portion thereof principally used for the operation of networked computer servers, electronic data-processing equipment, digital storage, artificial-intelligence computing, cloud computing, colocation, scientific computing, cryptocurrency computing or mining, or substantially similar high-density computing activities. Ordinary computer rooms, telecommunications equipment, and server equipment incidental and subordinate to another principal use are excluded.

**Major Industrial Facility:** Any M-2 use meeting one or more objective thresholds established in Section 165.11, M-2 Heavy Industrial District, subsection D(8).

**Material Expansion:** An enlargement or operational modification meeting the thresholds established in Section 165.11, M-2 Heavy Industrial District, subsection G(17).

**Sensitive Receptor:** An occupied dwelling, school, licensed child-care facility, hospital, nursing facility, place of worship, public library, public park facility, or comparable occupied use where protection from continuous industrial noise is reasonably necessary.

## SECTION V. CONFLICTS AND MORE RESTRICTIVE REQUIREMENTS

Nothing in this ordinance relieves a property owner or operator from compliance with any other applicable City, county, state, federal, utility, fire, building, environmental, or safety requirement. Where two applicable zoning provisions regulate the same subject and can lawfully be applied together, the more restrictive provision shall control, consistent with Section 165.03.

## SECTION VI. SEVERABILITY

If any section, subsection, paragraph, sentence, clause, phrase, or application of this ordinance is adjudged invalid or unconstitutional, such adjudication shall not affect the validity of the ordinance as a whole or any other section, provision, or application not adjudged invalid or unconstitutional.

## SECTION VII. EFFECTIVE DATE

This ordinance shall be in full force and effect after its final passage, approval, and publication as provided by law.

## B. Uniform District-Wide Approach

This draft intentionally avoids regulating Simple Mining, cryptocurrency mining, or data centers as uniquely disfavored businesses. It adds data centers as an expressly recognized M-2 use while regulating physical impacts through standards that apply throughout the district or through neutral thresholds such as MW demand, water use, generator capacity, and battery-storage capacity.

The approach should be reviewed for consistency with Iowa Code Chapter 414, including the zoning-uniformity provisions of Iowa Code section 414.2.

## D. Water Threshold

The 25,000-gallon-per-day threshold is used as an objective industrial-impact trigger because Iowa water-use permitting law generally uses a 25,000-gallon quantity in a 24-hour period as a significant regulatory threshold. City Attorney and engineering review should confirm the best local wording and interaction with Iowa DNR permitting.

## E. Acoustic Standards

The draft's dBA/dBC structure is intended to address both ordinary audible sound and low-frequency industrial hum. The reference to ANSI/ASA S12.9 provides a recognized environmental-noise measurement framework rather than creating a project-specific testing method.

## F. Stationary Generators

The draft distinguishes routine testing from actual emergencies and requires uniform testing-hour rules across M-2 businesses. Federal stationary-engine requirements should continue to control where applicable; zoning should not attempt to replace air permitting or emissions regulation.

## G. Items Better Addressed Outside the Zoning Ordinance

The following matters are generally better addressed through a development agreement, economic-development agreement, utility agreement, or other lawful contractual mechanism rather than being written as project-specific zoning requirements:

- LEED certification or equivalent sustainability certification;
- local hiring and workforce-development commitments;
- wage or benefit commitments;
- community-benefit payments;
- tax-incentive terms;
- project-specific infrastructure reimbursement mechanics;
- voluntary limits below otherwise applicable zoning thresholds; and
- project-specific representations concerning jobs, investment, tenant mix, or expansion.

If the City wants any of these concepts to become district-wide zoning rules, they should be rewritten to apply uniformly to all similarly situated M-2 businesses and reviewed for statutory authority.

## H. Required Legal Review Before Introduction

The City Attorney should specifically review:

1. Whether the Major Industrial Facility thresholds and Special Exception structure fit Cascade's current Section 165.34 procedure;
2. Application of new performance standards to existing lawful uses and nonconformities;
3. Authority and process for applicant-funded independent studies and compliance testing;
4. Financial-assurance authority and acceptable decommissioning instruments;
5. Cost-recovery and infrastructure-agreement authority;
6. Public-record treatment of security-sensitive facility information;
7. State or federal preemption affecting utilities, air emissions, fuel storage, batteries, and emergency systems;
8. Whether any separate general noise, nuisance, fire-code, or building-code amendments should accompany the zoning amendment; and
9. Final numbering and integration with all amendments to Chapter 165 adopted after the base zoning text.
