# Does Cascade's existing M-2 ordinance define distinct classes or kinds of buildings?

**Review date:** November 3, 2026. **Status:** review of the archived City zoning scan and identified subsequent amendments, **not a certified current codification or legal opinion**.

## Short answer

**Not in those words.** The original §165.11 M-2 Heavy Industrial District does **not expressly announce a separate taxonomy of “classes or kinds of buildings” for purposes of Iowa Code §414.2**. It does establish distinct **permitted principal uses and structures**, **accessory uses**, and **special-exception uses and structures**, and it imposes different parking, siting, screening and separation requirements on several of them.[^1]

Consequently, it would be incorrect either to say that **all M-2 uses must receive identical requirements** or to claim that every existing use listing is automatically a legally sufficient “class or kind of buildings” under §414.2. The statute requires uniform regulations for each class or kind of buildings **throughout each district**, while permitting different regulations between districts.[^2]

**For our proposed amendment:** Adding a data-center permitted-use entry need not, by itself, create a separate zoning district or authorize treating computer-server operators less favorably than materially comparable industrial facilities. Our defensible working recommendation remains **objective, uniformly administered M-2 performance triggers** for noise, water, electrical demand, generation, stored energy and neighboring land uses.[^2][^3]

## 1. What M-2 actually classifies

| Existing provision | Classification or rule present in the archived City ordinance | Is it an express §414.2 “class or kind of buildings”? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **§165.11(A) — Statement of Intent** | Describes **one M-2 Heavy Industrial District** for general manufacturing and related heavy-industrial uses, while protecting surrounding districts. | **No.** This is a district purpose statement, not a list of distinct building classes. |
| **§165.11(B)(1)–(17) — Permitted Principal Uses and Structures** | Lists farm-equipment sales/service, automotive operations, manufacturing and processing, concrete plants, warehouses, grain facilities, feed milling, machine shops, utility operations, terminals and other listed uses, with differing parking/loading requirements. | **Not expressly.** These are named permitted **uses and structures**. Their legal significance as classes depends on the specific rule being applied. |
| **§165.11(B)(18) — Fitness centers** | **Ordinance 05-22**, adopted October 10, 2022, adds fitness centers/exercise businesses, with one parking space per 200 square feet. | **A differentiated permitted use**, but not an express declaration that this is a standalone §414.2 building class.[^4] |
| **§165.11(C)(1)–(4) — Permitted Accessory Uses and Structures** | Addresses incidental uses, temporary construction buildings, caretaker dwellings and satellite receivers. | **No blanket designation.** Distinguishes accessory functions from principal land uses. |
| **§165.11(D)(1)–(7) — Special-Exception Uses and Structures** | Allows specified activities through the Board of Adjustment, subject to §165.34(2) and use-specific restrictions. | **Functional differentiated categories**, but the text does not identify them collectively as statutory “classes or kinds of buildings.” |
| **§165.11(E) — Yard, Lot and Height** | General M-2 dimensional controls: 25-foot front and rear yards; 20-foot side and street-side yards; 60-foot maximum height in the archived scan. | **Generally uniform baseline district dimensions**, subject to any enacted later amendment or other applicable rule. |
| **§165.11(F) — Signs; (G)(1) — Supplementary Rules** | Different sign standards for billboards, identification signs and wall/overhanging signs; G(1) incorporates §165.12. | **Regulates sign types and incorporates other requirements**, rather than defining general industrial building classes. |
| **§165.41 — Chapter-wide Definitions** | Defines “Building (Structure)” and various named terms, including “Junk Yard” and “Mini-Warehouse.” | **Provides vocabulary for applying the ordinance.** It does not expressly map each M-2 principal use to a distinct class of buildings under §414.2.[^5] |

## 2. Existing M-2 rules already treat certain industrial uses differently

The scanned M-2 text contains several concrete examples of differentiation **within the same district**.[^1]

| Existing special exception | Specific additional requirement described in the scanned text | What it demonstrates |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **D(1), stockyards, slaughterhouses and related animal operations** | One-quarter-mile separation from specified dwellings or public places, plus sanitation, drainage, fly-control, wind and parking conditions. | The City already considers impacts linked to an identified use type. |
| **D(3), chemical plants, explosive manufacture/storage, fertilizer blending, certain waste reduction and petroleum refining** | At least **1,000 feet** from specified dwellings, parks, schools, churches or assembly locations, with parking requirements. | The ordinance applies additional siting limits to certain listed hazardous or intensive industrial uses. |
| **D(4), automotive wrecking and junkyards** | Minimum **five-acre** site, frontage conditions, screening/fence and parking rules. | Some operational characteristics trigger rules that other M-2 businesses do not have. |
| **D(5), communications stations and towers** | A separation distance linked to **one-half the tower height** and other operating conditions. | A structure's specific physical form can justify a distinct measurable standard. |
| **D(7), adult-oriented establishments** | **1,600-foot** separation from defined protected uses and facilities; the provision supplies a method for measuring the distance. | The district already contains a detailed use-specific separation requirement. |

These examples **show that the City has distinguished certain uses in its text**. They do **not** prove that every distinction would withstand a challenge under §414.2, nor do they independently establish that data centers may be singled out for unrelated noise, water or energy restrictions. Different requirements should be evaluated in light of the class being regulated, the ordinance's purpose, the legislative record, consistency, and all applicable Iowa law.[^2][^3]

## 3. What §414.2 means for our amendment

The controlling sentence in the 2026 Iowa Code states:

> “All such regulations and restrictions shall be uniform for each class or kind of buildings throughout each district, but the regulations in one district may differ from those in other districts.”[^2]

**It does not say that every M-2 business must be regulated identically.** It also does **not automatically validate every named use as a distinct building class**. For Cascade, the question is whether any additional restriction is based on a defensible and consistently applied class or measurable impact, not simply the label of the applicant or its computing workload.

Consider two facilities, each with comparable stationary generators, equivalent nighttime sound at the same receptor, and similar electrical loads. A rule requiring noise testing **only because one contains servers** would demand a careful explanation of the regulatory distinction. By contrast, a rule triggered uniformly by **measured sound or installed generator capacity** directly addresses the physical effect.

A city may also establish a legitimate separate zoning district, subject to the usual approval process and statutory requirements. **Iowa Code §414.5** permits certain additional conditions in a rezoning or site-development-plan ordinance when the owner agreed to them **in writing before the hearing**, and they are reasonable and tied to public needs directly caused by the requested change. That statutory exception is distinct from imposing a data-center-only requirement generally throughout an existing district.[^6]

## 4. Implications for the November 3 proposed M-2 draft

| Draft component | Recommended treatment |
| --- | --- |
| **New §165.11(B)(19), Data Center or High-Density Computing Facility** | Treat this as a **proposed permitted-use listing**, not a new independent zoning district or an automatic finding of a uniquely regulated building class. |
| **New §165.11(D)(8), Major Industrial Facility** | Retain objective review triggers: **5 MW** peak coincident electrical demand, **25,000 gallons in 24 hours** water, **2 MW** aggregate stationary generation or **5 MWh** energy storage. Apply to every facility meeting the same trigger, whatever its line of business. |
| **Proposed §165.11(G)(2)–(19)** | Apply performance requirements consistently according to their stated physical-impact criteria, preserving the existing **G(1)** supplementary-regulations cross-reference. |
| **Noise, water, generators, cooling, screening and setbacks** | Base additional restrictions on measured operating characteristics, receptors, adjacency, supply-system effects and other defensible land-use considerations. A different rule for a genuinely different facility class needs a documented rationale and consistent application. |
| **Separate negotiated conditions** | Where legally available, use **§414.5** with the required written agreement, notice and project-caused nexus instead of improvised project-specific exceptions. |

**Proposed public explanation:** Cascade's original M-2 code differentiates among industrial land uses, including some with specific setbacks and other special-exception conditions. It does not expressly establish a separate statutory class for data-center buildings. Our proposed ordinance therefore adds data centers as a recognized use while applying new external-impact standards uniformly to similarly situated industrial facilities. A separate district or a justified, consistently defined class remains a potential legal alternative, subject to the City's established zoning procedures and legal review.[^1][^2][^6]

## 5. What remains unverified

The underlying scan is **historical**, not a certified complete version of all currently enacted amendments. We independently identified Ordinance 05-22 (2022), which added M-2 use B(18), and Ordinance 19-24 (2024), which adjusted fencing under §165.12(12). Other amendments could affect classification, dimensional rules or definitions. Deanna or the City Clerk should confirm the full adopted amendment record before anyone represents the analysis as an exhaustive statement of current law.[^4][^7]

The scanned text also contains OCR imperfections, particularly in part of D(2). The original PDF pages should govern any verbatim quote or enforcement decision.[^1]
