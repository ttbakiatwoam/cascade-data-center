# U.S. Chamber's 2026 economic study: Iowa figures and Cascade implications

**Independent review added November 4, 2026.** Original: U.S. Chamber of Commerce, *The Community and Economic Impacts of Data Centers*, published **October 15, 2026**.

[**Read the complete original PDF**](https://www.uschamber.com/assets/documents/The-Community-and-Economic-Impacts-of-Data-Centers.pdf) · [Publisher's report page](https://www.uschamber.com/infrastructure/the-community-and-economic-impacts-of-data-centers) · [Research provenance](https://github.com/ttbakiatwoam/cascade-data-center/blob/master/sources/economic-studies/US_CHAMBER_DATA_CENTER_IMPACTS_2026_SOURCE_RECORD.md) · [Detailed critical analysis](https://github.com/ttbakiatwoam/cascade-data-center/blob/master/dataset/impacts/45_CHAMBER_IOWA_ECONOMIC_IMPACTS_2026_CRITICAL_REVIEW.md)

## What does the study actually estimate for Iowa?

The Chamber modeled a hypothetical **50 MW, $1.69 billion** facility using a two-year construction phase and 20-year operation. Its **page 32** contains these statewide, **annual** estimates:<sup>[1](#note-chamber-1)</sup>

| Iowa economic measure | Construction, annual | Operations, annual |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Jobs supported in **all industries statewide** | **4,947** | **647** |
| Iowa GDP | **$592.47 million** | **$138.947 million** |
| Iowa personal income | **$343.235 million** | **$96.904 million** |
| Jobs in construction industry | 1,172 | 0 |
| Jobs in information industry | 18 | 53 |
| Jobs in retail trade | 1,276 | 188 |

**The 647 are not 647 jobs inside the data center or inside Cascade.** They include wider supply-chain and consumer-spending effects throughout Iowa. The Chamber's own methodology (p. 68) assumes **50 direct on-site data-center employees**.<sup>[2](#note-chamber-2)</sup>

## Compare the study with Simple Mining's Cascade proposal

| Item | U.S. Chamber's scenario | Cascade public description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Facility | 50 MW | Approximately 40 MW |
| Two-year investment | $1.69 billion | Approximately $400 million total investment projected |
| Direct facility workforce | 50 | Approximately 20–25 workers projected |
| Road/grid/water investment | Assumed $60m roads, $20m grid, $10m water if water cooling used | Final project-specific budgets and executed agreements not established |

The Cascade figures are **developer statements from the October 1 forum**, not guaranteed project terms. The Chamber also assumes $1 billion of electronic-equipment upgrades **every fifth year**. The difference in investment and staffing makes a simple 40/50 scaling unreliable.<sup>[2](#note-chamber-2)</sup><sup>[3](#note-chamber-3)</sup>

## Does Iowa have a wage advantage in the study?

Yes, for the **broad computing-infrastructure and hosting industry**. The Chamber's pages 9 and 11 report Iowa earnings of **$117,260** in NAICS 518210 versus **$67,340** across industries, citing Census Quarterly Workforce Indicators (2025 Q4). Earnings include bonuses and are not adjusted by occupation. **This is not the wage that Simple Mining has promised to pay.**<sup>[4](#note-chamber-4)</sup>

## What about tax revenue and power prices?

The Chamber models five-year **gross** tax impacts for **four counties outside Iowa** ranging from **$96 million to $174 million**. The figures exclude abatements and depend on local sales, real estate and business-property tax rules. **There is no Cascade municipal tax estimate in this study.**<sup>[5](#note-chamber-5)</sup>

The report also cites studies arguing that larger data-center loads do not inevitably raise average electricity rates. It does not examine **MVEC/CIPCO's actual costs or contract**. Its assumed grid and water infrastructure spending is **not an agreement to pay those amounts for Cascade**.

For independent, separately sourced City estimates, see [Cascade franchise fees and job-tax scenarios]({{ '/franchise-fee-and-jobs/' | relative_url }}) and [local power capacity evidence]({{ '/cipco-mvec-power/' | relative_url }}).

## Evaluation

The report is a **useful economic-benefit comparator**, especially because it provides Iowa numbers and identifies model assumptions. It is also a **business-advocacy publication based on simulated impacts**, not a measurement of a built Cascade facility. It cannot prove the project's actual local hires, property tax receipts, municipal utility costs, cooling-water requirements, neighborhood noise, or compliance with Iowa zoning law.

**Best next step:** Request a project-specific **net fiscal study** showing direct local employees and wages, construction labor sourcing, taxable values and exemptions, jurisdictional tax shares, the real MVEC tariff, and which party funds road, water, electrical, emergency-service and eventual closure costs.
