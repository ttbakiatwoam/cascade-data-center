## Original questions submitted

> 1. You are a part of city council working on drafting city ordinance around data centers. How would you go about defining a data center?
>
> 2. This is for Cascade, Iowa.

## What the answer contributes

The first response recommends a definition based on **primary activity**, with provisions for **multi-building campuses**, **electricity and floor-area triggers**, explicit **accessory-server-room exclusions**, and disclosures of water, cooling, power and backup-generation capacity. Those are legislative design choices, **not Iowa requirements**. A definition should cover all relevant computing business models while remaining technology-neutral.

## Legal corrections and limitations

| Topic | Verified finding |
| --- | --- |
| Iowa statutory definition | **§423.3(95)(f)(1)–(2)** defines data center and data center business for a sales/use-tax exemption. It does not itself set local zoning eligibility. [^1] |
| Investment thresholds | The $1m–$10m/$5m figures describe a **§423.4(7) partial-refund tier**, not what makes a facility a data center. The separate §423.3(95) exemption has materially different requirements. [^1][^2] |
| Property exemption | **§427.1(37)** covers qualified equipment, **not land and buildings**, when the statutory conditions are met. [^3] |
| Home rule and franchise fee | §364.1 allows city action within state-law limits; **§364.2(4)(f)** separately authorizes regulated franchise fees. A tax-exemption citation alone cannot answer the franchise-fee question. [^4] |
| Zoning and uniformity | §414.1 supports land-use regulation; **§414.2** requires uniform rules for each building class/kind within a district. §414.1's special nonconforming residential-home language is not a general industrial grandfathering rule. [^5] |
| Preemption and court cases | The source's claim that zoning might unlawfully prevent any data center is **not proven** by tax statutes alone. Its repeated court citations need case-by-case checking before relying on the claimed holdings. |

## Implication for Cascade's November 3 draft

Keep a **local zoning definition** separate from Iowa's **tax-incentive definition**. The existing proposal already uses a neutral **Major Industrial Facility** classification with impact triggers. Review the LexisNexis suggestions as potential refinements, but do not import an untested megawatt threshold, automatically exempt accessory infrastructure, or create unequal M-2 performance standards without examining Iowa §414.2.
