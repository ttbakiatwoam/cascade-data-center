# DRAFT - M-2 Heavy Industrial District Amendment (November 11 definitions; additional proposals for November 18, 2026)

> **DRAFT - FOR DISCUSSION ONLY - NOT ADOPTED LAW**  
> Original prepared October 6, 2026; October 26 draft preserved. This **November 11 committee-definition revision**, based on the November 3 proposal, records participant-reported votes on four definitions and avoids duplicating terminology already found in Cascade zoning/Iowa law. The proposed citywide Hyperscale prohibition and revised noise/testing provisions are **additional independent proposals for November 18**, not committee-adopted actions. Other performance standards remain independent project proposals. It is intended for City Attorney and Planning & Zoning review.  
> This is a project-generated draft and is not legal advice. Section numbering, amendment mechanics, grandfathering, enforcement, and consistency with Iowa law should be reviewed by the City Attorney before introduction.
>
> **Municipal ordinance comparison:** [Iowa city-first: hyperscale vs ordinary standalone data centers; Chapter 414 vs Chapter 335]({{ '/city-data-center-ordinances/' | relative_url }}) · [prior §414.2 uniformity research]({{ '/iowa-zoning-uniformity/' | relative_url }}). County ordinance comparisons are deferred to a separate stage.
>
> **Existing-law baseline:** [Does Cascade's M-2 ordinance already define classes or kinds of buildings?]({{ '/m2-existing-use-classes/' | relative_url }}) documents the permitted and special-exception categories and their existing use-specific conditions. The draft remains proposed law.
>
> **Uniformity principle:** The ordinance is intentionally written so that zoning and performance standards apply throughout the M-2 Heavy Industrial District to all similarly situated businesses. Non-hyperscale Data Centers are added as an expressly recognized M-2 use. Separately, a newly proposed citywide Hyperscale prohibition would apply throughout Cascade's lawful zoning jurisdiction, while noise, lighting, screening, water, emergency planning, generators, infrastructure, and other M-2 impacts are addressed through district-wide or neutral, objective thresholds.

**CITY OF CASCADE, IOWA**  
**ORDINANCE NO. ______**

## AN ORDINANCE AMENDING CHAPTER 165 OF THE CASCADE ZONING ORDINANCE TO PERMIT NON-HYPERSCALE DATA CENTERS IN THE M-2 HEAVY INDUSTRIAL DISTRICT, PROHIBIT HYPERSCALE DATA CENTERS THROUGHOUT THE CITY'S LAWFUL ZONING JURISDICTION, AND ESTABLISH UNIFORM PERFORMANCE STANDARDS FOR M-2 INDUSTRIAL DEVELOPMENT

**NOW, BE IT ENACTED by the City Council of the City of Cascade, Iowa, as follows:**

> **Amendment mechanics / controlling baseline.** This proposal uses §165.11 in the City's scanned Chapter 165 (the M-2 district is on original PDF pages 45–49), as modified by the October 10, 2022 Ordinance 05-22 adding fitness businesses as M-2 permitted use B(18). **Existing A, B(1)–(18), C(1)–(4), D(1)–(7), E, F, and G(1) remain unchanged.** This review also recognizes the 2024 fence ordinance in §165.12(12); no local requirement in this draft overrides that ordinance without a separately enacted amendment. The 2009 zero-lot-line document within the original scan is marked **DRAFT**, so adoption cannot be assumed. The City Clerk/Attorney must confirm the complete amendment history and current codification before introduction. See the [original M-2 scan and redlined comparison]({{ '/m2-concordance/' | relative_url }}).

## SECTION I. SECTION 165.11 M-2 - PERMITTED PRINCIPAL USES

<span class="m2-edit" style="color:#1c56a0"><strong>BLUE = copyedit/reference alignment; RED = substantive new proposed ordinance text. This is a working amendment, not enacted City law.</strong></span>

Iowa Code authorizes zoning of industrial land use and requires district-wise uniformity for classes or kinds of buildings; this proposal does not confer project approval.[^1][^2]

Section 165.11, M-2 Heavy Industrial District, subsection B, is amended by adding the following permitted principal use:

<span class="m2-add" style="color:#a41f27"><strong>19. Data Center.</strong></span>

<span class="m2-add" style="color:#a41f27">A Data Center as defined in Section 165.41 of this Ordinance, expressly excluding a Hyperscale Data Center. Hyperscale Data Centers are prohibited in all City zoning districts as provided in proposed Section 165.12(17).</span>

**Minimum required off-street parking:** one space per employee on the largest regularly scheduled shift, plus one space for each company vehicle regularly stationed at the premises.

> <span class="m2-edit" style="color:#1c56a0"><strong>Noncodified drafting note:</strong> Section 165.11(B)(19) now cross-references the committee-approved Data Center definition, and expressly excludes Hyperscale Data Centers. The committee reportedly approved the Hyperscale definition 5-3; that definition vote is not itself approval of the proposed citywide prohibition, the parking ratio, or any other operative performance provision.</span>

## SECTION I-A. SECTION 165.12 - SUPPLEMENTARY DISTRICT REGULATIONS: PROHIBITED USE

<span class="m2-add" style="color:#a41f27">Section 165.12 is amended by adding a new subsection 17, subject to confirmation of its current numbering:</span>

<span class="m2-add" style="color:#a41f27"><strong>17. Hyperscale Data Centers - Prohibited in All Zoning Districts.</strong></span>

<span class="m2-add" style="color:#a41f27">A Hyperscale Data Center, as defined in Section 165.41, is a prohibited land use in every zoning district regulated by this Ordinance within the City of Cascade's lawful zoning jurisdiction. A Hyperscale Data Center shall not be approved as a permitted principal use, accessory use, special exception, conditional use, or as a component of an otherwise permitted use. No zoning, development, building, or other City land-use approval shall be issued for the initial establishment, construction, conversion, or expansion of a facility to a Hyperscale Data Center.</span>

<span class="m2-add" style="color:#a41f27">The Major Industrial Facility special-exception review in Section 165.11(D)(8) does not make a Hyperscale Data Center eligible for approval. Project phases and functionally integrated buildings or facilities under a unified development plan shall be reviewed together to determine whether the defined Hyperscale Data Center thresholds are met; separate bona fide facilities shall not be aggregated solely because they are neighbors. Any change to a lawful permitted Data Center that would meet both Hyperscale Data Center thresholds after the change is prohibited unless and until the governing zoning ordinance is lawfully amended.</span>

<span class="m2-add" style="color:#a41f27">This subsection applies within the incorporated City and any territory in which Cascade has validly exercised municipal zoning jurisdiction under Iowa Code Section 414.23. It does not claim authority over unincorporated territory zoned by a county or other land beyond the City's legally established jurisdiction. It does not extinguish lawful existing nonconforming-use rights or displace appeal and variance authority independently granted by state law.</span>[^31]

> <span class="m2-edit" style="color:#1c56a0"><strong>Drafting note (noncodified):</strong> This citywide prohibition is a new project proposal, not a policy separately approved by the committee's November 11 vote of 5-3 on the definition of Hyperscale Data Center. The City Attorney must review jurisdiction, Iowa zoning uniformity, permissible land-use exclusions, any variance/vested-rights issues, and the objective method of determining facility floor area and electrical demand before introduction. The addition of Section 165.12(17) assumes Section 165.12(16) is the last subsection in the current codification; verify with the City Clerk.</span>

## SECTION II. SECTION 165.11 M-2 - SPECIAL EXCEPTION USES

Section 165.11, M-2 Heavy Industrial District, subsection D, is amended by adding the following **proposed review classification**. The Board of Adjustment's statutory powers, including special exceptions and the separate standards governing variances, must be observed.[^3]

<span class="m2-add" style="color:#a41f27"><strong>8. Major Industrial Facility.</strong></span>

Any otherwise permitted principal use within the M-2 District meeting one or more of the following thresholds shall require Special Exception approval pursuant to Section 165.34(2). <span class="m2-edit" style="color:#1c56a0">This special-exception classification does not authorize a Hyperscale Data Center prohibited by Section 165.12(17):</span>

1. Maximum anticipated coincident electrical demand of **five megawatts (5 MW) or greater**;
2. Anticipated industrial, process, cooling, or facility water demand of **25,000 gallons or more during any twenty-four-hour period**;
3. Aggregate stationary electrical generation capacity, excluding fire pumps, of **two megawatts (2 MW) or greater**;
4. Aggregate stationary battery or other electrical-energy-storage capacity of **five megawatt-hours (5 MWh) or greater**; or
5. A material expansion of an existing facility that causes the facility to exceed any threshold above.

The requirements of this subsection shall apply **without regard to the business type, technology, product, customer, tenant, ownership structure, or industrial process involved.** Similar physical impacts shall be treated consistently, and the City shall document the comprehensive-plan and public-welfare rationale for each objective threshold.[^2][^4]

## SECTION III. SECTION 165.11 M-2 - UNIFORM PERFORMANCE STANDARDS

**Existing §165.11(G)(1), “Supplementary District Regulations (Section 165.12),” remains unchanged.** Add the following as §165.11(G)(2) through (19), immediately after the existing G(1). These provisions supplement, rather than replace, existing M-2 requirements and §165.12. Existing allowed uses, special exceptions, yard and height limits, signs, and later amendments remain in force.

### <span class="m2-add" style="color:#a41f27">2. Uniform Applicability</span>

The requirements of this subsection apply uniformly to M-2 uses, structures, and operations unless a provision expressly establishes an objective physical or operational threshold. No requirement shall be imposed, waived, or varied solely because of the identity of the owner, operator, tenant, customer, industry, computing workload, product, or business model.

A lawful preexisting use, structure, or condition shall be treated in accordance with Sections 165.14 through 165.20 and other applicable law. To the maximum extent legally permissible, new construction, material expansion, replacement equipment, and materially modified operations after the effective date shall comply with the standards applicable to that work or modification.

### <span class="m2-add" style="color:#a41f27">3. Site Development Plan</span>

Before issuance of a building permit for a new principal M-2 use or Major Industrial Facility, the applicant shall submit an engineered site development plan identifying, as applicable:

- buildings and structures;
- parking and loading areas;
- access roads;
- exterior mechanical and cooling equipment;
- generators;
- electrical substations and transformers;
- energy-storage systems;
- fuel and chemical storage;
- water and sewer connections;
- stormwater facilities;
- exterior lighting;
- landscaping and screening;
- fences;
- property boundaries; and
- existing sensitive receptors located within 1,000 feet of the property.

For a Major Industrial Facility, the plan shall additionally identify maximum anticipated electrical load, water demand, stationary generation capacity, fuel storage, cooling technology, principal noise-generating equipment, and proposed phasing.

For any Major Industrial Facility using stationary backup or on-site generation, the applicant shall provide a generator schedule identifying each engine's proposed manufacturer/model, rated electrical and thermal capacity, fuel type, exhaust configuration, fuel tank and containment, anticipated emergency and non-emergency operating modes, reasonably foreseeable simultaneous-operation scenarios, planned testing frequency and duration, and applicable state or federal emissions permits or permit-exemption determinations. Information requiring legitimate security protection may be submitted in an appropriately redacted public version and a protected version, only as authorized by Iowa law. The City shall not purport to issue or replace air-quality permits.

### <span class="m2-add" style="color:#a41f27">4. Operational Noise</span>

All M-2 uses shall be designed, constructed, operated, and maintained so that noise attributable to the use does not exceed the following standards, measured in accordance with generally accepted professional acoustic practice:

**a. M-2 property-line standard:** The facility-attributable one-hour sound level at any property boundary shall not exceed <span class="m2-add" style="color:#a41f27"><strong>65 dBA and 75 dBC from 7:00 a.m. to 10:00 p.m.; or 60 dBA and 70 dBC from 10:00 p.m. to 7:00 a.m.</strong></span> <span class="m2-edit" style="color:#1c56a0">Compliance with the sensitive-receptor measurement provisions below is assessed separately.</span>

**b. Residential or sensitive-receptor standard:** At each existing dwelling, school, licensed child-care facility, hospital, nursing facility, place of worship, public library, or comparable occupied sensitive use, compliance shall be evaluated **inside the closest normally occupied room on the side of the structure nearest the M-2 noise source**. The initial assessment shall include the nearest existing qualifying occupied structure. Additional qualifying structures shall be evaluated where source orientation, terrain, or shielding could produce higher exposure.

The sound-level meter microphone shall be placed **within five (5) feet of the interior face of the nearest source-facing exterior wall**, but **at least 1.5 feet (0.5 meter) from that wall and other large reflecting surfaces**, where physically practicable, at approximately four (4) to five (5) feet above the finished floor. **All doors and windows shall be closed throughout the measurement.** **In a dwelling with a controllable forced-air HVAC blower, the fan control shall be set to ON for continuous operation during the entire indoor measurement, with the thermostat set within 65°F–75°F where reasonably practicable; the detailed protocol in subsection G(5) applies.** The qualified acoustical professional shall identify the room and measurement positions and document any necessary deviation from these placement requirements. The following **indoor, facility-attributable one-hour equivalent continuous sound levels (Leq,1h)** shall not be exceeded:

- <span class="m2-add" style="color:#a41f27"><strong>65 dBA and 75 dBC from 7:00 a.m. to 10:00 p.m.</strong></span>; and
- <span class="m2-add" style="color:#a41f27"><strong>60 dBA and 70 dBC from 10:00 p.m. to 7:00 a.m.</strong></span>.

**c. Tonal noise:** Where an industrial sound source produces a prominent recurring tone, whine, screech, or hum, the applicable A-weighted limit shall be reduced by five (5) decibels.

**d. Measurement:** The one-hour average is the energy-equivalent continuous sound level (Leq,1h), not an arithmetic mean of decibel readings. A qualified acoustical professional shall use a calibrated Type 1 or Type 2 sound-level meter appropriate for the purpose, identify A- or C-weighting, document meteorology for exterior measurements, receiving location, background sources, room conditions for interior measurements, and source operating conditions. Outdoor source assessment shall use a documented ANSI/ASA S12.9 or successor environmental-noise assessment method. Indoor sensitive-receptor assessment shall use a documented method appropriate for room sound and low-frequency industrial noise, including ANSI/ASA S12.72 and S12.9 Part 7 where applicable.[^17][^18] The City shall evaluate only facility-attributable noise using a documented background-noise adjustment procedure, accounting for unrelated indoor sound sources. Entry into an occupied structure for testing requires occupant consent or other lawful authority. If access is unavailable, the professional may report modeled indoor exposure with explicit assumptions, but shall not represent an outdoor measurement as a direct indoor compliance test. These measurement protocols shall not be construed to set a new statewide noise standard.[^5][^6]

**e. Voluntary indoor access and outdoor alternative:** The owner or lawful occupant of a sensitive-receptor structure may decline voluntary indoor noise testing. Declining consent does not invalidate a complaint or waive other remedies authorized by law. Interior or exterior entry onto private property requires consent or an independent lawful basis.

If indoor testing is declined, the qualified acoustical professional shall measure the facility-attributable Leq,1h outdoors at the point on the sensitive-receptor parcel boundary closest to the industrial property, provided the location is lawfully accessible and acoustically representative. Otherwise, a nearby lawfully accessible representative location shall be selected and the departure documented. The report shall document background conditions, microphone position, source operating conditions and any distance, shielding or reflection adjustments needed to estimate sound incident on the protected structure.

To assess the proposed indoor thresholds, the professional shall use documented, source-spectrum-appropriate outdoor-to-indoor noise reduction (OINR) or equivalent acoustical transmission-loss data for the building assembly, including walls, windows, doors and openings. Separate A- and C-weighted estimates and low-frequency assessment shall be provided where applicable. A calculated indoor result shall be labeled an **estimate**, not a direct indoor measurement. The applicable M-2 property-line limit remains unchanged.

**Exterior-wall insulation R-values in Iowa energy requirements measure thermal resistance, not acoustical isolation.** R-values may inform assembly identification but shall not be converted directly into dBA/dBC corrections. If reliable facade sound-reduction evidence is unavailable, a zero-decibel reduction may be used for cautious preliminary screening only, not by itself as conclusive proof of an indoor violation. The source of any acoustic correction and its uncertainty shall be disclosed.[^21][^23]

### <span class="m2-add" style="color:#a41f27">5. Acoustic Study and Verification</span>

Every Major Industrial Facility shall provide, at the applicant's expense:

1. A **preconstruction baseline acoustic evaluation**, completed before site construction or grading materially changes ambient sound, documenting representative exterior A- and C-weighted hourly equivalent levels (Leq,1h) at nearby sensitive-receptor parcels and M-2 property boundaries. The evaluation shall include **voluntary indoor baseline measurements** inside nearby existing sensitive-receptor structures wherever the property owner or lawful occupant consents, as specified below;
2. A predictive acoustic model demonstrating compliance at property boundaries and nearby sensitive receptors under maximum reasonably anticipated operating conditions, including hot-weather conditions where relevant;
3. A post-construction compliance evaluation within sixty (60) days after commencing normal operations at reasonably representative load and weather conditions, or within a documented alternative period approved for commissioning constraints; and
4. A recurring compliance evaluation at least once every three (3) years, or following a material modification reasonably expected to affect noise levels.

**Preconstruction indoor-receptor baseline and consent.** The applicant shall identify the closest existing dwelling or other sensitive-receptor structures and any additional receptors reasonably expected to receive greater facility-related noise because of equipment orientation, terrain, or shielding. Before construction, the applicant shall offer the owner or lawful occupant of each selected structure an opportunity, **at no cost to that owner or occupant**, to consent to baseline testing within the nearest normally occupied room facing the proposed industrial source. When consent is granted, a qualified acoustical professional shall record representative **daytime and nighttime indoor dBA and dBC Leq,1h levels** under the placement, closed-door, and closed-window conditions described in subsection G(4)(b), together with operating indoor HVAC or ventilation conditions, existing sound sources, weather where relevant, and measurement times. The study should obtain paired exterior measurements at the same structure when lawful access and conditions permit. This provides a documented **preconstruction ambient baseline**, not a presumption that existing sound is attributable to the proposed facility.

**Declined or unavailable access.** Neither a property owner nor a lawful occupant shall be required by this subsection to permit voluntary entry. The applicant shall document reasonable efforts to offer indoor baseline testing and record whether consent was granted or declined without disclosing unnecessary private household information. Where indoor consent is declined or lawful access is unavailable, the preconstruction study shall perform the exterior alternative in subsection G(4)(e), including a source-facing receptor-parcel boundary measurement **only where lawful access is available**, or the nearest suitable lawful exterior location. Any estimated interior baseline shall use independently substantiated acoustical transmission-loss assumptions; **Iowa exterior-wall thermal R-values shall not be converted into decibel adjustments**. Estimated indoor levels shall be identified as *modeled*, not as measured values. A refusal of voluntary interior testing shall not be treated as a waiver of the applicable noise protections.

**Thermostat and HVAC fan setting during indoor testing.** During every indoor preconstruction baseline, postconstruction verification, and complaint-response acoustic measurement, **the forced-air HVAC circulation fan shall be set to ON for continuous operation throughout the entire measurement interval**, wherever the building has an occupant-controllable fan ON setting. **AUTO is not the default for this test.** For dwellings, the heating/cooling thermostat setpoint shall be within **65°F to 75°F (inclusive)** where reasonably practicable and consistent with occupant health and safety. Heating and cooling demand may still cycle normally in response to the thermostat; the blower must remain on between those calls. Do not deliberately force heating or cooling cycles. The qualified acoustic professional shall verify and record the actual continuous blower operation, fan speed or mode, system type, thermostat setpoint and mode, measured interior temperature, heating/cooling cycles, other ventilation, and any deviation. These identical test settings shall be used for the comparable preconstruction and postconstruction measurements, or differences must be explained and accounted for in the technical assessment. Continuous fan sound is part of the measured indoor background and may mask industrial noise; the report shall distinguish facility-attributable sound from HVAC sound using a technically appropriate method. Supplemental measurements under other fan settings may inform the assessment but do not replace the required fan-ON measurement. If no blower or continuous-ON control is present, or use would be unsafe or medically inappropriate, document the reason and measure under representative existing conditions; lack of such equipment does not invalidate a complaint. The **65–75°F setpoint and mandatory fan-ON protocol are proposed local measurement conditions**, not Iowa residential code requirements.[^25]

**Evidence for a claimed increase after construction.** A finding that a Major Industrial Facility **increased noise inside a neighboring sensitive-receptor structure** shall be supported by reliable evidence of the **preconstruction indoor baseline** and a comparable **postconstruction indoor level**, measured in the same room when practicable, **with exterior doors and windows closed**, using the same A- and C-weighted Leq,1h metrics and comparable daytime/nighttime periods, ventilation settings, and sound-source conditions. The analysis must establish that the later level is higher than the earlier level beyond the stated measurement uncertainty and must separately substantiate the amount **attributable to the facility**, rather than assuming all ambient sound comes from that facility. A preconstruction home need not have been silent; the relevant question is whether its indoor noise demonstrably increased because of the facility.[^24]

Where an owner declined the preconstruction indoor evaluation, the City may consider credible earlier sound surveys, contemporaneous records, and the documented exterior-to-interior acoustic model described in subsection G(4)(e). An unsupported assertion of an increase alone shall not establish a violation **based solely on a change from preconstruction conditions**. This evidentiary requirement **does not prevent anyone from submitting a complaint**, does not require a historical baseline to investigate or enforce an **absolute indoor or M-2 property-line limit**, and does not displace rights or remedies under Iowa nuisance law.[^5][^24]

The required acoustic model and post-startup testing shall examine ordinary peak operations, reasonably foreseeable simultaneous generator testing or emergency backup operation, identifiable low-frequency or tonal equipment sources, and nearby residential and other sensitive receptors. Where safety or operational requirements prevent actual simultaneous generator testing, a qualified professional may combine measured source data with documented predictive modeling, with assumptions stated in the report.

Any separate structure-borne or ground-vibration concern must be evaluated with measurements appropriate to vibration (such as peak particle velocity), not inferred from dBA/dBC sound measurements. A numeric vibration ceiling is **not adopted in this draft** pending independent engineering and legal review.

The City may require reasonable corrective measures where testing demonstrates noncompliance.

### <span class="m2-add" style="color:#a41f27">6. Generator and Elevated-Noise Testing</span>

Routine and pre-planned testing of stationary generators or other equipment producing substantially elevated noise shall occur only between <span class="m2-add" style="color:#a41f27"><strong>6:00 a.m. and 8:00 p.m., Monday through Friday</strong></span>, except where operation outside those hours is:

1. Required during an actual emergency;
2. Necessary to protect life, property, or critical equipment;
3. Required by a governmental agency, applicable code, manufacturer, utility, insurer, or reliability authority; or
4. Specifically authorized by the Zoning Administrator because the testing cannot reasonably be completed during ordinary testing hours.

This restriction applies uniformly to M-2 businesses operating such equipment. "Emergency generator" as classified for Iowa air-quality permitting follows applicable state air regulations; local zoning testing-hour rules do not alter air-permit classifications or authorize impermissible emissions.[^7]

> <span class="m2-edit" style="color:#1c56a0"><strong>Blue - cross-reference clarification (noncodified):</strong> The testing window begins at 6:00 a.m., but the proposed nighttime noise limits of 60 dBA / 70 dBC remain in effect until 7:00 a.m. Permission to conduct testing during an hour does not, by itself, exempt that activity from applicable noise limits.</span>

### <span class="m2-add" style="color:#a41f27">7. Generator Operating Records</span>

Any M-2 facility having aggregate stationary generation capacity of two (2) MW or more shall maintain records identifying:

- date and duration of each operation;
- whether the operation was emergency or non-emergency;
- reason for operation; and
- equipment operated.

Records shall be retained for at least three (3) years and made available to the City upon reasonable request for zoning-compliance purposes, subject to applicable public-record and confidentiality law.

### <span class="m2-add" style="color:#a41f27">8. Major Industrial Facility Setbacks</span>

Existing M-2 dimensional requirements shall remain applicable generally. In addition, for a new Major Industrial Facility:

1. A principal building shall be located at least **100 feet from a property boundary adjoining a residential zoning district**.
2. Stationary generators, exterior cooling equipment, large transformers, substations, compressors, industrial fans, and comparable exterior mechanical equipment shall be located at least **200 feet from a residential zoning district boundary**.
3. The Board of Adjustment may approve a reduced equipment setback, but never less than 100 feet, where an applicant-funded independent acoustic analysis demonstrates that all applicable noise standards will be met under maximum reasonably anticipated operating conditions.

Nothing in this subsection authorizes a setback reduction that conflicts with any other applicable code, easement, fire requirement, or utility clearance.

### <span class="m2-add" style="color:#a41f27">9. Exterior Lighting</span>

All new or replacement exterior lighting within the M-2 District shall:

1. Be fully shielded and directed downward;
2. Be designed to prevent direct glare onto residential property and public streets;
3. Not exceed **0.5 foot-candle at a residential zoning boundary**, excluding existing public street lighting; and
4. Use automatic controls or reduced lighting levels during periods when full illumination is unnecessary, except for safety, security, and emergency lighting.

### <span class="m2-add" style="color:#a41f27">10. Screening</span>

Exterior mechanical equipment, generators, fuel tanks, refuse areas, substations, and comparable industrial equipment visible from an adjoining residential district or public right-of-way shall be screened through landscaping, berms, walls, fencing, equipment enclosures, or a combination thereof.

Screening shall not interfere with required ventilation, fire access, utility clearance, security, or equipment safety.

### <span class="m2-add" style="color:#a41f27">11. Water Use and Cooling</span>

Any M-2 use reasonably expected to use **25,000 gallons or more of water in a twenty-four-hour period** shall provide:

1. Projected average and peak water demand;
2. Identification of the proposed water source;
3. Applicable Iowa Department of Natural Resources permits, approvals, or documentation, with any determination of a **regulated** or **nonregulated** water use made under Iowa Code §455B.261 and applicable implementing rules, not inferred from the City's impact-review threshold alone;[^8]
4. An engineering analysis demonstrating adequate water supply without unreasonable interference with existing users;
5. Separate metering of material industrial or cooling-water use where practicable; and
6. Annual water-use reporting to the City.

**Once-through cooling using potable municipal water is prohibited for new Major Industrial Facilities.**

Closed-loop or non-evaporative cooling shall be preferred. An evaporative system may be approved where professional engineering demonstrates that the alternative will not create an unreasonable demand upon municipal water resources and adequately controls drift, plume, condensation, and icing.

### <span class="m2-add" style="color:#a41f27">12. Electrical Utility Verification</span>

Before issuance of a building permit for any M-2 use having maximum anticipated electrical demand of five (5) MW or more, the applicant shall provide written documentation from the serving electric utility confirming that:

1. The proposed maximum electrical demand has been disclosed;
2. Required utility infrastructure and service upgrades have been identified sufficiently for the utility to provide service;
3. Responsibility for project-specific utility improvements has been addressed between the utility and customer; and
4. The facility's electrical service may be constructed and operated in accordance with applicable utility, electrical, and safety requirements.

Nothing in this ordinance regulates electric rates, utility service territories, generation procurement, or matters preempted or otherwise committed by law to a public utility, electric cooperative, state agency, or federal authority.

### <span class="m2-add" style="color:#a41f27">13. Fuel, Batteries, Hazardous Materials, and Emergency Response</span>

A Major Industrial Facility shall provide an Emergency Response Plan to the City and appropriate emergency-response agencies before occupancy.

The plan shall identify, where applicable:

- emergency contacts;
- facility access;
- electrical disconnects;
- generator locations;
- fuel quantities and shutoffs;
- coolant and chemical inventories;
- battery-energy-storage systems;
- fire-suppression systems;
- evacuation procedures;
- spill response; and
- procedures for providing emergency responders access to the site.

The plan shall be reviewed following material facility modifications and at reasonable intervals requested by the City or responding agencies.

### <span class="m2-add" style="color:#a41f27">14. Heat Rejection, Drift, Condensation, and Icing</span>

No M-2 mechanical exhaust, cooling, or heat-rejection system shall be operated in a manner that causes ice, condensate, cooling-tower drift, mist, or similar discharge to accumulate upon an adjoining property or public right-of-way or create an unreasonable safety hazard.

### <span class="m2-add" style="color:#a41f27">15. Stormwater and Drainage</span>

All new M-2 development shall comply with applicable City, state, and federal stormwater requirements.

A Major Industrial Facility shall submit engineered stormwater calculations demonstrating that development will not cause an unlawful or unreasonable increase in off-site flooding, erosion, or drainage impacts, **subject to Iowa Code §364.3 limitations on municipal runoff and topsoil requirements**. An independently imposed more restrictive runoff standard may require the City to bear specified engineering, construction, or property costs, and shall not be presumed authorized by this zoning section.[^9]

The property owner shall be responsible for repair of documented project-caused damage to City drainage infrastructure to the extent permitted by law.

### <span class="m2-add" style="color:#a41f27">16. Public Infrastructure</span>

Where an M-2 development creates a documented need for road, water, sewer, drainage, emergency-service, or other public improvements beyond existing system capacity, the City may require a development or infrastructure agreement allocating proportionate project-related costs to the applicant to the extent permitted by Iowa law.

This provision shall apply equally to every M-2 development creating the same type and degree of infrastructure burden.

Where substantial construction hauling is reasonably expected to affect City roads, the City may, pursuant to an independently authorized permit or written road-use agreement, require a preconstruction photographic/engineering condition survey, defined haul routes and axle/load limits, reasonable maintenance and restoration terms, postconstruction verification, and proportionate financial security supported by an engineering estimate. No fixed multiplier or new fee is prescribed here without legal review.

### <span class="m2-add" style="color:#a41f27">17. Decommissioning of Major Industrial Facilities</span>

An application for a Major Industrial Facility shall include a decommissioning plan describing:

1. Anticipated facility life;
2. Equipment and structures to be removed;
3. Environmental remediation and site restoration;
4. Estimated decommissioning cost;
5. Salvage assumptions; and
6. Proposed method of financial assurance.

A facility shall be considered abandoned when its principal industrial operation has ceased continuously for twelve (12) months, excluding temporary shutdowns caused by reconstruction, documented market conditions accompanied by evidence of a bona fide intent to resume operations, casualty, litigation, government order, utility interruption, or similar circumstances approved by the Zoning Administrator.

Following abandonment, decommissioning shall be completed within twelve (12) months unless extended for good cause.

The form and amount of financial assurance shall be established in a manner approved by the City Attorney and shall be reasonably related to the documented cost of decommissioning and site restoration.

A qualified professional's decommissioning cost estimate shall be reviewed at least once every three (3) years and following a material expansion or ownership/operator change that materially alters restoration obligations. The City may require adjustment of assurance to the reasonable documented cost of performance, including contingencies, only through a legally authorized and objectively administered instrument.

To the extent enforceable under Iowa law, approval conditions and any separate recorded agreement shall identify responsible owners and operators, notice requirements upon transfer, acceptable successor security, and procedures for default and lawful recovery. Private contracts shall not transfer away obligations imposed by law or approval conditions.

### <span class="m2-add" style="color:#a41f27">18. Material Expansion</span>

An approved Major Industrial Facility shall require an updated Special Exception review where a proposed modification would increase any of the following by **twenty percent (20%) or more** above the previously approved maximum:

- electrical demand;
- water demand;
- stationary generation capacity;
- energy-storage capacity;
- exterior mechanical-equipment capacity; or
- developed building area.

Crossing a Major Industrial Facility threshold for the first time shall require Special Exception approval regardless of percentage increase.

### <span class="m2-add" style="color:#a41f27">19. Compliance, Inspection, and Corrective Action</span>

The Zoning Administrator may require documentation reasonably necessary to verify compliance with conditions of approval and the objective standards of this section. Where specialized testing or engineering is reasonably required because of a credible compliance question involving a Major Industrial Facility, the City may require the owner or operator to provide qualified professional testing, subject to applicable law and any fee or cost-recovery provisions adopted by the City.

Each Major Industrial Facility shall submit a brief annual compliance certification to the Zoning Administrator by March 1 for the prior calendar year, identifying the facility owner and operator; material changes in electricity demand, generation equipment, cooling and water use; whether prescribed acoustic tests and generator logs have been maintained; reported material incidents or violations; and the status of financial assurance. The certification is a compliance statement, not automatic approval of unverified representations.

Where reasonably necessary and authorized by an adopted fee schedule or agreement, the City may retain or approve an independent qualified professional to review applicant studies and required remedial testing at reasonable, documented applicant cost. For complex matters, the City may require a deposit or escrow established through lawful procedures with an accounting and refund of unused amounts. The applicant must not control or direct the independent expert's findings.

Nothing in this section limits other remedies available under Chapter 165 or applicable law.

## SECTION IV. SECTION 165.41 - DEFINITIONS

Section 165.41 is amended **only for terms not already defined by Cascade law**, subject to reconciliation with a certified current code. **Do not add new local definitions of Building, Structure, Lot, Dwelling, Accessory Use, or Construction.** Cascade §165.41 already defines Building (Structure), Structure (Building), Lot, Dwelling Unit, and Accessory Use; Iowa Code §103A.3(5) defines Construction within the state building-code program. Existing local definitions continue to govern City zoning unless the Council expressly amends them. State air, water, waste, building, and tax definitions apply within their respective statutory programs; this draft does not silently enlarge state definitions or confer tax eligibility.[^10][^11][^27][^28][^29]

<span class="m2-add" style="color:#a41f27"><strong>Data Center (committee-approved November 11, 2026):</strong></span> A structure or building rehabilitated or constructed to house a group of networked server computers in one physical location in order to centralize the storage, management, and dissemination of data and information pertaining to a particular business, taxonomy, or body of knowledge. A data center business's facility typically includes the mechanical and electrical systems, redundant or backup power supplies, redundant data communications connections, environmental controls, and fire suppression systems.[^10]

<span class="m2-add" style="color:#a41f27"><strong>Hyperscale Data Center (committee-approved November 11, 2026; reported vote 5–3):</strong></span> A Data Center meeting both of the following conditions:

1. **100,000 square feet or larger.**
2. **Electrical demand exceeding 60 megawatts (MW).**[^30]

<span class="m2-add" style="color:#a41f27"><strong>Data Center Business (committee-approved November 11, 2026):</strong></span> An entity whose business among other businesses, is to operate or lease a data center in the city of cascade.[^10]

<span class="m2-add" style="color:#a41f27"><strong>Sustainable Design (committee-approved November 11, 2026):</strong></span> Construction design that reduces negative environmental effects and improves occupant health and comfort through resource conservation, waste minimization, and healthier environments.[^26]

> **Editorial reconciliation (noncodified):** The November 11 committee-reported definitions above preserve approved substance, including the 5–3 Hyperscale Data Center threshold. The Data Center and Data Center Business texts adapt Iowa's **sales-tax** definitions in §423.3(95)(f)(1)–(2); neither is verbatim. In particular, Data Center adds **“structure or”** and omits the statute's restricted-access/lease clauses; Data Center Business adds **“in the city of cascade.”** Sustainable Design paraphrases, rather than quotes, the controlling definition in §103A.3(25). A later change to match state language **verbatim** would require committee review; statutory building-code and tax-program definitions do not automatically bind City zoning. The earlier independent project term **“Data Center or High-Density Computing Facility”** is superseded in this *definitions* section only. The §165.11(B)(19) use entry has been aligned with the approved Data Center definition; the distinct proposed §165.12(17) citywide Hyperscale prohibition is not itself a November 11 committee vote.

<span class="m2-add" style="color:#a41f27"><strong>Major Industrial Facility:</strong></span> An M-2 use exceeding any one threshold in proposed §165.11(D)(8): 5 MW coincident demand, 25,000 gallons in any 24 hours, 2 MW aggregate stationary generation, or 5 MWh storage. Also includes a modification that crosses a threshold.

<span class="m2-add" style="color:#a41f27"><strong>Material Expansion:</strong></span> A modification increasing an approved maximum by 20 percent or more for electrical demand, water demand, stationary generation, energy storage, exterior mechanical capacity, or developed building area, or crossing a Major Industrial Facility threshold for the first time.

<span class="m2-add" style="color:#a41f27"><strong>Sensitive Receptor:</strong></span> An occupied dwelling, school, licensed child-care facility, hospital, nursing facility, place of worship, public library, public park facility, or comparable occupied use requiring protection from continuous industrial noise.

<span class="m2-add" style="color:#a41f27"><strong>Ambient Noise / Baseline Sound:</strong></span> The sound environment at a designated receiving location without the regulated facility's incremental contribution, determined under representative day, night, and meteorological conditions. Ambient noise is not the Iowa air-quality term "ambient air."[^7][^6]

<span class="m2-add" style="color:#a41f27"><strong>Facility-Attributable Noise:</strong></span> The sound contribution attributable to a regulated M-2 use at the specified receiving point, including reasonably foreseeable simultaneous operation of equipment; exclude unrelated transportation, natural, and third-party background noise through an approved, documented acoustical procedure.[^6]

<span class="m2-add" style="color:#a41f27"><strong>dBA:</strong></span> A-weighted decibels of sound pressure, measured with calibrated equipment using the applicable professional noise measurement method.

<span class="m2-add" style="color:#a41f27"><strong>dBC:</strong></span> C-weighted decibels of sound pressure, which place greater relative weight on low-frequency sound than A-weighting.

<span class="m2-add" style="color:#a41f27"><strong>Hourly Average Sound Level (Leq,1h):</strong></span> The energy-equivalent continuous sound-pressure level over sixty (60) minutes, expressed using the specified A- or C-weighting; intermittent contributions are averaged on an acoustic energy basis, not by taking an arithmetic mean of decibel values. The qualified professional shall identify source operation, background sound, measurement location, and calibration.[^6]

<span class="m2-add" style="color:#a41f27"><strong>Prominent Tone / Tonal Noise:</strong></span> An identifiable recurring or steady audible frequency component distinguishable from neighboring sound bands when evaluated under the ordinance's specified professional acoustical method.

<span class="m2-add" style="color:#a41f27"><strong>Acoustic Study:</strong></span> A qualified professional's documented baseline survey, maximum-design-operation model, receptor analysis, assumptions, equipment schedule, and verification protocol.

<span class="m2-add" style="color:#a41f27"><strong>Maximum Anticipated Coincident Electrical Demand:</strong></span> The greatest reasonably anticipated simultaneous electrical load, expressed in MW, for the complete site during normal or design-maximum operation. A sum of independent equipment nameplate ratings does not establish coincident site demand without a documented load calculation.

<span class="m2-add" style="color:#a41f27"><strong>Aggregate Stationary Generation Capacity:</strong></span> The sum of rated electrical outputs of all stationary generators covered by the section, in megawatts, excluding fire pumps only where expressly provided.

<span class="m2-add" style="color:#a41f27"><strong>Aggregate Electrical-Energy-Storage Capacity:</strong></span> The summed rated stored electrical energy of covered stationary storage systems, in megawatt-hours, using documented nameplate energy ratings.

<span class="m2-add" style="color:#a41f27"><strong>Exterior Mechanical Equipment:</strong></span> Permanently located outdoor fans, compressors, cooling equipment, generators, transformers, heat-rejection systems, and comparable mechanical installations serving an M-2 principal use.

<span class="m2-add" style="color:#a41f27"><strong>Residential Zoning District Boundary:</strong></span> The official delineation between an M-2 parcel or district and land designated residential by the City's controlling zoning map.

<span class="m2-add" style="color:#a41f27"><strong>Sensitive-Receptor Measurement Location:</strong></span> The specified exterior wall, occupied outdoor-use area, or other objectively identified receiving point at a sensitive receptor, documented on an approved site/acoustic plan.

<span class="m2-add" style="color:#a41f27"><strong>Once-Through Cooling:</strong></span> A process in which municipal potable water passes through a cooling system once and is discharged rather than being recirculated for heat rejection.

<span class="m2-add" style="color:#a41f27"><strong>Closed-Loop Cooling:</strong></span> A recirculating coolant system intended to keep working fluid contained, subject to any limited makeup or process losses specifically disclosed.

<span class="m2-add" style="color:#a41f27"><strong>Emergency Response Plan:</strong></span> A written, site-specific plan documenting access, contacts, utility isolation, fuel, chemicals, battery systems, suppression, evacuation, spills, and coordination with first responders.

<span class="m2-add" style="color:#a41f27"><strong>Decommissioning:</strong></span> The safe disconnection, dismantling, lawful removal, cleanup, and site restoration work required at the end of a covered facility's life.

<span class="m2-add" style="color:#a41f27"><strong>Abandonment:</strong></span> Continuous cessation of a facility's principal approved industrial operation for the period specified in §165.11(G)(17), subject to its listed reasonable exceptions.

<span class="m2-add" style="color:#a41f27"><strong>Financial Assurance:</strong></span> A surety, letter of credit, escrow, performance bond, trust, or other legally authorized and enforceable arrangement in a reasonable amount covering documented decommissioning and site restoration duties. The statutory definition applicable to sanitary-disposal facilities under Iowa Code §455B.301(9) does not itself grant the City authority to impose that requirement on M-2 data centers; a separate legal basis must be confirmed by the City Attorney.[^13]

<span class="m2-add" style="color:#a41f27"><strong>Independent Qualified Professional:</strong></span> An appropriately licensed or credentialed expert with relevant technical competence and no financial or managerial interest that would compromise independent evaluation.

<span class="m2-add" style="color:#a41f27"><strong>Material Modification (for monitoring):</strong></span> A change in equipment, site layout, generation operation, or other covered operation reasonably likely to change the regulated environmental or infrastructure impact.

### Avoid duplicate definitions and use existing law

No new entries are proposed for **Building/Structure**, **Lot/Lot Line**, **Dwelling Unit**, **Accessory Use**, or **Construction**. Read the already-existing Cascade definitions in §165.41(1), (8), (12), (30), and (51), and the Iowa state building-code definition of Construction in §103A.3(5), **without importing a building-code definition into zoning automatically**.[^11][^27] The removed proposed “Property Boundary / Lot Line” entry duplicated basic lot-line terminology; actual permit and acoustic boundaries should be grounded in the City's zoning map and recorded survey/plat, not a competing general definition.

Do not insert municipal redefinitions of **Air Contaminant**, **Air Contaminant Source**, **Air Pollution**, **Emission**, **Major Stationary Source**, or **Potential to Emit**. Those terms are defined by **Iowa Code §455B.131(1)–(3), (6), (8), (11)** for the state air-quality program. Cite the official statute when applying that program, and retain the City's authority to regulate land-use impacts only within applicable law.[^28]

**Definitions that remain local and technically necessary** (such as facility-attributable noise, Leq, sensitive receptor, coincident demand, material expansion, and aggregate generator capacity) have specific roles in the proposed performance standards and are not replaced with superficially similar state tax/building/environmental definitions. The separate 86-term research glossary is **noncodified** and does not add 86 City definitions.

### Relationship to other Iowa-defined terms

For interpretation **within the respective state-regulated subject**, the following meanings are governed by the stated Iowa authority: water-allocation terms "aquifer," "beneficial use," "nonregulated use," and "regulated use" (Iowa Code §455B.261); state air-emissions terms "air contaminant," "emission," and "major stationary source" (§455B.131 and Iowa Administrative Code 567—22.1); hazardous-condition response terms (§455B.381); hazardous-waste terms (§455B.411); and stormwater permitting terms (Iowa Administrative Code 567—60.1 and incorporated federal definitions). Those terms **are not silently repurposed** as independent municipal zoning thresholds. State-defined "major stationary source" is distinct from the City's proposed "Major Industrial Facility."[^8][^7][^12][^14]

### Interpretation and conflicts

The originally scanned M-2 dimensional standards are a 25-foot front yard, 25-foot rear yard, 20-foot side yard, 20-foot street-side yard for corner lots, and 60-foot maximum height, except where duly adopted later provisions modify them. The source PDF itself includes an unsigned and undated **2009 draft** proposing a zero-lot-line exception; the scan alone cannot establish whether it was enacted. A later Ordinance 05-22 adds permitted fitness uses as item B(18), and Ordinance 19-24 permits fences up to eight feet in M-2 subject to its additional conditions. **No unconditional assertion of current-law completeness is made.**[^15][^16]

The proposal preserves existing Cascade definitions of "Building/Structure," "Accessory Use," "Lot," and "Dwelling" except where a later duly adopted amendment controls. The City Attorney shall verify all intervening amendments to the 1992 base zoning code and confirm final section numbering, cross-references, and special-exception authority before introduction.[^3][^11]

## SECTION V. CONFLICTS AND MORE RESTRICTIVE REQUIREMENTS

Nothing in this ordinance relieves a property owner or operator from compliance with any other applicable City, county, state, federal, utility, fire, building, environmental, or safety requirement. Where two applicable zoning provisions regulate the same subject and can lawfully be applied together, the more restrictive provision shall control, consistent with Section 165.03.

## SECTION VI. SEVERABILITY

If any section, subsection, paragraph, sentence, clause, phrase, or application of this ordinance is adjudged invalid or unconstitutional, such adjudication shall not affect the validity of the ordinance as a whole or any other section, provision, or application not adjudged invalid or unconstitutional.

## SECTION VII. EFFECTIVE DATE

This ordinance shall be in full force and effect after its final passage, approval, and publication as provided by law.

This draft intentionally avoids regulating Simple Mining, cryptocurrency mining, or data centers as uniquely disfavored businesses. ## E. Acoustic Standards

The draft's dBA/dBC structure is intended to address both ordinary audible sound and low-frequency industrial hum. The reference to ANSI/ASA S12.9 provides a recognized environmental-noise measurement framework rather than creating a project-specific testing method.

**November 3 indoor-receptor revision:** Proposed §165.11(G)(4)(b) now evaluates the nearest normally occupied interior room, within five feet of the source-facing exterior wall, with doors and windows closed. The separate M-2 property-line standard remains outdoors. The five-foot maximum placement distance is a **proposed local rule**, not a distance mandated by ISO or ANSI. A 0.5-meter minimum clearance from reflective surfaces and documenting room conditions draw on accepted acoustic measurement principles.[^18]

**Substantive policy question (updated November 11 working draft):** The proposed 65/75 dBA/dBC daytime and 60/70 dBA/dBC nighttime limits now also apply to the *indoor* sensitive-receptor test, but an indoor limit is not equivalent to an outdoor limit because walls and closed windows attenuate exterior sound. WHO's community-noise guidance identifies 30 dBA Leq for bedrooms at night as a sleep-protection guideline. The proposed 60 dBA nighttime indoor limit should therefore be explicitly reviewed by an independent acoustician and the City Attorney, not presented as a WHO-derived or state-mandated standard.[^19] Closing windows also removes the draft's prior direct protection of outdoor living areas. Occupant consent or other lawful authority is required for indoor access; modeled exposure is a screening tool, not a substitute for a direct measurement.

**Iowa home envelopes after 2015 (research, not a codified noise reduction credit):** Iowa's statewide residential energy code remained the **2012 IECC with state amendments** following its April 1, 2014 effective date. It requires thermal insulation and a blower-door air-sealing result not exceeding **4 ACH50**, and allows common wood-frame prescriptive exterior wall constructions such as **R-20 cavity insulation or R-13 cavity plus R-5 continuous insulation**. These are *energy-efficiency* provisions, **not** requirements that every residence attenuate noise by a fixed number of dBA or dBC.[^20]

FHWA's *Noise Measurement Handbook*, Table 6-1, illustrates **20 dB outside-to-inside reduction** for light-frame buildings with ordinary **closed** windows, **25 dB** for light-frame/storm-window assemblies and **25–35 dB** for listed masonry/window combinations. The values are **traffic-noise modeling assumptions**, **not Iowa housing acoustic standards**. Applying 20 dB to a hypothetical 65 dBA exterior value yields about 45 dBA inside **only if that particular sound spectrum and building actually achieve 20 dB attenuation**. These figures cannot be used as a blanket **20 dBC** deduction for low-frequency data-center, transformer or HVAC hum.[^21]

For local context, Cascade Code **§6-12-8**, Ordinance **02-22** (August 22, 2022), adopted the current State of Iowa Building Code by reference. Dubuque County was identified as IECC Zone **5A**, Delaware County as **6A**, under the 2012-era county map; neither county classification establishes acoustic transmission loss. The City and acoustician should **not presume a specific residential noise attenuation solely from a construction date**, and should identify facade-specific, frequency-dependent performance or clearly labeled modeling assumptions.[^22] The [full building-code and exterior-noise review](https://ttbakiatwoam.github.io/cascade-data-center/house-noise-attenuation-iowa/) is part of the repository's non-codified evidence, not a new enforceable provision.

## G. Items Better Addressed Outside the Zoning Ordinance

The following matters are generally better addressed through a development agreement, economic-development agreement, utility agreement, or other lawful contractual mechanism rather than being written as project-specific zoning requirements:

- LEED certification or equivalent sustainability certification;
- local hiring and workforce-development commitments;
- wage or benefit commitments;
- community-benefit payments;
- tax-incentive terms;
- project-specific infrastructure reimbursement mechanics;
- voluntary limits below otherwise applicable zoning thresholds; and
- project-specific representations concerning jobs, investment, tenant mix, or expansion.

If the City wants any of these concepts to become district-wide zoning rules, they should be rewritten to apply uniformly to all similarly situated M-2 businesses and reviewed for statutory authority.

## H. Required Legal Review Before Introduction

The City Attorney should specifically review:

1. Whether the Major Industrial Facility thresholds and Special Exception structure fit Cascade's current Section 165.34 procedure;
2. Application of new performance standards to existing lawful uses and nonconformities;
3. Authority and process for applicant-funded independent studies and compliance testing;
4. Financial-assurance authority and acceptable decommissioning instruments;
5. Cost-recovery and infrastructure-agreement authority;
6. Public-record treatment of security-sensitive facility information;
7. State or federal preemption affecting utilities, air emissions, fuel storage, batteries, and emergency systems;
8. Whether any separate general noise, nuisance, fire-code, or building-code amendments should accompany the zoning amendment; and
9. Final numbering and integration with all amendments to Chapter 165 adopted after the base zoning text;
10. Whether an annual March 1 certification, third-party review deposit, generator documentation, commissioning deadline, and recorded decommissioning obligations require separate enabling ordinances, adopted schedules, or development agreements;
11. How Iowa Code §414.23 limits municipal zoning beyond Cascade city limits when county zoning applies, and the distinct plat-review authority under Iowa Code §354.9;
12. Whether a later separate engineering study supports measurable vibration standards; Jackson County's proposed peak-particle-velocity figures are not imported without independent review; and
13. Whether the requested generator equipment schedules or security-sensitive response plans require protection under Iowa Code Chapter 22 while preserving lawful public access; and
14. Whether interior-receptor levels, microphone placement, closed-window conditions, indoor access limitations, and modeled-versus-measured verification adequately protect occupied structures and can be administered fairly.
