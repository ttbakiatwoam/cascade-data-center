# November 18, 2026 - Diesel Generation, Air Standards, and Iowa Municipal Zoning Comparisons

**For the Cascade Data Center Advisory Committee, 7:00 p.m. November 18, 2026**  
**Research cutoff:** November 16, 2026. **Independent research briefing; not an approved committee position or legal/engineering opinion.**

## Read this before discussing a Tier 4 mandate

A committee participant has reported **approximately 15 proposed diesel generator sets** associated with Simple Mining's **approximately 40 MW** preliminary data-center concept. The 15-unit count, electrical output, fuel, maker/model, manufacture date, engine displacement, certificate, air permit and intended operating use have **not** been corroborated by a final generator schedule or issued permit. An illustrative 40 MW / 15 calculation equals **2.67 MW per unit**, **not** proof of individual generator rating, Tier 4 status, 40 MW of installed generator capacity, or how redundancy is allocated. The 40 MW figure has been presented as **facility demand**, not necessarily diesel backup capacity. The project scope remains preliminary.

**Decision-critical finding:** Neither Iowa law nor federal law assigns a stationary diesel engine's emissions tier solely by generator count or by facility megawatts. Section **40 CFR 60.4202** provides **Tier 2 or Tier 3 pathways** for many newly manufactured **emergency** stationary compression-ignition engines; **40 CFR 60.4201** and **60.4204** cover **nonemergency** engines and can point to other standards. Correct selection requires **each engine's construction/manufacture/model year, horsepower or engine kW (not merely alternator output), liters per cylinder, EPA family/certificate and emergency-use classification**. [Federal §60.4202](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-40/section-60.4202) · [§60.4205](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-40/section-60.4205) · [§60.4204](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-40/section-60.4204).

**Do not conflate:**
1. **Exhaust certification limits** for NOx, hydrocarbons, CO and particulate matter, generally expressed in **grams per engine kilowatt-hour (g/kW-hr)**, with different model-year and displacement categories.
2. **Fuel sulfur content**, expressed as **parts per million by weight** in 40 CFR 1090.305 (generally **15 ppm** for stationary engines **under 30 liters/cylinder** following §60.4207(b)). The **§60.4207(d)** fuel rule for engines **30 or more liters/cylinder** separately specifies **1,000 ppm**; do **not** blanket-apply 15 ppm before determining displacement and other applicable law/permit conditions.
3. **Outdoor ambient-air standards**, such as **NO2 100 ppb = 0.100 ppm, one-hour; SO2 75 ppb = 0.075 ppm, one-hour; CO 35 ppm, one-hour / 9 ppm, eight-hour**. These are concentration standards **at ambient receptors**, with specific regulatory statistical forms, **not diesel stack outlet limits**. [EPA NAAQS table](https://www.epa.gov/criteria-air-pollutants/naaqs-table). Iowa **567 IAC 22.11** incorporates national ambient standards; it does not generally set a universal generator NOx-in-exhaust ppm ceiling.

**Emergency-use conditions:** Under §60.4211(f), genuine emergency operation is not subject to a uniform annual hour ceiling, but routine maintenance/readiness operation is ordinarily covered by the **100 hours per calendar year** provisions; a limited **50 hours of nonemergency operation is part of, not added to, that 100 hours**, and cannot generally be used for peak shaving or ordinary economic dispatch, except the tightly specified §60.4211(f)(3)(i) grid-protection conditions. Engine classification can change if nonemergency-use restrictions are breached.

**Regulatory hierarchy:** Federal engine certification and NESHAP are not replaced by a municipal zoning definition; a local Tier 4-only requirement, if considered, would be an **additional proposed local policy** requiring an identified legal rationale, feasibility analysis, compatibility with Iowa Code Chapter 414, and separate committee/Council consideration.

**Scientific caution:** A fuel sulfur value of 15 ppm does **not** mean NOx at the stack is 15 ppm. No valid direct conversion exists from a g/kWh engine limit to a stack ppm without engine fuel/exhaust composition, operating load, flow, basis, oxygen correction and testing conditions. Ambient ppm is evaluated at receptors by dispersion and background analysis. Applying ambient NAAQS as exhaust limits or converting Tier rating to stack ppm with an arbitrary factor would be technically indefensible.

## Iowa law and the actual permitting question

The packet's verbatim Iowa-law appendix draws **relevant Chapter 22 pages** from the official [Iowa Administrative Code Chapter 22, June 10, 2026 archive (IAC text dated January 7, 2026)](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/iac/chapter/06-10-2026.567.22.pdf); paper reproduction will identify the actual edition and page ranges.

- **567 IAC 22.1 - definition of "emergency generator":** limited to providing emergency power in an unforeseen utility interruption; it excludes ordinary utility peaking and generators operated while utility power is available. **Iowa's definition can be materially narrower than an operator's casual label "backup".**
- **567 IAC 22.1(1)-(3):** stationary-source **air construction permits** generally apply before construction or modification unless a specified exemption applies. A 15-engine installation should not be presumed exempt or permit-free.
- **567 IAC 22.1 - potential-to-emit (PTE) calculation:** special **500 hours/year** for certain historical operation of emergency generators **is a PTE calculation assumption, not permission to run for 500 hours**; alternative 8,760 or federally enforceable hour caps may apply. For new generators, require DNR's project-specific PTE method and permit classification.
- **567 IAC 23.1(2) and 23.1(4):** Iowa adopts federal NSPS and hazardous-air-pollutant requirements by reference (with state-specific adoption dates/exceptions). Check the current Subpart IIII and ZZZZ entries and Iowa permit applicability; do not assume "Tier 4" appears as an Iowa generic diesel-generator mandate.
- **567 IAC 22.11:** Iowa's ambient air standards refer to federal **40 CFR Part 50** standards; the pollutant ppm/ppb values in this memo are **ambient** values, not direct Iowa generator stack concentration thresholds.
- **Iowa DNR permit controls:** An individual issued permit can create narrower **unit-specific** limitations (lb/hr, tons/year, hours, fuel limits, stack parameters, monitoring) and may require modeling cumulative emissions from all installed engines. A general 15-generator source permit, permit-to-construct or Title V/PSD classification cannot be resolved without a complete specification, emissions inventory and enforceable operating hours. [Iowa DNR air construction permit guidance](https://www.iowadnr.gov/environmental-protection/air-quality/air-quality-construction-permits).

**Iowa's fuel/ambient standards are not a substitute for the applicable federal engine-certification emissions limits.** Present federal and Iowa authorities **separately**; do not characterize Iowa as setting a universal "NOx 15 ppm diesel generator" standard.

## Iowa CITY zoning comparisons - clauses for highlighted consideration

**Status matters:** "Adopted" means an enacted City ordinance; "draft" means proposed text with **no assumed legal effect**. County drafts are isolated in the next subsection. The following concise source-linked summaries identify **paragraphs to highlight**, not wholesale legislative endorsements.

### Central City - November 2026 public draft, NOT enacted

**Primary reference:** The user-supplied 10-page *Zoning_Code_Amendment.pdf* (pages cited below), also linked in the project's [city comparison](../dataset/policy/49_CITY_FIRST_DATA_CENTER_ZONING_AND_HOME_RULE_2026-11-11.md). [November 10 reporting on release/status](https://www.kcrg.com/2026/11/10/central-city-releases-draft-ordinance-outlining-rules-proposed-data-center/). The document has blanks for ordinance number, readings, signatures and adoption date.

- **Pages 1-2, Sec. 2 & Secs. 3-4:** tiers: **Tier 1 <=30,000 sq ft; Tier 2 >30,000 and <100,000; Tier 3 >=100,000** cumulative gross floor area. Tier 1 proposed M-1/M-2; Tier 2 and Tier 3 M-2. **No >60 MW condition**. Related server rooms serving another principal use are excluded from the proposed definition.
- **Page 4, General Industrial Standards 6:** **65 dBA** combined operational noise at **any industrial site property line**, with a site-specific acoustic-method measurement; other generally applicable stricter noise restrictions remain. The draft provides an emergency-operation exception.
- **Page 5, General Industrial Standards 8:** routine testing and required maintenance, commissioning and load-bank testing proposed **8:00 a.m. to 6:00 p.m., Monday-Friday**, excluding federal holidays, and subject to attenuation and 65 dBA limits; genuine emergencies may operate at any time. **A zoning time window is not a substitute for EPA operating-hour and emissions requirements.**
- **Page 3, General Industrial Standards 1-4:** proportional water/wastewater, electricity, infrastructure, and site review, with specific written findings and cost attribution.
- **Pages 6-7, Supplemental Standards 4, 6, 8:** acoustical studies for all three tiers, disclosure of cooling and water, and tier transition on cumulative expansion.
- **Pages 8-10:** separate **proposed** Chapter 90 emergency water-service restriction procedure, with written determination and an expedited hearing; not a generally enacted law shown by this blank draft.

**Comparison:** Central City's draft permits even Tier 3 data centers in M-2, whereas the separate **independent Cascade proposal** would prohibit facilities meeting the two-part Hyperscale definition citywide. The Central City tier system **cannot** be described as an Iowa legal default.

### City of Palo - Ordinance 93-2026, adopted EU-1 data center district

**Official source:** [City of Palo Ordinance 93-2026](https://cityofpalo.com/vertical/sites/%7B434D4528-2EBA-41FD-BB30-AF0F1451A315%7D/uploads/93-2026_Zoning_Regulations-_EU-1_Data_Center_Exclusive_Use_District.pdf), §165.16A, particularly **Permitted Principal Use; Permitted Accessory Uses and Structures; Major Site Plan Required; Additional Information**. The ordinance establishes an **EU-1 exclusive-use district** for both large and small Data Centers. **On-site standby or backup power generation** is an accessory use; a facility generally requires EU-1 rezoning and a major site plan. This is a **dedicated zoning district**, not a 100,000-ft2-and-60-MW citywide ban. Do not assume EU-1 alone imposes a Tier 4 federal emissions classification.

### City of Dubuque - 2026 enacted hyperscale MORATORIUM only

**Official source:** [City data center information](https://www.cityofdubuque.org/datacenters) and [Ordinance 37-26](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/dubuqueia/latest/dubuque_ia/0-0-0-59603). The temporary moratorium applies when **any one** of the following is met: **>=100,000 sq ft OR >75 MW OR >=5,000 servers OR >50 gpm** process/cooling water. The **OR** design differs from Cascade's committee-approved **AND** definition. The moratorium is **temporary**, not an enacted permanent citywide ban, and does not supply standalone diesel-generator ppm limits.

## Public discussion checklist - specific documents to request

1. **Generator inventory:** each engine's manufacturer/model, nameplate **mechanical kW/HP and electrical output**, year/serial, EPA engine family and emissions certificate, displacement **liters per cylinder**, fuel sulfur specification, catalytic/DPF/SCR/urea hardware and exhaust-stack design.
2. **Intended purpose:** emergency-only backup, routine readiness tests/load-bank testing, nonemergency peak shaving, dispatch into the grid, or continuous/on-site generation? Provide **one-hour and annual operating scenarios** for each unit plus concurrent-operation assumptions.
3. **Air permits:** Iowa DNR pre-application consultation, each application number, PSD/Title V/major-source screening, and demonstration whether federal NSPS/RICE NESHAP applies, together with any federally enforceable limits.
4. **Emission calculations:** **NOx, CO, SO2, VOC, PM2.5, PM10, HAP** in **g/kW-hr, lb/hr and tons/year**, including startup and simultaneous testing. Show chemical-speciation and source test basis and avoid confusing ambient NO2 ppm with stack NOx.
5. **Dispersion and receptors:** AERMOD or accepted permitting analysis for simultaneous stacks, nearby homes/schools, meteorology, background concentrations and NAAQS compliance. Require documentary evidence rather than new arbitrary municipal ppm limits.
6. **Testing and noise:** expected maintenance frequency, duration, number of generators simultaneously operated, generator acoustics at sensitive homes, mufflers/barriers and EPA hourly operating categories.
7. **Fire and fuel:** inventory and tank containment, spill prevention, emergency-response access, and stormwater impacts.
8. **Legal scope:** identify where City zoning and general nuisance controls apply **in addition to** state/federal air permits, without assigning the City powers reserved for Iowa DNR or the EPA.
